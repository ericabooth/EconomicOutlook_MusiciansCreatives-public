# Figure QA pass, 2026-08-02

Visual review of all 27 PNGs in `04_figures/`. Every file was opened and inspected at full resolution (2000 px wide), with additional crops at 2x to 3x magnification wherever text sat close to another element or to the canvas edge. This pass checks rendered images only; it does not re-verify the underlying estimates.

**Headline count: 5 PASS, 8 MINOR, 14 FIX.**

Two automated checks supported the visual read:

- An edge-ink scan of the outer 3 px of every canvas found hard clipping on exactly two figures (fig03, fig11). No other figure has ink running off the trim.
- A margin scan found three figures whose rightmost ink sits within 20 px of the canvas edge (fig03 at 0 px, fig11 at 0 px, fig04 at 8 px, fig20 at 15 px).

Criterion 7 is clean. No figure carries a `Source:` line, a footnote, or a citation block baked into the image. Several subtitles do name the dataset (ACS PUMS, OEWS, ZORI, NASAA, the 2022 Greater Austin Music Census); those read as universe and vintage descriptors rather than source notes, so they are left alone. The one case that does cross the line is fig17, which puts "BEA satellite-account data" in the main title. See that row.

## Publication-ready as they stand

**Five figures need no changes: fig02, fig05, fig15, fig21, and fig27.** Each was checked against all seven criteria, including a magnified pass on every label that sits near another element.

**Eight figures are usable with known compromises (MINOR): fig09, fig12, fig13, fig22, fig23, fig24, fig25, fig28.** None has a clipped string, a colliding label, or a title contradicted by its data. The recurring compromises are truncated axes without a disclosure note, dual axes that share units but not scales (fig13 is the clearest case), and inconsistent styling between figures that a reader will compare (fig22 against fig24).

**Fourteen figures need work before this document goes to city and state policymakers.** Of those, the six Tier 1 items are the ones that would survive a skim and damage the report's credibility, because each states or implies something the reader can check against the chart itself and find wrong.

## Two cross-cutting notes

**Axis-truncation disclosure is inconsistent.** fig20 states "axis does not start at zero" in its subtitle. Seven other line and dot charts truncate an axis without saying so: fig07, fig08, fig09 (both panels), fig19 (empty lower third), fig22, fig23, fig26. Consider adopting fig20's sentence as the house pattern. All bar charts in the set correctly start at zero, so criterion 6 is otherwise met.

**Print size.** At 6.5 in the 2000 px exports resolve to about 308 dpi, which is adequate everywhere. At 3.2 in, type drops to roughly half its 6.5 in size, and four figures would stop being legible: fig16 (top panel rows are already crowded at full size), fig21 (16 rows), fig25 (five-segment stacks with in-bar labels), and fig10 (markers already indistinct). Consider reserving the 3.2 in slot for fig05, fig15, or fig12.
