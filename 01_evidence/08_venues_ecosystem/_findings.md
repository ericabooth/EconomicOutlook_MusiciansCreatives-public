# Findings — Austin live-music venue ecosystem: supply, revenue, attrition

Evidence compiled 2026-08-01. All dollar figures are **real 2026-06 dollars** (CPI-U deflated)
unless the word "nominal" appears. All receipts figures exclude the 10 festival-aggregation rows
documented in `_sources.md` §4.2 unless noted. Read `_sources.md` before quoting anything.

---

## A. The headline: the venue panel is shrinking while the bar market around it grows

1. **A curated panel of 114 Austin live-music venues took in $101.5M in 2025, down 6.9% in real terms from $109.0M in 2019.** Over the same period, *all* mixed-beverage permittees in Travis County and Austin grew **+2.8%** in real receipts. The music venues are not tracking the market they sit inside.

2. **Narrow to the 37 venues whose primary business is live music and present in both years, and the real decline is 16.9%** ($47.3M → $39.3M). For the full balanced panel of 63 venues present in both 2019 and 2025, the decline is **16.2%**. The **median** venue in that group lost **19.7%** of its real receipts.

3. **46 of the 60 venues with more than $250k of real receipts in 2019 were smaller in real terms by 2025** — roughly three in four.

4. **The contrast with citywide licensing is the sharpest single number here.** Active mixed-beverage permits reporting receipts in Travis/Austin rose from **1,283 in 2019 to 1,640 in 2025 (+27.8%)**. Austin added bars and restaurants at pace while its live-music rooms contracted. The matched panel held a steady **~9% share of citywide receipts** across the whole period (9.3% in 2013, 8.9% in 2019, 9.2% in 2025), so this divergence is not a coverage artifact.

5. **This is a post-2019 phenomenon, not a long decline.** The same style of balanced panel grew **+16.1% in real terms from 2013 to 2019** (58 venues). The inflection is recent and sits after the pandemic, not at it — see finding 8.

## B. COVID and the shape of the recovery

6. **April 2020 receipts across the matched panel were $70,485 — 0.8% of the 2019 monthly average of $9.09M.** A 99.2% collapse in a single month. Calendar 2020 finished **68.3% below 2019** in real terms.

7. **The recovery was fast and then reversed.** Real panel receipts: 2019 $109.0M → 2020 $34.6M → 2021 $82.9M → 2022 $129.6M → 2023 $128.6M → **2024 $116.0M → 2025 $101.5M**. The peak was 2022, and the panel has fallen in each of the three years since. 2026 H1 (Jan–Jun) stands at $55.5M, an annualized pace near 2025.

8. **The core live-music tier never recovered at all.** Real receipts for that tier: 2019 $54.7M, 2024 $47.1M, **2025 $44.5M** — 18.6% below 2019 and below every pre-pandemic year back to 2013. The aggregate panel was held up by large venues (Moody Center, Moody Amphitheater, ACL Live/3TEN, The Concourse) that opened or expanded after 2021. Strip those out and the recovery narrative disappears.

## C. Attrition: who stopped filing, and when

9. **41 of the 114 matched venues have stopped reporting receipts.** Exits split evenly across eras — **20 in 2010–2019 and 20 in 2020–2026** — but the composition changed: **16 of the 20 post-2020 exits were core live-music rooms.**

10. **The turn from expansion to contraction dates to about 2016.** Net venue entries minus exits ran **+80 across 2007–2015** and **−7 across 2016–2026**. The panel stopped adding rooms roughly a decade ago.

11. **Recent exits by last month of receipts:** Container Bar (2024-01), Skylark Lounge (2025-05), Spider House (2025-06), Dozen Street/Outer Heaven (2025-07), The Parish at 501 N IH-35 (2025-07), The Belmont (2026-04).

12. **Twenty venues have filed every one of the 234 months from Jan 2007 to Jun 2026** — the survivor set: Mohawk, Stubb's, Elysium, Continental Club, Saxon Pub, Broken Spoke, Donn's Depot, Elephant Room, Hole in the Wall, Flamingo Cantina, Paramount Theatre, Speakeasy, Maggie Mae's, Esther's Follies/Velveeta Room, Blind Pig, Barton Springs Saloon, Shoal Creek Saloon, G&S Lounge, Lala's, Nutty Brown Cafe.

13. **The Red River Cultural District is contracting faster than the city.** Real receipts across matched Red River permits fell **22.1% from 2019 to 2025** and **25.7% from the 2015 peak**. Permits filing in the district dropped from **11 in 2011 to 8 in 2025**.

## D. Individual venues: the spread is enormous

14. **Steepest real declines, 2019 → 2025:** Maggie Mae's **−89.5%** ($2.68M → $0.28M), Spider House **−87.8%**, Skylark Lounge **−86.0%**, The Lost Well **−81.7%**, Buzz Mill **−80.6%**, Nomad Bar **−76.9%**, Clive Bar **−70.7%**, The Belmont **−66.1%**, Blind Pig **−53.1%**, Moon Tower Saloon **−49.1%**.

15. **Steepest real gains, 2019 → 2025:** ACL Live at Moody Theater / 3TEN **+683%**, Mala Vida **+189%**, The Far Out Lounge **+154%**, Elephant Room **+64%**, Donn's Depot **+46%**, Hotel Vegas/Volstead **+42%**, Paramount Theatre **+41%**. The winners are either large ticketed rooms or venues that expanded their footprint — not small clubs.

## E. Supply-side confirmation from City of Austin permits

16. **Outdoor Music Venue permits issued by the City fell 31.9% from their peak** — 138 in both 2014 and 2017, 99 in 2019, **94 in 2025** (2026 shows 42 through July, a partial year). This is an independent City administrative series and it moves the same direction as the receipts panel.

17. **595 distinct venue names appear across 1,751 Outdoor Music Venue permits, 2009–2026.** Median permitted capacity is **190** — Austin's outdoor live-music supply is overwhelmingly small rooms. **77% of these permits (1,348) sit in Council District 9**, the central-city district, which concentrates both the activity and the residential noise conflict.

18. The City's `Creative Workspaces and Performance Venues` inventory carries **679 records with a live-music flag on 263**, and the 2022 Austin Music Census microdata (**2,227 respondents × 107 variables**) is downloaded here for the demand/worker side of the story.

## F. Closure causes from the news record (71-row timeline)

19. **Of 36 dated closures, the cited reasons cluster as: COVID 13, rent increase 7, sale of property 5, unknown 4, financial 3, redevelopment 3, permit/regulatory 2, lease expiration 2.** Rent and property transactions together (15) outnumber COVID.

20. **The cleanest rent evidence in the record is September 2015, when Holy Mountain and Red 7 closed together on adjacent East Seventh lots.** Renewals went from **$5,500 to $8,000/month (+45%)** and **$9,000 to $14,000/month (+56%)** — base rent, before triple-net. Both venues sat inside the Red River Cultural District, created in 2013 partly to protect them; the designation raised the land values that displaced them. Our receipts data independently puts both venues' last filing at **2015-10**.

21. **The Sidewinder closed 12 May 2018 after the Fire Marshal cut its back-patio capacity from 239 to 49** — a re-interpretation of what counts as an exit, landing the week before SXSW, the single largest revenue week of the year. Regulatory risk, not rent. Receipts confirm a last filing of 2018-05.

22. **Three Red River clubs closed in one week in June 2020** — Barracuda, Plush, Scratchouse. All three occupied rooms vacated in the 2015 rent cycle, so the pandemic removed tenants already selected for fragility. Surveyed Austin venues reported an average of **30 days** of operating savings.

23. **State highway construction removed two venues in 15 days:** The Lost Well (27 Oct 2024) and Stars Cafe (10 Nov 2024, after 50+ years), both to the I-35 expansion. Not a market outcome.

24. **The Parlor lost its building in April 2024 over a ~$60,000 down payment on a $3M purchase — about 2% of the price — despite having loan approval**, after the landlord died. This is a capital-access failure rather than a rent or demand failure, and it points toward a different policy instrument than rent subsidy.

25. **Austin's Iconic Venue Fund drew 45 proposals requesting more than $300M, shortlisted 14, and has made one award** — $1.6M to Hole in the Wall on 15 Aug 2023, roughly three years after the fund was created. Demand for venue capital exceeds supply by roughly two orders of magnitude.

26. **Property support has not been sufficient on its own.** Empire Control Room took a **$2.2M Rally Austin acquisition loan in Oct 2024**, cut 20% of staff in Dec 2025, and by Jan 2026 carried **$103,701.77 in delinquent taxes** plus two suits alleging misappropriation. Our receipts data shows Empire still filing through 2026-06.

27. **Cheer Up Charlies was locked out on 11 March 2026 over $15,529.58 — one day before SXSW** — after running $58,000 behind in Aug 2025 and a failed sale. Access was restored.

## G. Two corrections a hostile reader will otherwise find

28. **Do not use "90% of Austin venues will close" as an outcome.** Two separate claims get conflated: a UH Hobby School projection (Austin, by Halloween 2020) and a NIVA projection (national, six months). Both were **conditional on no relief arriving**, and $16.25B of federal Shuttered Venue Operators Grant money then arrived. Austin's actual 2020 closure cohort was roughly **11 named venues**. The Hobby School survey polled 1,000+ owners across all industries and never states the venue subsample size.

29. **Counting venue *names* overstates lost stages, because addresses recycle.** 617 Red River ran Blue Flamingo → Plush → Chess Club; 711 Red River ran Beerland → Green Jay → 13th Floor; 715 Red River ran Red Eyed Fly → Sidewinder → Birdie Num Nums → The Cut. The receipts panel keeps successor tenants as separate rows precisely so this churn is visible. **Capacity and paid performance slots are the right units, not venue names.**

## H. Cross-validation: where the receipts data corrects the news record

30. Matching the 36 dated closures against permit filings (`closure_crossvalidation.csv`) gives **10 agreements, 11 cases where receipts continue past the reported closure year, and 15 venues absent from the mixed-beverage file entirely**.

31. **The receipts data resolves the One-2-One Bar conflict** the news research flagged: One-2-One appears on the Chronicle's 2020 closure list, but the permit filed continuously through **2023-02**. It did not close in 2020.

32. **Relocations show up cleanly as address changes under one operator** — Giddy Ups (12000 Manchaca → 10509 Old Manchaca, first filing 2026-05), The Lost Well (2421 Webberville → 1141½ Airport, first filing 2025-10). Both confirm the "closed_relocated" coding.

33. **Several reported closures mask partial survival.** Spider House Ballroom closed in 2020, but the Spider House permit filed through **2025-06**; the North Door is listed as a 2020 closure but filed through **2023-06**. Treat venue-level closure dates as the death of a *programming operation*, which can precede the death of the *business* by years.

## I. Texas Music Office directory: gated, but the taxonomy is recovered

34. **The TMO directory cannot be scraped.** `texasmusic.reel-scout.com` returns **HTTP 403 with `cf-mitigated: challenge`** (Cloudflare bot challenge, not bypassed). The predecessor app at `gov.texas.gov/Apps/Music/Directory/` is retired and returns HTTP 500. The site is ASP.NET WebForms rendering results server-side into a table, with filtering via `__VIEWSTATE` postback — so it is **Cloudflare-gated, not JavaScript-gated**, and there is no JSON endpoint to target.

35. **The directory structure was recovered from April 2025 Internet Archive snapshots:** three entry points only (`type=S` Music Business, `type=B` Radio Stations, `type=M` Musicians), **106 music-business categories across 11 groups**, 73 musician options, 32 genres, 35 markets, 488 cities. The **VENUES group has exactly four subcategories**: Auditoriums/Arenas; Clubs/Dancehalls/Small Venues; Concert halls/Performing arts centers; Stadiums/Amphitheaters/Fairgrounds.

36. **A filtering trap worth flagging: "Austin" and "Austin Area" are two separate market options.** Selecting only one undercounts the metro. Any future manual export must run both.

37. **No TMO registration counts are published anywhere we could verify.** Figures circulating in search results ("6,955 musicians", "1,900 DFW") trace to pages that now return HTTP 500 — stale search-index artifacts, rejected. The only count claim found, "15,000+ listings", comes from an advocacy nonprofit rather than TMO. **Do not cite a TMO directory count in the paper.**

38. **The TMO's own economic-impact numbers are usable but must be caveated.** *The 2024 Economic Impact of Music in Texas* (TXP Inc., Jan 2025, PDF saved here) reports **71,385 direct music-business jobs, $4.039B direct earnings, $11.327B direct revenue**, and total impact of **195,979 jobs / $10.484B earnings / $31.655B activity / $564M state tax**. Critically, **the report derives its job estimates from the TMO directory itself** — so the headline is registration-dependent — and derives revenue from the **2017 Economic Census**, now nine years old. The report's own text says direct jobs "stagnated" while wages rose.

## J. Data-quality findings that will otherwise bite

39. **Festival receipts are filed through venue-named shell entities and will wreck any naive venue trend.** `EMOS CONCESSION COMPANY, LLC` reported **$14,567,301 in Oct 2024** against an Emo's East median month of $116,344 (**125x**). `SCOOT INN CONCESSION COMPANY, LLC` reported **$12,540,139 in Oct 2025** against a Scoot Inn median of $38,434 (**326x**). Taken at face value, Scoot Inn would appear to have grown **1,358%** from 2019 to 2025. These are almost certainly ACL Festival receipts. Ten such rows are flagged and excluded.

40. **`cover_charge_receipts` is not a usable live-music series before about 2022.** The entire matched panel reported **$5,488 of cover charge in 2019 (0.01% of receipts)** versus **$2,085,610 in 2024 (1.62%)**. That is a reporting-compliance change, not growth in ticketed shows. Do not read a trend into it.

41. **BLS never published a CPI-U value for October 2025** (federal shutdown). October is the ACL Festival month and carries outsized receipts, so an unhandled gap silently erased roughly **$22M** of 2025 real receipts in an earlier cut of these tables. The value here is interpolated and flagged `cpi_imputed = 1`.

42. **The mixed-beverage file structurally omits beer-and-wine-only rooms.** Carousel Lounge, Meanwhile Brewing, and Whip In are absent for permit-type reasons, not because they closed. Cactus Cafe, Bass Concert Hall, and Central Presbyterian are invisible for lack of a separable alcohol permit; Geraldine's is buried inside the Hotel Van Zandt permit.

## Gaps and blockers

- **Red River Cultural District loading-zone advocacy: nothing found.** Not on the RRCD site; the search budget was exhausted before a targeted query ran. Largest open item.
- **The RRCD economic-impact PDF was never downloaded.** Its `$23.1M tax revenue` and `1,040 artists paid` figures come from press coverage, so methodology and multipliers are uninspected. It is a City-co-funded advocacy study measuring **gross** activity off a **2021 pandemic trough** — treat with care.
- **"Bring Music Home" publishes no closure counts.** It is a qualitative book/podcast project; secondary sources citing it for "200 venues shuttered" are misreading it.
- **No Music Venue Alliance Austin website, roster, member count, or post-2020 leadership exists.** All MVAA material is quotation via the Chronicle and Austin Monitor. NIVA publishes no Austin or Texas member counts.
- **No Live Music Fund dataset exists**, and the City's Cultural Funding Awards file has only 3 columns, so LMF awards cannot be separated from cultural-arts or heritage awards.
- **TABC license files cannot identify live-music venues** — neither `7hf9-qc9f` (125,902 rows) nor `kguh-7q9z` (78,187 rows) carries a premise-type or business-type field, and ~10,514 rows have a blank county.
- **TMO listings are unavailable.** `tmo_listings_austin.csv` holds the **40 Austin rows** recoverable from archive snapshots, and **none are venues** (VENUES sorts after COMMERCIAL MUSIC alphabetically on the unfiltered first page). It is evidence of record shape, not a census. A manual export is described step-by-step in `tmo_directory_notes.md`; requesting an extract directly from TMO is the faster path.
- **The 114-venue panel is curated, not a census.** It over-represents nameable venues in Red River, East Austin, and South Congress.
- **The `address` column in `venue_timeline.csv` is its weakest field** — many entries come from general listings rather than the cited article. Do not geocode without re-verification.
- **~37 venues from the target list have no timeline row** (Stay Gold, Sam's Town Point, White Horse, C-Boy's, Donn's Depot and others). Absence means "not found", not "nothing happened".
- **Alcohol receipts are a proxy for venue activity, not musician pay.** Nothing here measures the door split, guarantees, or what performers actually earned. That gap has to be closed from the Music Census and pay-survey beats.
