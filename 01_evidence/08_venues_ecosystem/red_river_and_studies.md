# 1. Red River Cultural District (RRCD)

**Site:** https://redriverculturaldistrict.org/ (retrieved 2026-08-01)

## 1.1 Formation and geography

- Approved by Austin City Council **October 17, 2013**.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Cultural_District_(Austin,_Texas) (retrieved 2026-08-01)
- The district historically covers the **600–900 blocks of Red River Street**. The associated Red River
  Merchants' Association, formed **February 2016**, extends across the 600–1200 blocks.
  Same source.
- The 2026 economic impact report describes the study area as running **Fifth to 15th Street**, which is
  wider than the Wikipedia block description. The two definitions are not identical; use the report's
  boundary when citing report figures.
  Source: https://communityimpact.com/austin/central-austin/business/2026/04/02/over-2b-in-local-impact-findings-from-red-river-cultural-districts-economic-impact-report/

## 1.2 Member and venue counts

| Count | Figure | Source | Retrieved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Members listed on RRCD site ("Things To Do") | **50** venues and businesses, including bars, live music venues, restaurants, four hotels, and cultural institutions | https://redriverculturaldistrict.org/ | 2026-08-01 |
| Live music venues, per 2026 impact report | **"over 12"** | Community Impact, 2026-04-02 | 2026-08-01 |
| Total businesses, per 2026 impact report | **"over 40"**, including hotels and retail | Community Impact, 2026-04-02 | 2026-08-01 |

**Caution on the "12 venues" figure.** The site's own listing of 50 members mixes venues, bars,
restaurants, hotels, and civic institutions. The report's "over 12 live music venues" is the number to
use for venue counts; the 40–50 figures are business counts for the district as a whole. Do not present
them as interchangeable.

**Report page:** https://redriverculturaldistrict.org/economic-impact-report/ (retrieved 2026-08-01)
**Author:** AngelouEconomics, funded in part by the City of Austin.
**Released:** April 2, 2026. **Study period:** 2021–2025.

| Measure | Figure |

| Direct tax revenue | **$23.1 million** (reported in secondary coverage; see caveat) |

Festival-level figures from the RRCD report page itself:

| Festival | Impact | FTE jobs | Labor income |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free Week | $2.2 million | 15 | $731,100 |

- Free Week and Hot Summer Nights together provided **paid opportunities to more than 1,040 artists in
  2025** (per secondary coverage of the report; see caveat below).
- 2024 Chronicle reporting: **125+ local artists across 14 venues over three days** of Hot Summer Nights.
  https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/why-venues-are-struggling-to-stay-open-in-the-live-music-capital-of-the-world-13283901/
- Hot Summer Nights 2026 dates listed on the RRCD site: **July 16, 17, 18, 2026**.

### Caveats on the impact report — read before citing

1. **The $23.1M tax revenue figure and the 1,040-artist figure came from secondary coverage
   (KXAN/Community Impact/CultureMap summaries), not from the report page I retrieved.** The RRCD report
   page I fetched did not surface either number. Verify against the downloadable PDF before publication.
2. **The full PDF was not downloaded or read.** All figures here are from the report's summary page plus
   press coverage. Methodology, multipliers, and the direct/indirect/induced split were not inspected.
3. **This is a commissioned advocacy study**, partly City-funded, produced by AngelouEconomics for the
   district it measures. Input-output impact studies of entertainment districts routinely count visitor
   spending that would have occurred elsewhere in the metro absent the district. The $2.3B figure is a
   gross-activity estimate, **not** a net welfare or net-to-Austin figure, and Texas 2036 should not
   present it as one.
4. **The 2021 baseline is a pandemic-depressed year.** The reported growth from ~$380M (2021) to ~$530M
   (2025) is measured off a trough. It is recovery, not necessarily trend growth, and the report's
   "post-pandemic resilience" framing should be attributed to the report rather than adopted.

## 1.4 RRCD financial stress

In October 2024 the Chronicle reported the district itself was in a financial crisis threatening both
Free Week and Hot Summer Nights, and was seeking a major sponsor or philanthropic backer.
Source: https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/closures-abound-for-beloved-austin-hangouts-and-more-culture-news-13284302/ (2024-10-24)

Public support identified:
- **Red River Special Revenue Fund: $150,000**, effective the fiscal year beginning October 2024.
- **$45,000** Texas Commission on the Arts grant for Hot Summer Nights, July 2024.
Both: Austin Chronicle, 2024-07-11.

Set against a district that reports half a billion dollars of annual economic activity, these are very
small numbers. That gap is a usable finding.

# 2. "Bring Music Home" (bringmusichome.com)

**Retrieved 2026-08-01:** https://bringmusichome.com/

## What it actually is

A **qualitative documentation project**, not a data source. It captures the stories of **more than 200
music venues across 30 U.S. cities** in the aftermath of the COVID-19 shutdowns, produced by a team of
60+ volunteer photographers, producers, and designers. Outputs are a limited-edition coffee table book,
a podcast, and a forthcoming docuseries. It partners with NIVA; poster proceeds benefit NIVA's emergency
relief fund.

## Austin coverage

Roughly **10 Austin venues** are featured, including Antone's, Barracuda, Continental Club, Emo's,
Stubb's, and Threadgill's.

## Critical caveat

**Bring Music Home publishes no venue closure counts and no Austin-specific closure statistics.** The
"200 venues" figure is the number of venues *documented*, not the number closed. This distinction is
frequently lost in secondary citation — a Billboard headline in the search results framed it as "200
music venues shuttered," which the site's own description does not support. **Do not cite Bring Music
Home as a closure-count source.** If the white paper needs national closure counts, use NIVA survey data
or SVOG administrative data instead.

# 3. National Independent Venue Association (NIVA)

**Site:** https://www.nivassoc.org/ (referenced via search results, 2026-08-01; site not directly fetched)

## Verified figures

- NIVA formed in **2020**, representing roughly **2,000 members** at the time of its COVID advocacy.
- **90% of independent venues** said they expected to close permanently within a few months absent
  federal funding — a poll of NIVA's own membership, 2020.
  Source: https://variety.com/2020/music/news/music-venues-foresee-permanent-closure-1234629793 (via search, 2026-08-01)
- The **Save Our Stages Act** became the **Shuttered Venue Operators Grant (SVOG)**, enacted December
  2020, nine months after NIVA's founding. SVOG delivered **$16.25 billion** to the independent live
  entertainment sector — described by NIVA as the largest arts investment in U.S. history.
- **Austin venues received roughly $113 million in COVID relief grants.**
  Source: https://www.axios.com/2021/09/29/austin-music-venues-113-million-covid-relief-grants
  **UNVERIFIED IN DETAIL** — this URL appeared in search results with that headline figure but was not
  directly fetched. Cross-check against SBA SVOG award data (see evidence folder 07_svog_federal_relief)
  before citing.

## Austin-relevant NIVA figures — **LARGELY NOT VERIFIED**

- I found **no published count of NIVA member venues in Austin or Texas.**
- The only named Austin-area NIVA figure located was **Lane Gosnay**, founder and executive director of
  The Bugle Boy (La Grange, TX — outside Austin).
- NIVA's 2025 "Live List" showcase was held at **Antone's Nightclub, March 12, 2025**, during SXSW.
- **Open item:** NIVA does not appear to publish a member directory by metro. If Austin membership counts
  are needed, contact NIVA directly.

# 4. Music Venue Alliance Austin (MVAA) and the "90%" claim

This is the item most at risk of being miscited, so the provenance is set out carefully.

## 4.1 There are TWO distinct 90% claims. They are routinely conflated.

### Claim A — Austin-specific, University of Houston Hobby School survey

> **90% of Austin's live music venues predicted to permanently close by Halloween 2020.**

- **Source:** https://austinmonitor.com/stories/2020/07/survey-predicts-90-percent-of-austin-live-music-venues-to-close-by-halloween/
  Austin Monitor, **published 2020-07-08**, retrieved 2026-08-01 (directly fetched).
- **Survey conducted by:** University of Houston, Hobby School of Public Affairs.
- **Sample:** over 1,000 business owners **across all industries**. The music-venue subsample size is
  **not stated** in the article — a real limitation on the Austin-specific figure.
- Accompanying venue findings: 19% could pay June 2020 rent in full; 67% paid less than half; 62% said
  they would survive four months or less; 83% laid off full-time staff; 79% suspended payments to
  vendors, suppliers, and landlords.

### Claim B — National, NIVA membership poll

> **90% of U.S. independent venues would close permanently within six months without federal aid.**

- Cited in Austin Chronicle, 2020-06-18, and in Variety, 2020 (see §3).
- Different population (national, NIVA members), different horizon (six months), different survey.

## 4.2 The critical caveat

**Both are conditional projections, not outcomes, and the condition did not hold.** Federal relief
arrived: SVOG, $16.25 billion nationally, ~$113 million to Austin venues. The mass extinction did not
occur. Austin's documented 2020 closure cohort was roughly **11 named venues** (Threadgill's, Shady
Grove, Barracuda, Plush, Scratchouse, the North Door, One-2-One Bar, B.D. Riley's, Dirty Dog Bar, the
Townsend, Buffalo Billiards), plus DIY spaces such as Unit 108 and Spider House Cafe — a serious loss,
but nowhere near 90% of the venue stock.

**Recommendation:** if the white paper uses the 90% figure at all, present it as a contemporaneous
projection used to justify federal intervention, and pair it with the actual outcome. Presenting it as
what happened would be wrong, and it is the kind of error a hostile reader will find.

## 4.3 MVAA attributable statements

**Rebecca Reynolds**, founder, Music Venue Alliance Austin:

- On Barracuda's closure (Austin Chronicle, 2020-06-18):
  > "Barracuda is a really big deal, and it's frightening."
  Full context in the article: she reported speaking daily with owners of legendary Austin venues who
  did not see how they would reopen.
- On the pace of relief (same article): she noted venues had been told since March that help was coming
  and still lacked access to emergency disaster relief.
- On preservation politics (Austin Chronicle, 2020-09-24): "Austinites are paying very close attention."
- On sector ranking (Austin Monitor, 2020-07-08): venues were, in her words, at the top of every category
  for hardest hit, imminent closure, and layoffs.

**Also useful, adjacent attributions:**
- **Cody Cowan**, RRCD executive director (Austin Monitor, 2020-07-08):
  > "I feel like we're playing Frogger and just leaping from one disaster relief pad to the next."
- **Stephen Sternschein**, co-owner, Parish and Empire (Austin Monitor, 2020-07-08), on relief adequacy:
  > "We all know that what these folks need is like 10 times that."

## 4.4 What could not be verified about MVAA

- **No MVAA website, membership roster, or published member count was located.** All MVAA material here
  is quotation via the Chronicle and Austin Monitor.
- **No MVAA statement giving a specific count of Austin venues closed was found.** The closure lists in
  this evidence set come from Chronicle and KUT reporting, not from MVAA.
- Whether MVAA is still active in 2026, and who leads it, was **not established.** Reynolds is
  consistently identified as founder in 2020 coverage only.

# 5. City of Austin venue preservation and music funding programs

## 5.1 Iconic Venue Fund

- **Established:** late 2020.
- **Funded by:** hotel occupancy tax revenue plus a 2018 bond package.
- **Eligibility:** venues reflecting Austin's "unique character and brand," per the founding ordinance.
- **Priority venues named in the founding resolution:** Victory Grill, Broken Spoke, Continental Club,
  Hole in the Wall, Antone's, Stubb's.
- **Demand:** **45 proposals requesting over $300 million**; **14 venues** short-listed.
- **First and (as documented here) only award:** **Hole in the Wall**, **$1.6 million**, **August 15,
  2023**, enabling a **20-year lease**.

Source: https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/2023-08-25/what-hole-in-the-walls-freshly-inked-20-year-lease-means-for-other-at-risk-venues/
Austin Chronicle, Genevieve Wood, 2023-08-24 (directly fetched 2026-08-01).

**Two findings worth surfacing.** First, roughly **three years elapsed** between the fund's creation
(late 2020) and its first dollar out the door (August 2023) — during which the closures in the timeline
continued. Second, **$300 million requested against $1.6 million awarded** is a ratio of roughly 190:1.
Even discounting request inflation heavily, the instrument is small relative to stated need.

**UNVERIFIED:** whether any Iconic Venue Fund awards were made after Hole in the Wall. I found none, but
absence of evidence here is weak — I did not reach a City program page listing awards.

## 5.2 Live Music Fund

- **Established 2019**, paid for by hotel occupancy tax.
- **First round (2023):** **$3.5 million** distributed; **368 recipients** from **660 applicants**;
  grants of $5,000 and $10,000.
- **2024 restructure:** individual artist grants raised from the $5,000–$10,000 band to
  **$15,000–$30,000**; a **new venue grant tier of $30,000–$60,000** was added. The venue tier is the
  substantive change — earlier rounds funded artists, not rooms.
  Source: https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/why-venues-are-struggling-to-stay-open-in-the-live-music-capital-of-the-world-13283901/ (2024-07-11, directly fetched)
- **2026 round:** approximately **$7–7.14 million to 399 grantees**, spanning venues, professional
  musicians, and independent promoters.
  Sources (via search results, 2026-08-01, **not directly fetched**):
  https://www.austintexasmusicians.org/blog/2026-live-music-fund-recipients ;
  https://www.austintexas.gov/atxmusic/live-music-fund

## 5.3 Broader City arts funding context (2026)

- City of Austin announced approximately **$24 million** in arts and culture grants for 2026, and
  separately launched **$23 million** in grants for the creative community.
- **Heritage Preservation Grant: $3 million across 22 projects**, including capital improvements to
  historic assets. **The Broken Spoke** is a named recipient.
  Sources (via search results, 2026-08-01, **not directly fetched**):
  https://www.austintexas.gov/communications/news/city-austin-announces-2026-cultural-funding-awards ;
  https://austin.culturemap.com/news/arts/acme-grants-musicians-venues/ ;
  https://www.kxan.com/news/local/austin/broken-spoke-among-austins-heritage-preservation-grant-recipients/

## 5.4 Rally Austin (property acquisition support)

- **October 2024:** Rally Austin lent the Empire Control Room & Garage operators **$2.2 million** to
  purchase the Garage property at 604 E. Seventh St. and expand.
  Source: https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/rally-austin-lends-empire-control-room-team-22-million-to-buy-garage-expand-13286511/
- **January 2026 follow-up:** Empire was disputing **$103,701.77** in delinquent property taxes accrued
  since the 2024 acquisition, had cut roughly **20% of staff** in December 2025 (15 of 45+ people), and
  faced two civil suits alleging misappropriation of funds and commingled accounts.
  Source: https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/a-threat-to-the-empire/ (Caroline Drew, 2026-01-15,
  directly fetched)

**This pairing is the single most policy-relevant sequence in the evidence set.** Austin's largest
venue-preservation intervention to date, a $2.2M acquisition loan, was followed within about 14 months
by layoffs, tax delinquency, and litigation at the recipient. Real-estate support does not by itself
repair operating economics. Any recommendation for acquisition-based preservation should address
post-award monitoring and operating viability, not just ownership.

## 5.5 COVID-era City relief (for contrast)

As of September 2020, total City relief to venues, studios, and production companies was **under
$1 million**. Seven venues received more than $40,000 each: Antone's, Continental Club, Mohawk, Cheer Up
Charlies, Hotel Vegas, Hole in the Wall, Venue ATX. The Creative Worker Relief Grant reached
**1,866 recipients, 42% from the music industry**.

Against reported fixed costs of ~$18,000/month (Continental Club) and ~$40,000/month (Venue ATX), the
whole City program was worth weeks of runway for a single mid-sized room.
Source: https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/2020-09-25/faster-than-sound-its-now-or-never-for-a-music-venue-preservation-fund/ (directly fetched)

**UNVERIFIED:** a KVUE headline indicates Austin City Council approved **$15 million** in COVID relief
spanning music venues, arts organizations, child care providers, and restaurants
(https://www.kvue.com/article/money/austin-live-music-venue-relief-arts-child-care-providers-coronavirus/269-07911dad-6a15-4086-be2c-c27abe300f2d).
The venue-specific share of that $15M was not established and the article was not fetched. Do not cite
the $15M as venue funding.
