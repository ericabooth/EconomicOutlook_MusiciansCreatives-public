## Summary

Three closures in a single month, each with a different mechanism, plus a warning about the district's
festival programming.

**The Lost Well** — 2421 Webberville Rd., closed October 27, 2024 after 11 years. Heavy metal and punk
venue, owner Marcello Murphy. The landlord sold the property in connection with the I-35 expansion;
the club was given 60 days to vacate.

**Stars Cafe** — 3101 N. I-35, closed November 10, 2024 after more than 50 years, displaced directly
by the I-35 expansion project. Planned to relocate.

**Club Eternal** — 207 W. Fourth St., closed November 9, 2024. DIY dance venue run by Matthew Shogi
and Alexandra Damalas. Lease renewal offered on terms the article characterizes as
"less-than-favorable." Final event featured Detroit techno artist 2Lanes.

**Red River Cultural District:** reported to be in a financial crisis threatening both of its annual
free festivals, Free Week and Hot Summer Nights, and seeking a major sponsor or philanthropic backer.

## Relevance

The **I-35 expansion** is a distinct displacement driver from rent or private redevelopment, and it
appears twice in one month here. It is a state highway project, not a market outcome, which makes it
directly policy-relevant for a Texas 2036 audience and worth separating from the rent narrative in any
analysis.

Note the Lost Well did not stay closed: it reopened at 1141 Airport Blvd. in August 2025. Coding this
as a permanent loss would be wrong.

One discrepancy to flag: KUT's 2024 year-end list attributes the Lost Well closure to the landlord not
renewing the lease, while the Chronicle attributes it to the landlord selling for the I-35 expansion.
Both agree the landlord initiated it.
