## Summary

This is the traceable source for the widely repeated "90% of Austin venues will close" claim. It is
important to get its provenance right, because the number is often quoted without its conditions.

**Survey:** University of Houston Hobby School of Public Affairs.
**Sample:** over 1,000 business owners across all industries (music venues are a subgroup, and the
venue-specific subsample size is not given in this article).

Findings reported for music venues:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Predicted to permanently close by Halloween 2020 | 90% |
| Able to pay June 2020 rent in full | 19% |
| Paid less than half of rent owed | 67% |
| Said they would survive four months or less of continued closure | 62% |
| Laid off full-time employees | 83% |
| Suspended payments to vendors, suppliers, landlords | 79% |

## Important caveat for citation

The 90% figure is a **projection conditional on no further relief**, not an outcome. Federal relief
did arrive: the Save Our Stages Act became the Shuttered Venue Operators Grant in December 2020,
delivering $16.25 billion nationally. Austin venues drew roughly $113 million (Axios, 2021-09-29).
The projected mass extinction did not occur. Any use of "90%" in the white paper should state the
conditionality and the counterfactual, or it will misrepresent the evidence.

Note also that a separate national 90%-in-six-months figure from NIVA circulated at the same time.
The two are distinct claims that are frequently conflated.
