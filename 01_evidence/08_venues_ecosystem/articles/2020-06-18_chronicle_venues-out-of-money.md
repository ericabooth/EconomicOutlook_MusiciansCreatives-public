## Summary

Written at the moment the COVID closure wave broke. Barracuda, Scratchouse, and Plush had all just
announced permanent closure within days of each other, all on the East Seventh / Red River corridor.
Threadgill's, Shady Grove, and the Townsend had already gone. DIY space Unit 108 on Bastrop Highway
closed citing COVID plus an uncooperative landlord.

Two statistics anchor the piece:

- Surveyed Austin venues held an average of **30 days of operational savings**.
- Nationally, **90% of venues** were projected not to survive six months of closure absent federal aid
  (the NIVA figure).

The article also supplies the structural backstory: Barracuda and Scratchouse occupied the very
spaces vacated by Holy Mountain and Red 7 after the 2015 rent hikes. The corridor had already been
through one displacement cycle before the pandemic arrived; COVID hit tenants who were newer,
thinner-capitalized, and paying post-increase rents.

## Quote

MVAA founder Rebecca Reynolds: "Barracuda is a really big deal, and it's frightening."

## Relevance

Source for the 30-days-of-savings figure and for the MVAA leadership voice. Establishes that the 2020
closures were not a clean natural experiment: the venues that died had already been selected for
fragility by the 2015-2016 rent cycle.
