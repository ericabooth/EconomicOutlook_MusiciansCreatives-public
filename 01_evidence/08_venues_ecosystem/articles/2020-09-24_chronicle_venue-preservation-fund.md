## Summary

A mid-pandemic accounting of what Austin had actually delivered to its music sector, set against the
scale of loss.

**Venues confirmed permanently closed by September 2020:** Threadgill's, Shady Grove, Barracuda,
Plush, Scratchouse, the North Door, One-2-One Bar, B.D. Riley's, Dirty Dog Bar, the Townsend,
Buffalo Billiards.

**City relief actually disbursed at that point:** under $1 million total across venues, studios, and
production companies. Seven venues received more than $40,000 each: Antone's, Continental Club,
Mohawk, Cheer Up Charlies, Hotel Vegas, Hole in the Wall, and Venue ATX.

**Scale mismatch, in the article's own numbers:** the Continental Club reported roughly $18,000/month
in fixed costs; Venue ATX roughly $40,000/month. Under $1 million spread across the whole sector was
therefore worth on the order of weeks, not months, for a single mid-sized room.

**Adjacent program:** the Creative Worker Relief Grant reached 1,866 recipients, 42% of them from the
music industry.

City staff were directed to report back by October 1, 2020 with venue support recommendations. No
dedicated preservation fund existed yet. The Iconic Venue Fund was established later in 2020 but did
not make its first award until August 2023.

## Quote

MVAA founder Rebecca Reynolds said "Austinites are paying very close attention."

## Relevance

Best single list of the 2020 closure cohort, and the clearest documentation of the gap between
municipal relief and venue burn rates. The nearly three-year lag between the Iconic Venue Fund's
creation and its first disbursement is itself a finding.
