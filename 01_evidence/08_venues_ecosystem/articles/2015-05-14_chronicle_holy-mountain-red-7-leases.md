## Summary

The clearest documented case of rent displacing Austin music venues. Two adjacent East Seventh Street
clubs, both inside the Red River Cultural District, were told their leases would renew at sharply
higher rates when the existing terms expired October 1, 2015.

Rent figures reported:

| Venue | Address | Opened | Rent then | Rent proposed | Increase |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Holy Mountain | 617 E. Seventh St. | 2012 | $5,500/mo | $8,000/mo | +45% |
| Red 7 | 611 E. Seventh St. | 2006 | $9,000/mo | $14,000/mo | +56% |

Both figures were base rent only. On top of them sat a triple-net charge covering property taxes,
building insurance, and maintenance, so the effective increase to the operators was larger than the
base-rent delta implies. Holy Mountain partner James Taylor and Red 7 partner Jared Cannon both said
the numbers did not work for a music venue's margins.

The article makes a point worth carrying into the white paper: the Red River Cultural District,
created by City Council in 2013 partly to protect these clubs, raised the desirability of the
surrounding blocks and therefore contributed to the land-value pressure now pushing them out. That is
a direct policy feedback loop, not a coincidence.

Beerland owner Randall Stockton is quoted acknowledging the same exposure, noting music venues do not
generate the margin to absorb increases of that size.

## Outcome

Both venues closed in fall 2015. Holy Mountain's last show was September 27, 2015. Red 7's partners
went on to open The Sidewinder in the former Red Eyed Fly space at 715 Red River. The 611 E. Seventh
space became Barracuda in 2016; the 617 space became Scratchouse. Both successor venues closed in
June 2020.

## Quote

Taylor called the proposed increase "completely unsustainable."
