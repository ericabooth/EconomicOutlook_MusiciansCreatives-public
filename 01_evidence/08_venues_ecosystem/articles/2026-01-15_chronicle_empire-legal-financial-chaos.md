## Summary

The most recent detailed account of distress at a major Austin venue, and a caution about how public
support gets evaluated.

**Empire Control Room & Garage**, 604-606 E. Seventh St., opened 2013.

Timeline of support and distress:

- **2018-2019:** the landlord negotiated to sell to World Class Capital's Nate Paul, who had bought
  and immediately shuttered dance clubs Kingdom and Ethics Music Lounge in July 2018. After Chronicle
  coverage, Paul withdrew. Operators subsequently bought the land.
- **October 2024:** Rally Austin lent the operators **$2.2 million** to purchase the Garage property
  and expand. This was publicly framed as securing the venue's future.
- **December 2025:** roughly 20% of staff cut — 15 employees and contractors, with 30+ remaining.
- **January 2026:** **$103,701.77** in property taxes delinquent (disputed by the venue), accrued
  since the 2024 acquisition. Two civil suits pending, from AdVentures executives and from investor
  Andrew Sernovitz, alleging misappropriation of funds, commingled corporate accounts, and property
  title problems. History of late or missed employee paychecks and unpaid amounts to independent
  booking collectives.

The venue remained operational at publication.

## Quote

"Times are rough for venues – bar sales have plummeted and we can't afford in-house security."

## Relevance

Directly relevant to evaluating Austin's venue-preservation instruments. A $2.2 million public-backed
acquisition loan in October 2024 was followed within roughly 14 months by layoffs, delinquent taxes,
and litigation. Whatever the merits of the individual disputes, the sequence shows that property
acquisition support does not by itself fix operating economics, and that outcome tracking after an
award matters. It also shows the bar-sales decline cited across the sector hitting a venue that had
just received the largest form of help the City offers.

Note the falsifying detail on the other side: the same operator survived the 2018-2019 Nate Paul
threat, and Kingdom and Ethics did not. Ownership of the real estate did prevent that specific
failure mode.
