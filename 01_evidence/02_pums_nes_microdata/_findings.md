# Findings — ACS PUMS + Nonemployer Statistics evidence pull
Retrieved/computed 2026-08-01. All PUMS dollar figures are **nominal/unadjusted** (no ADJINC applied; see `_sources.md`). All are **descriptive tabulations**, not modeled estimates, and unweighted N's are small for some cells, so treat them as directional rather than precision estimates.

**Population used in items 3 through 7:** employed persons aged 18 to 64 (`ESR` in {1,2,4,5}, `AGEP` between 18 and 64), which is the same population as the all-Texas baseline those items compare against. The occupation extract as saved applies no age or labor-force filter, so this restriction has to be added before any comparison against the baseline file; see `_sources.md`.

1. **Sample base:** the 2020-2024 5-Year ACS PUMS Texas person file contains 1,326,295 unweighted person records. Of these, 5,694 (0.43%) report one of the 9 target creative OCCP codes as their current/primary occupation, representing a weighted estimate of ~120,572 Texans.

2. **Musicians and Singers (OCCP 2752) in Texas:** 885 unweighted records -> weighted estimate ~19,256 people statewide who report this as their current or most recent occupation (2020-2024 average). Restricted to those employed and aged 18 to 64, the population used in items 3 through 7, the count is 500 unweighted and ~12,081 weighted. Music Directors and Composers (OCCP 2751, a distinct, smaller and more institutional occupation covering school, church, and university directors) is separately estimated at 196 unweighted / ~4,193 weighted.

3. **Self-employment gap:** **51.5%** of Musicians and Singers (OCCP 2752) are classified COW=6 or 7 (self-employed, incorporated or unincorporated), against **9.8%** for the all-Texas 18-64 employed baseline (weighted N=13,595,139). Pooling Musicians and Singers with Music Directors and Composers (OCCP 2751+2752) gives **47.2%**, and the 9-occupation creative group as a whole is **34.0%**. Self-employment across these creative occupations runs 3.5x (9-occupation group) to 5.2x (Musicians and Singers) the statewide worker rate.

4. **Income gap:** weighted median total personal income (PINCP, nominal) for Musicians and Singers is **$25,000**, about 56% of the $45,000 median for the all-worker 18-64 baseline. Median personal earnings alone (PERNP, wages plus self-employment income, excluding investment, retirement, and other income) is **$21,000**, or 48% of the baseline worker's $43,700. Working-age employed musicians earn less than half what the median Texas worker earns. The gap between their total income and their earnings is $4,000, so most of a working musician's personal income is earned rather than coming from investment, retirement, or other non-work sources. Both PINCP and PERNP are personal, not household, measures: neither captures a spouse's earnings, so any claim about how a musician household gets by needs the housing-file join in item 13 rather than these two variables.

5. **Hours and weeks:** median usual hours worked per week (WKHP) for Musicians and Singers is **30 hours**, against 40 hours for the baseline worker, alongside a **52-week** median for weeks worked in the past year (WKWN, the same as the baseline). The pattern is part-time hours worked year-round rather than seasonal work.

6. **Music Directors and Composers earn more than twice what Musicians and Singers earn:** median PINCP is **$55,600** against **$25,000**, a ratio of 2.2. This likely reflects that "Music Director/Composer" captures more salaried, institutional roles (school, university, church, media) than the freelance and gig-heavy "Musicians and Singers" bucket.

7. **Austin MSA punches above its weight for musicians:** using the verified 2020-PUMA-to-county crosswalk, the Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos MSA (Travis/Williamson/Hays/Bastrop/Caldwell) accounts for an estimated ~2,126 of the state's ~12,081 Musicians and Singers (**17.6%** of the statewide total), while the Austin MSA holds only about **9.4%** of Texas's 18-64 employed baseline workforce (1,280,953 of 13,595,139 weighted). Austin is 1.9x over-represented in musicians relative to its share of the overall workforce.

   **Austin's musicians are more self-employed than musicians statewide:** **72.2%** of Austin MSA Musicians and Singers are COW=6 or 7, against 51.5% for musicians statewide and 9.8% for the all-Texas baseline worker. Unweighted N is 77, so read the level as directional; the Austin-versus-statewide direction is the more durable part.

8. **The two music occupation codes are easy to transpose, so verify them against the data dictionary before use.** OCCP **2751 = Music Directors and Composers** and OCCP **2752 = Musicians and Singers**. The other seven target codes are Graphic Designers 2634, Photographers 2910, Actors 2700, Producers/Directors 2710, Dancers/Choreographers 2740, Writers/Authors 2850, and Other Media and Communication Equipment Workers 2905. All 9 were checked directly against the 2020-2024 dictionary saved in this folder.

9. **No PUMS code exists for "sound engineer" specifically.** The closest available OCCP code (2905, "Other Media and Communication Equipment Workers") is a combined bucket for audio/video techs, broadcast techs, sound engineering techs, and lighting techs — even the more granular SOCP variable collapses these into one aggregate code. In Texas this combined bucket is 514 unweighted records / ~11,719 weighted. Any write-up referencing "sound engineers" from this data should describe it as this broader combined category, not sound engineers alone.

10. **Nonemployer (gig/solo) growth in independent-artist establishments, Travis vs. Harris, 2012-2023 (NAICS 7115, Independent Artists, Writers, and Performers):** Travis County nonemployer establishments grew from 5,382 to 8,696 (**+61.6%**), versus Harris County's 7,470 to 11,067 (**+48.2%**) over the same 11 years. Travis grew faster in relative terms despite Harris being the larger metro in absolute establishment count throughout.

11. **Statewide NES growth, same NAICS 7115, 2012-2023:** Texas nonemployer establishments in independent-artist/writer/performer businesses grew from 45,021 to 74,834 (**+66.2%**), and reported receipts grew (nominal, not inflation-adjusted) from $787.7 million to $1.86 billion (**+136.1%**) — receipts growth well outpacing establishment growth, implying rising average receipts per nonemployer business (again, nominal dollars only; some of this is plain inflation, not real growth).

12. **NES dip in 2020, then sharp rebound:** statewide NAICS 7115 establishments dipped slightly in 2020 (59,846, down from 64,011 in 2019) before rebounding to 66,985 (2021), 74,152 (2022), and 74,834 (2023) — consistent with a pandemic-era shock to independent performers followed by a boom in gig/solo-artist formalization.

13. **Housing cost-burden data is pulled and ready but not yet joined.** 606,114 Texas household records with GRPIP (rent burden %), OCPIP (owner cost burden %), TEN (tenure), and GRNTP (gross rent $) are saved; join to the occupation extract via `SERIALNO` in the analysis phase (not done here per data-collection-only scope).

14. **Small-sample caveat:** several of the target occupation x geography cells are thin even after pooling 5 years. On the employed-18-to-64 population used in items 3 through 7, Austin MSA Musicians and Singers is only **77** unweighted records and statewide Musicians and Singers is 500, so the Austin figures are the thinnest numbers in this folder. Any Austin-only breakdown by race, age, or similar will have wide margins of error; consider using the PWGTP1-80 replicate weights (available in the raw source file, dropped from the curated extract for size) if the paper needs formal confidence intervals.

15. **Both target APIs (ACS PUMS microdata API and Nonemployer Statistics API) are now key-gated** — confirmed directly via HTTP headers (`X-DataWebAPI-KeyError: 1`) — so this pull used Census's bulk downloadable files instead (fully public, no key, same underlying data).
