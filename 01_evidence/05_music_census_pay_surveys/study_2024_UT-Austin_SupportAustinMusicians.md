# Support Austin Musicians — UT Austin research project (2024)

- **Source:** https://supportaustinmusicians.org/final-summary/ (Final Summary page)
- **Affiliation:** University of Texas at Austin; contact ATXmusicians@utexas.edu; UT professor **Eric Drott** named on the site. Site references the UT School of Music.
- **Vintage:** Drafted July 2024; cites 2024 awards and April 2024 housing data.
- **Retrieved:** 2026-08-01
- **Method:** Qualitative — interviews with **nearly 60 individuals** across genres (blues, country, hip hop, rock, afrobeat, techno, Latin), plus synthesis of existing City and census data. **Not a probability survey.** Many figures are re-reported from other sources rather than independently measured; treat this as a secondary compilation with primary interview color.

**Why it matters:** This is the closest thing to a post-2022 Austin musician study, and it is the main "UT Austin study" the brief asked for. It supplies several numbers with no other public source — notably the HAAM member income distribution and the SXSW/ACL artist pay rates — and it is the source of the current $200/hour City baseline framing.

---

## Income and pay

> "average pay for live performances in Austin hasn't changed in the past 40 years"

> "most citing $100 [as] the typical rate paid per musician for a set"

> reported incomes "ranging from $15,000...to $120,000 per year"; "most common income reported was $30,000-35,000"

> "**75% of HAAM members make less than $30,000 per year**"

> "57% of them reported earning barely enough or not enough to get by"

> "34% reported being worried about affording food"

> "City of Austin's baseline rate of **$200 per person per hour**" (updated 2019)

> "SXSW artist payments range from **$100 for a solo artist to $250 for a band** per performance"

> "full-time musicians typically perform between **2-4 shows a week**"; part-time "1-2 shows a week or as little as a show every two weeks"

Living-wage benchmark used in the recommendations: a livable wage in Austin is "currently calculated in Austin as about **$58,000 a year**."

**Note the tension with the 2014 census.** The most common income reported here is $30,000–$35,000, well above the 2014 census picture where 68.4% earned under $10,000 from music. These are not contradictory — the census figure is music-only income and this one appears to be total income from a small interview sample — but the two must not be presented as a time series. The $100/set figure is consistent with KUT's $50–$100 range.

## Health coverage

> "**17% of Austin musicians**" are HAAM members; "members receive an average of an **$8,000-10,000 healthcare cost offset per year**"

> "43% of musicians surveyed receive health insurance through an employer"; "14% through a family member's plan"; "16% pay themselves for an insurance plan"; "**16% have no insurance**"

## Housing and cost of living

> median home cost "rose from **$295,000 in 2019**...to over $630,000"; "April 2024 data places it at **$564,995**" against a "national average of $408,732"

> "percentage of the housing market affordable for the average single family fell from **20% in 1990 to just 2%**"

> "less than 10% of new housing built during that period was priced for those making under the median household income"

> "2023 MFI for Austin is **$122,300**"; "majority of Austin's musicians would qualify as low-income (80% or less of MFI)"

> Foundation Communities: "**900 applications for 100 units**"

> Parking: "It's $35 to park my car" (Rainey Street); musicians "lose **10% or more of their total income** paying for parking"

## Festivals

> SXSW economic impact "ranged from **$350 mil. in 2018 to $381 million in 2023**"; ACL "increased...from **$264 million in 2018 to $499 million in 2023**"; combined "annual economic impact of nearly $1 billion per year"

> SXSW Music 2024: "**1,248 official acts**...**422 (or 33%) were international**"; "more than **80 locals boycotting** the festival"

> ACL "features more than 140 acts over two weekends"; in 2023 "**22 (or about 15%) of the 140 ACL acts featured were local**"

> "payments made to all artists total roughly **$28 million annually**" (attribution ambiguous on the page — verify before citing)

## Climate as an economic variable

> 2023: "more than **80 days over 100F, 40 of which topped 105F**"

> a band's "average monthly performances drop from about **12-14 in the winter and spring to 2-3 during summer months**"

This is a distinctive and underused finding — heat is a direct income shock for outdoor-dependent performers, and it is not captured in either music census.

## Live Music Fund

> "2023, the LMF provided funds directly to **368 artists**"; 2024 awards expanded to include venues and promoters

## Post-pandemic recovery

> "almost **30% of respondents** suggested that they have yet to achieve the same level of performance activity as before the pandemic" (consistent with the 2022 census's 29% "not recovered")

## Demographics of the music workforce (as characterized)

> "overall rat[i]o of male to female performers is about 2:1"; "roughly 60% identify as white, 15% as Hispanic, 6% as Black, 4% as Asian"; "60% of musicians are renters, 40% home owners"

These track the 2022 census closely, which suggests the page is reusing census figures rather than reporting independent measurements.

## Policy recommendations (condensed; 15 sections on the source page)

- **Structural over one-time:** "place less emphasis on one-time awards and instead attempt broader structural support"
- **New revenue:** a "Music Investment Fund" or "Cultural Sustainability Fund" funded by business-sector contributions
- **Direct income:** direct payments to reach a livable wage (~$58,000/yr); guaranteed income programs; a campaign urging venues to pay $200/hr; a matching fund doubling earnings up to a ceiling; a retirement fund paying performers over 65 an extra $2,000/month
- **Housing:** dedicated artist communities near entertainment districts; City purchase and conversion of class-C hotels/motels; workforce-housing models; use HAAM eligibility criteria to means-test; subsidized single-family sales to artists; an "Affordable Real Estate for Artists Initiative (AREA)" model
- **City events:** require paid live performance at City activities where possible
- **Venues:** City role in establishing/managing/booking more stages beyond the airport and City Hall; family-friendly, non-alcohol-dependent locations; an Armadillo World Headquarters-style recreation
- **Festivals:** raise local representation at SXSW and ACL
- **Portal:** a single website linking all available musician support
- **Training:** financial literacy, touring management, budgeting, copyright/licensing, retirement planning; formalized mentorship
- **Transportation:** free/subsidized parking via ParkATX passes, rideshare credits, venue-provided parking, more buses to entertainment districts, "gigging busses," expansion of ATX Musicians' "Gig Buddy"
- **Nonprofits:** sustained (not episodic) funding for HAAM, SIMS, HOME, and Foundation Communities
- **Film:** incentives for local composers/performers via the Texas Moving Image Industry Incentive Program
- **Grants:** community navigators from underrepresented communities; partner with Austin Texas Musicians, DAWA, Women in Music Professions
- **Climate plan:** earlier or after-dark start times; shade, misters, fans, water; subsidize indoor performance in the hottest months
- **Further study:** promoters and booking agents; festival payment structures; direct-to-musician vs. venue funding; a venue rating system; John Pointer's Live Music Capital Foundation venue-guarantee model

## Caveats

- No named lead author or formal peer review; a project website rather than a published report.
- ~60 interviews, non-random, with no published sampling frame or weighting. Percentages drawn from the interview pool should be described as "among roughly 60 musicians interviewed," never as Austin-wide.
- Several statistics are re-reported from HAAM, the 2022 census, and news coverage without inline citation. The **75% of HAAM members under $30,000** figure is the most valuable unique number here and should be confirmed directly with HAAM before publication.
- The "$28 million annually" artist-payment figure has ambiguous attribution on the page. Do not cite without tracing.
