## Why it matters

The primary news account of the 2022 census. Two things matter most for a pay-focused paper: the **sample collapsed** from the 2015 round (about 2,200 vs about 3,900 respondents), and the **2022 instrument dropped the annual-earnings questions**, which is why no 2022 successor to the "$15,000 or less" figure exists.

## Key figures

**Sample**
- **~2,200 respondents** to the 2022 survey.
- A decrease of **~1,700 respondents** versus the 2015 census (implying ~3,900 in 2015).
- Three respondent groups: music creatives (performers, songwriters, producers); music industry professionals; venue owners / live music presenters.

**Retention and intent**
- **84%** of music professionals plan to continue in music over the next three years.
- Only **64%** plan to remain in Austin.

**Cost of living context**
- Austin metro population grew from **1.6 million (2014)** to **2.2 million (2022)**.
- Median home prices rose from **~$240,000 (2014)** to **~$450,000 (2022)**.
- City of Austin median price **~$525,000 (December 2022)**.

**Geographic displacement**
- **10% drop** in musicians living in Austin's 10 most central ZIP codes over eight years.
- Growth in musician populations in San Marcos and Bastrop.

**Government support**
- Only **26%** received COVID relief through their music job.
- **80%** said government support was a first-time experience.
- **84%** expressed interest in receiving grants.

## Key excerpts

> "There's such a vibrant music scene here in Austin. This is a deal killer—if people can't even, like, afford to live month-to-month somewhere." —Peter Schwarz, consultant on the census

> The census found musicians had diversified income sources relative to 2015, earning "from a greater range of categories, and they're doing it more consistently than they did in 2014."

## Caution

Note the mixed vintage labels in the source: the prior census is referred to as both "2014" and "2015" (fieldwork vs. release). Fix the vintage convention before citing. The **10%** central-ZIP decline here and the **12%** figure in the Austin Chronicle's write-up of the same census are not identical — reconcile against the census document itself.
