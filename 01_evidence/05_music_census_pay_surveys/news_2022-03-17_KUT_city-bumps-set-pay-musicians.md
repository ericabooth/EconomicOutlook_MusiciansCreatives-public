## Why it matters

This is the actual source of the **$150** figure that later gets loosely attached to the "unchanged since the 1980s" claim. The $150 is the City of Austin Economic Development Department's own standard booking rate, per musician per hour, in force **since 2016** — not a market-wide venue rate and not a 1980s rate. This article documents the increase to $200.

## Key excerpts

> Previous pay rate: **$150 per musician per hour**, in effect **since 2016**.

> New pay rate: **$200 per musician per hour**.

> Department: City of Austin **Economic Development Department, Music and Entertainment Division**.

> Scope: applies only to city-sponsored performances; the City does not regulate what private venues pay.

> "Our commitment to the standard rate of pay is a great opportunity for everyone to learn ab[out]…" —Stephanie Bergara, Music and Entertainment Division

> "What we can do is, say, when you play a City of Austin event that has a city logo and is…" —Stephanie Bergara

> Bergara's rationale, on musicians being asked to "play for the same amount of money they would have played for 10 years ago because that's [the going rate]."

> "We're hoping that all of this work that we're doing will inspire other city departments…" —Stephanie Bergara

## Additional implementation details

- The increase was recommended by the **Austin Music Commission** on a **unanimous** vote.
- First showcase at the new rate: a Spaceflight Records SXSW event at Auditorium Shores.
- Non-cash terms bundled with the rate: the City provides a **full PA system**, and imposes **no radius clauses**.
- Next phase: encourage adoption by the **airport (ABIA), Parks and Recreation, and Capital Metro**.

## Provenance caution

Note the unit: **per musician per hour**, not per gig. A four-piece band playing a two-hour city set at the $200 rate is a materially different figure from "$200 a gig." Any use of the $150 or $200 numbers should carry the unit and the city-booking-only scope.
