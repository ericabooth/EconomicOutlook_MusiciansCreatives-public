# Faster Than Sound: Recapping the Year SXSW Got Canceled

- **Title:** Faster Than Sound: Recapping the Year SXSW Got Canceled (subtitle: Venue closures, fundraising, and industry layoffs headlined Austin music's difficult 2020)
- **Outlet:** The Austin Chronicle
- **Author:** Rachel Rascoe
- **Publication date:** 2020-12-30 (issue dated 2021-01-01)
- **URL:** https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/2021-01-01/recapping-the-year-sxsw-got-canceled/
- **Retrieval date:** 2026-08-01

## Key excerpts

> South by Southwest was canceled for the first time in its 34-year history after the city declared a local state of disaster due to COVID-19.

> SXSW's insurance did not cover pandemic-related cancellation. The organization laid off roughly one-third of staff.

> C3 Presents, a major local concert promoter, underwent substantial reductions affecting most employees except leadership. Texas' largest independent concert promoter, Margin Walker Presents, closed in December.

> Eighteen music venues shuttered in 2020, including Threadgill's, Barracuda, North Door, Buffalo Billiards, and others.

> The Red River Cultural District warned surveyed venues had approximately 30 days of operational savings on average.

> City Council passed the SAVES resolution on October 1, securing $5 million for clubs. The Live Music Venue Preservation Fund guidelines were approved December 3.

> The Austin Music Disaster Relief Fund distributed $1,000 grants to individual musicians through MusiCares.

> The June livestream "A Night for Austin" raised over $500,000.

> The Austin Music Commission designated half the Live Music Fund to BIPOC artists and organizations.

> Multiple nonprofits expanded services, including the Health Alliance for Austin Musicians and Banding Together ATX.

## Why it matters

Single best year-end tally of Austin music's 2020: 18 venue closures, SXSW's first cancellation in 34 years and roughly one-third staff layoffs, Margin Walker's closure, the $5M SAVES resolution (Oct 1), and the Music Commission's decision to direct half the Live Music Fund to BIPOC artists and organizations.
