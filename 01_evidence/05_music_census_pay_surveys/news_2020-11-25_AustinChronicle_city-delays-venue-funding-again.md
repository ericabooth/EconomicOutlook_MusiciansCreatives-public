# Faster Than Sound: City of Austin Delays Venue Funding – Again

- **Title:** Faster Than Sound: City of Austin Delays Venue Funding – Again (subtitle: Live music venues have asked for dedicated COVID-19 relief since March, but the city says applications won't open until next year.)
- **Outlet:** The Austin Chronicle
- **Author:** Rachel Rascoe
- **Publication date:** 2020-11-25 (issue dated 2020-11-27)
- **URL:** https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/2020-11-27/faster-than-sound-city-of-austin-delays-venue-funding-again/
- **Retrieval date:** 2026-08-01

## Key excerpts

SAVES Resolution structure and delay:

> Passed Oct. 1, the effort promised $5 million for music venues, alongside another $5 million for eligible "legacy businesses," including venues and restaurants.

> The update announced applications for venue aid won't open until mid-to-late January.

> Under current plans, clubs first apply for emergency grants up to $20,000. Then they have to undergo small business training before applying for additional grants up to $40,000 per month, totaling up to $140,000 over six months.

> "Venues cannot wait that long," declared Nakia Reynoso of Austin Texas Musicians during a rally last Thursday at City Hall. "[The city] moves the carrot time and time again. If they really care about the 'Live Music Capital of the World,' the way they market it in every brochure and commercial, then where is the money?"

Peer-city comparison, from Amplified Sound Coalition co-founder Jeannette Gregor:

> Nashville, Houston, Denver, Oklahoma City, and St. Paul made the list [of cities that had already distributed venue-specific preservation money].

> "Why aren't we leading the country in venue funding?" she asked.

Equity requirements:

> As recommended by the Austin Music Commission, entities must present a detailed equity plan in the application process. Alongside required training on systemic racism, program plans suggest residencies for "emerging artists of color," internships, and collaboration with BIPOC talent buyers. Council votes on specifics Dec. 3.

Black Live Music Fund:

> Fundraising is now open for the new Black Live Music Fund, launched by Chaka Mahone within the nonprofit Austin Community Foundation ... Black Fret kicked off with a contribution of $10,000.

> "When COVID hit, people scrambled to find funds for musicians going through financial struggle," he said. "I was here for all that conversation, but I didn't hear a conversation about how hard it is to be a Black musician in Austin. If it's hard for folks the industry is set up for, what about the folks it's not set up for?"

> The idea, to utilize half of the music industry-designated Live Music Fund to support Black musicians and organizations, passed unanimously. In October, he expanded use of half of the civic stockpile generally to BIPOC ... musicians and organizations in another unanimously-passed item.

> the BLMF now shifts to the private sector, with a goal of raising $50,000 in the next six months. Mahone hopes to begin distributing grants of $2,000-2,500 to music workers in the same time frame.

> Previously, Mahone raised over $60,000 for essential workers and artists of color through his organization Diversity Awareness and Wellness in Action (DAWA).

## Why it matters

The clearest documentation of implementation lag in COVID venue relief: SAVES passed Oct. 1, 2020 with $5M for venues, but applications had not opened by late November, with a mid-to-late January target. Gives the exact grant ladder ($20,000 emergency, then up to $40,000/month to a $140,000 six-month cap) and names five peer cities that moved faster. Also the best in-window source on Black Fret and the Black Live Music Fund.
