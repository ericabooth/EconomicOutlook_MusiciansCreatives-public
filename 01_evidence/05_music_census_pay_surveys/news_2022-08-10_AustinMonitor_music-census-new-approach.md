## Why it matters

Field-period and methodology documentation for the 2022 census, plus the key accountability finding: the 2015 census produced a 2016 City resolution with 25 goals, most of which **stalled or remained incomplete six years later**. Useful for any section on whether measurement has translated into policy.

## Key figures and excerpts

- Austin's **second** music census (first was 2015).
- Field period originally scheduled to close **August 15, 2022**, but "likely to be extended into September."
- No budget figure disclosed in the article.

> 2015 census finding restated: nearly **one-third of local working musicians earned less than $15,000 annually**.

> That data informed the city's **2016 creative ecosystem omnibus resolution** with "25 broad goals and many more subtasks" — most of which "stalled or remain incomplete six years later."

> "It provides a road map for music communities that are inherently siloed and fragmented." —Don Pitts, president of Sound Music Cities and former head of Austin's Music and Entertainment Division

**Comparison cities cited:** Washington, D.C.; Pittsburgh; Sacramento (policy consultation work). Huntsville, Alabama — a 2019 music audit there led to a **$50 million amphitheater**.

**Methodology change:** results to be presented through "infographics and other bite-sized formats" for easier community sharing. (Read alongside the Chronicle's finding that the earnings question was dropped — the simplification came at the cost of the income time series.)
