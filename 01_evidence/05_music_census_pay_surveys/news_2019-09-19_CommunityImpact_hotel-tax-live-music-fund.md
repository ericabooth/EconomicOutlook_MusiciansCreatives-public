# In nod to Austin's live music industry, City Council carves out more than $3 million in hotel tax funding

- **Title:** In nod to Austin's live music industry, City Council carves out more than $3 million in hotel tax funding
- **Outlet:** Community Impact
- **Author:** Christopher Neely
- **Publication date:** 2019-09-19 (updated 2019-09-20)
- **URL:** https://communityimpact.com/austin/central-austin/arts-entertainment/2019/09/19/in-nod-to-austins-live-music-industry-city-council-carves-out-more-than-3-million-in-hotel-tax-funding/
- **Retrieval date:** 2026-08-01

## Key excerpts

> Austin's City Council allocated 15% of the city's 2% hotel occupancy tax to establish a "live music fund." With estimated annual hotel tax revenue of $21 million from the additional 2% levy, this translates to approximately $3.15 million annually for live music support.

> The remaining occupancy tax revenue is divided between historic preservation (15%) and the Austin Convention Center (70%).

> State law restricts fund usage to tourism-related commercial music activities, prohibiting direct venue support.

> Council Member Jimmy Flannigan noted: "hotel tax revenue specifically cannot be used on music venues."

> Cat Whittington, Austin Tourism Commission chairman: "Austin is the Live Music Capital of the World; it's not the chamber music capital."

> Patrick Butcha, executive director of Austin Texas Musicians: "This is historic—let's make no mistake about it."

> The initiative builds on the city's 2016 Music and Creative Ecosystem Omnibus Resolution.

## Why it matters

Gives the arithmetic behind the Live Music Fund: 15% of the incremental 2% hotel occupancy tax, on an estimated $21 million base, equals roughly $3.15 million per year, against 70% routed to the Convention Center. Also records the state-law restriction to tourism-related commercial music activity that bars direct venue support, which is the constraint the 2020 SAVES fund had to work around.

## Cross-check note

This article says hotel tax revenue "cannot be used on music venues," while the Austin Monitor's same-week story describes the allocation as a first for direct funding to "musicians and for-profit venues." Reconcile against the adopted ordinance before citing either characterization.
