## 1. HAAM — Health Alliance for Austin Musicians (EIN 80-0147620)

Founded 2005. Serves musicians, DJs, and music teachers in Travis and surrounding counties.

### 1a. MEMBER INCOME — the headline ask

HAAM **does not publish an exact median or mean member income** in any report 2014–2025. What it publishes is a **share-below-threshold** statistic, and the threshold changes year to year. The closest thing to a published median is the "half of members make less than $X" framing that begins in 2022.

| 2015 | **76%** of members earn less than **$20,000** annually | — | 2015 AR, p. 4 |
| 2017 | "The average HAAM member makes **$12,060 to $18,090 per year**." **63%** of members report making just barely enough to get by | — | 2017-18 AR, p. 3 |

**Best available "median" claim, stated carefully:** HAAM's 2024 report says half its members make less than **$1,975/month (~$23,700/year)**; the 2025 report puts the same midpoint at **$24,000/year ($2,000/month)**. These are self-reported member-survey midpoints for HAAM's membership, **not** a median for all Austin musicians, and HAAM does not publish the underlying survey n or methodology in the reports.

**On the federal poverty line — important correction.** No HAAM report states a share of members below the federal poverty line. The only FPL reference found is an **eligibility ceiling**, not a poverty rate:
- 2025 AR: in-kind provider contributions "Extend access to essential healthcare for working musicians **below 400% Federal Poverty Line**." (2025 AR, in-kind providers section)
- Do **not** convert this into a poverty-rate claim.

**Comparison anchor for context (not a HAAM statistic):** The 2019 AR attributes the "83% earning less than $24,000" and "8,000+ working musicians in Austin" figures to **The Austin Music Census, 2015** (2019 AR, p. 3). The 2015 and 2017-18 reports repeat the same Census-derived economic-impact block ($2 billion economic activity, $38 million local tax revenue, 8,000+ working musicians, 18,148 music industry jobs, 300,000+ tourists). **Vintage caveat:** these Census figures were still being reprinted in HAAM's 2019 report despite being four years old at the time.

### 2c. 2024–2025 funding crisis

- SIMS **paused services for 90 days beginning in November** after "it was hitting a funding cliff." Reported by KVUE (https://www.kvue.com/article/news/local/sims-foundation-revamping-mental-health-recovery-resources-musicians-austin/269-0daa1622-06d9-4ee1-8d10-b21512a9cfe2). ⚠️ **The year of the pause is not stated in the material I could retrieve** — context suggests November 2024, but this needs confirmation before citing.
- SIMS marked its **30th anniversary in May 2025**; it launched the **Dick Chalmers Music Venue Program**, a fund for venue workers' counseling, psychiatric care, and recovery support, reported January 5, 2026 (https://www.kut.org/health/2026-01-05/sims-foundation-austin-tx-dick-chalmers-music-venue-program-mental-health-fund). **The fund's dollar size is not published in that article.**
- Travis County District Attorney's Office pledged **over $15,000** from its discretionary fund to SIMS; since 2018 SIMS has provided free naloxone training to the music community (Austin Chronicle).

## 7. Cross-cutting cautions for the white paper

1. **HAAM's income statistics describe HAAM's members, not Austin musicians generally.** HAAM members are self-selected into a program targeting low-income, uninsured working musicians (eligibility below 400% FPL). Using them as a proxy for all Austin musicians will bias earnings downward. The 2022 report states HAAM served "over 3,000 out of the more than 8,000 musicians in the community."
2. **The "8,000+ working musicians in Austin," "$2 billion economic activity," and "$38 million local tax revenue" figures all trace to the 2015 Austin Music Census** and were still being reprinted by HAAM in 2019. Anything relying on them is using 2015-vintage data.
3. **Thresholds move across years.** "Share earning less than $X" is not comparable over time when X changes ($17,000 in 2014 → $20,000 in 2015 → $24,000 in 2019 → $30,000 in 2023 → $31,000 in 2025). Neither is it inflation-adjusted. The only near-comparable series is the median proxy: **<$1,750/mo (2022) → <$1,975/mo (2024) → <$2,000/mo (2025)**, which is roughly flat in nominal terms and therefore **declining in real terms** across a period of high Austin inflation. That is a defensible and striking finding — but confirm the underlying survey is consistent year to year before asserting it, which HAAM does not document.
4. **"Value of healthcare services" is charge-based, not cost-based.** The 2019 report explicitly says its Ascension Seton figures "represent charges for HAAM members at Ascension Seton facilities." Cumulative "$215 million in services" is a charge-master aggregate and overstates economic cost.
5. **Distinguish raised / distributed / awarded / obligated** throughout, especially for Black Fret and the Live Music Fund.
