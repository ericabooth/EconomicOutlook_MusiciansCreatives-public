# Survey predicts 90 percent of Austin live music venues to close by Halloween

- **Title:** Survey predicts 90 percent of Austin live music venues to close by Halloween
- **Outlet:** Austin Monitor
- **Author:** Chad Swiatecki
- **Publication date:** 2020-07-08
- **URL:** https://austinmonitor.com/stories/2020/07/survey-predicts-90-percent-of-austin-live-music-venues-to-close-by-halloween/
- **Retrieval date:** 2026-08-01

## Key excerpts

> A University of Houston survey of over 1,000 Austin business owners found that 90 percent of live music venues face permanent closure by fall 2020. The study [was] commissioned by the Austin Chamber of Commerce.

> 62 percent of music venues could survive four months or less without reopening.

> Only 19 percent paid full June rent; 67 percent paid less than half.

> 83 percent eliminated full-time positions.

> 79 percent suspended payments to vendors and landlords.

> Rebecca Reynolds, head of the Music Venue Alliance, stated that venues are "skyrocketing to the top of every category in terms of hardest hit, eminent closure, layoffs" and emphasized the need for targeted relief, noting venues carry approximately $40,000 in monthly overhead costs.

> The city approved $16.5 million in CARES Act grants (up to $40,000 per business) and a $1 million Creative Space Assistance Program. However, venue operators argued this assistance fell short of actual needs.

## Why it matters

The most numerically complete account of the 2020 Austin venue collapse risk: survey of 1,000+ business owners by the University of Houston Hobby School for the Austin Chamber, with a full distress profile (62% four-month survival horizon, 19% full June rent, 83% full-time layoffs, 79% suspended vendor payments) and the mismatch between $40,000 CARES grants and $40,000 monthly overhead.

## Cross-check note

The KUT/Texas Standard write-up of the same survey (2020-07-10) frames the headline differently: it reports 60%+ of venues at permanent closure risk on a four-month horizon, with the 90% figure attributed to a single respondent's estimate. Treat the "90 percent" headline with care and prefer the itemized percentages.
