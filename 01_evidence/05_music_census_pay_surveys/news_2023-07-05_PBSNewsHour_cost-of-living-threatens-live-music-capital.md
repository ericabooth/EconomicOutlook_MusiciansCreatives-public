## Why it matters

The national-outlet framing of the 2022 census findings, and the source of two macro numbers a policy paper will want: **250+ live music venues** and **$1 billion+ annual economic contribution**. Both should be traced to their underlying City/consultant sources before use.

## Key figures

- Austin has been the **fastest-growing large metro region for over a decade**.
- Nearly **40% of music industry workers** struggle to afford housing.
- **12% drop in musicians** living in Central Austin between **2014 and 2022**.
- Over **one-third** of musicians considering leaving the region within three years.
- Austin hosts **more than 250 live music venues**.
- The music industry generates **over $1 billion annually** for Austin.

## Key excerpts

> "The very artists that created the experience … are getting priced out." —Erica Shamaly, Austin Music & Entertainment Division

> "I'd have to win the lottery" [to move back to Austin]. —Bob Mann, musician, after relocating to Elgin

> "This city's really just turned into this moneymaking thing" [focused on commercial real estate development]. —Scott Strickland, Austin Music Commission

## Context noted in the segment

Tech relocations (Tesla, Apple, Google) drove housing costs up and displaced musicians. The 2022 Greater Austin Music Census is the documentary basis for the exodus claim.

## Caution

PBS uses **12%** for the central-Austin decline, matching the Austin Chronicle and **not** matching KUT's 10%. Two of three sources say 12%. Still verify against the census report. The "$1 billion annually" and "250+ venues" figures are stated without attribution in the segment — do not cite these to PBS as original evidence.
