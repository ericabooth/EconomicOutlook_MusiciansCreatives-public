# Council approves $3M annually from hotel tax for musicians, venues

- **Title:** Council approves $3M annually from hotel tax for musicians, venues
- **Outlet:** Austin Monitor
- **Author:** Chad Swiatecki
- **Publication date:** 2019-09-23
- **URL:** https://www.austinmonitor.com/stories/2019/09/council-approves-3m-annually-from-hotel-tax-for-musicians-venues/
- **Retrieval date:** 2026-08-01

## Key excerpts

> Austin's City Council unanimously approved directing approximately $3 million annually from Hotel Occupancy Tax revenue to support the local music industry — a historic first for direct city funding to musicians and for-profit venues.

> Previously, state law capped cultural arts funding at 15 percent of total hotel tax revenue and restricted these funds to nonprofit organizations. The new allocation became possible after Council raised the hotel tax to the maximum 17 percent allowed by state law, with the additional revenue designated for the music sector.

> Catlin Whitington, Tourism Commission chair: "This is a long overdue way to recognize that by allocating a very small slice of a bigger pie for that industry sector."

> Cody Cowan, Red River Merchants Association executive director, noted the funding could "provide enough revenue to clubs to cover one or more months' rent."

> The new money was expected to become available near year-end, with the Music Commission tasked to help determine spending allocation through a community input process.

## Why it matters

Documents the creation of the Live Music Fund, the main standing municipal money stream for Austin musicians, and the specific state-law constraint it had to route around (15% cultural arts cap, nonprofit-only restriction, 17% maximum hotel tax rate). Directly relevant to a Texas policy-instrument discussion because the binding constraint is state statute, not city will.
