# Live Music Fund collections show hotel taxes dipped slightly last fiscal year

## Why it matters

Gives the Live Music Fund's actual revenue line for two consecutive fiscal years, and establishes that the fund is **state-mandated allocation of Hotel Occupancy Tax collections** — so musician support in Austin is structurally tied to tourism revenue, not general fund appropriation. That dependency is a policy-relevant fragility.

## Key figures

- **Fiscal 2024 Live Music Fund total: $4.445 million**
- **Fiscal 2023 Live Music Fund total: $4.472 million**
- **Change: a decrease of just under 1 percent**

## Key excerpts

> The Live Music Fund "receives a state-mandated allocation of the city's hotel tax collections."

> The drop surprised some Music Commission members, as "the local tourism industry has seen as strong rebound since the Covid-19 pandemic."

> Staff and commissioners in tourism roles attributed the flat numbers to other data "showing hotel revenues as being essentially flat, perhaps as a response to ongoing inflation concerns."

## Note

Compare to the $3 million *accumulated and undistributed* balance reported by KUT in March 2022 — the fund now runs roughly $4.4M/year in collections. Useful for a "money exists, distribution is the bottleneck" argument.
