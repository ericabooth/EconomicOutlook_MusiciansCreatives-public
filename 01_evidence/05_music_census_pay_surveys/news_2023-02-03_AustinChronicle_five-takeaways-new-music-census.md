## Why it matters

The most numerically complete news account of the 2022 census, and the clearest statement of the **data gap that breaks the pay time series**: the 2022 instrument dropped the annual-earnings question, so the famous 2014 "one-third earn $15,000 or less" figure has no 2022 successor. Also carries HOME's own grantee income data, which is a separate and citable source.

## Key excerpts (verbatim)

> "Just over 2,200 musicians and music industry participants completed the online survey … reflecting a 43% decrease from the almost 4,000 replies in 2014."

> "Find some of the most noteworthy conclusions to draw from an early run through the 87-page, chart-heavy report below."

> **Affordability Bites:** "Thirty-eight percent of respondents said they are struggling to afford housing, with 36% saying they plan to leave the Austin area in the next three years."

> **Outer City Limits:** "The overall musical exodus from the area means more musicians living farther away from Downtown Austin, with a rough average of 11 miles' distance from respondents' homes to what used to be the center of music and cultural activity. The city's 10 central ZIP codes saw residency of survey respondents decline by 12%, while out-of-county areas (San Marcos, Pflugerville, Manor) saw an increase of 7.7%. In short, Austin musicians are becoming Central Texas musicians…"

> **No Data on Dollars:** "While the affordability info paints a clear picture of the dire financial straits faced by Austin musicians, the lack of specific questions about annual earnings from music does somewhat defang the findings. The 2014 census had an easy and loud reference point that almost one-third of Austin musicians earned $15,000 or less per year. The lack of a follow-up inquiry on that matter means there's no opportunity to do an apples-to-apples comparison of earning power…"

> **Aging Trend:** "The 25-to-39 cohort dropped from half of all respondents to 38%, with those age 40-54 and age 65 and over showing single-digit increases. That trend suggests the area's music industry is likely to shrink in the coming decades unless there's a substantial change of course."

> **Eyes on Inclusion:** "One of the most noteworthy new features of the census was the polling on 14 diversity, equity, and inclusion matters. Those responses painted a mixed picture, where concerns over ageism, sexism, and elitism are the clearest concerns. Analysts within the report noted that minority groups tended to view the treatment of their community more harshly…"

> **HOME grantee data:** "Hanna Cofer, president of HOME, said 22% of its grant recipients now live outside of the city limits, with 50% reporting they earn less than $1,500 per month including Social Security." (HOME = Housing Opportunities for Musicians and Entertainers, which serves musicians 55 and older.)

> **James Russell**, executive director of Trail of Lights: "As the exodus from Austin continues with folks seeking a cheaper cost of living and quality of life, the surrounding communities have a huge opportunity to learn from the data that has been compiled and figure out how to embrace the folks that are coming to their communities from Austin. Be it sound ordinances or land development codes, they can learn from things that we've been chasing our tail on by having policies necessary to preserve the culture early on and not have to play catch up."

> **Mayor Kirk Watson**, prepared statement: "Austin music has long been essential to the character of our city, but we know that the people behind that music are struggling with the same affordability emergency that's affecting families throughout our community. The findings of the music census underscore the urgency of addressing our affordability issues to ensure interesting, innovative, and creative folks can continue to make magic here."

## Provenance notes

- The 2022 census was conducted by **Sound Music Cities**, founded by **Don Pitts**, the former head of Austin's city music office who commissioned the 2014/2015 census while at City Hall. Same principal on both rounds — worth flagging as a continuity strength and an independence caveat.
- **Discrepancy to resolve:** central-ZIP decline is **12%** here vs **10%** in KUT's 2023-02-08 write-up of the same census. Reconcile against the 87-page report.
- Sample decline stated two ways across sources: "43% decrease from almost 4,000" (Chronicle) vs "~1,700 fewer than 2015" (KUT). Both are consistent with ~2,200 in 2022 and ~3,900–4,000 prior.
