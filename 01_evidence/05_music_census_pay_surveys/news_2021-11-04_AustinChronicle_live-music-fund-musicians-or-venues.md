# Faster Than Sound: Should the Live Music Fund Go to Musicians or Venues?

- **Title:** Faster Than Sound: Should the Live Music Fund Go to Musicians or Venues? (subtitle: While eligibility is worked out, Music Commission nails down "PIE" equity plan)
- **Outlet:** The Austin Chronicle
- **Author:** Rachel Rascoe
- **Publication date:** 2021-11-04
- **URL:** https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/faster-than-sound-should-the-live-music-fund-go-to-musicians-or-venues-12124639/
- **Retrieval date:** 2026-08-01

## Key excerpts

> The Austin Music Commission unanimously approved a framework centered on Preservation, Innovation, and Elevation & Collaboration (PIE) to guide how the city's Live Music Fund allocates resources.

> The $3.2 million fund, created in 2019 from a 2% hotel occupancy tax increase, remains in development regarding who should receive grants.

Two competing proposals:

> Economic Development Department plan: Direct grants of $5,000-$10,000 to independent musicians and promoters (not tied to venues), requiring minimum $150 hourly musician payments and $15 minimum for all contractors.

> Music Makes Austin proposal: Include venues as eligible recipients, capping band payments at $3,000 maximum per performance.

Artist testimony:

> Tameca Jones stated: "This city has broken me to the point where I have to leave it to thrive," citing her relocation to Los Angeles.

> Megz Kelli of Magna Carda noted venues had "received bailouts" through COVID relief while musicians faced wage disparities.

> Mayor Steve Adler questioned whether library performances represented genuine industry development, comparing musician growth to circus animal conditioning—remarks city staff later clarified were incompatible with state funding restrictions on venue purchases.

## Why it matters

Closes the 2014-2021 arc: two years after the Live Music Fund was created, the money still had not moved, and the open question was whether it should go to musicians or venues. Supplies concrete proposed pay floors ($150/hour for musicians, $15/hour for contractors, $3,000 cap per band performance) and the fund's revised size ($3.2 million). The Tameca Jones quote is the strongest single artist statement of the exodus theme in the window.
