# Austin Music Census reveals fault lines in industry

- **Title:** Austin Music Census reveals fault lines in industry
- **Outlet:** Austin Monitor
- **Author:** Elizabeth Pagano
- **Publication date:** 2015-06-01
- **URL:** https://austinmonitor.com/stories/2015/06/austin-music-census-reveals-fault-lines-in-industry/
- **Retrieval date:** 2026-08-01

## Key excerpts

> Over 20% of working musicians live below the federal poverty line, with approximately half qualifying for federal housing assistance.

> The report warns of a potential "tipping point" where the community could experience "erosion and disintegration of critical parts."

> Venues report that "either they are not charging cover at all anymore, or they are charging the same or less than they were 10 years ago."

> The census identifies the city's permitting system as "confusing and arcane," requiring contact with "a minimum of seven different city departments." Nearly two-thirds of venue operators report inconsistent sound ordinance enforcement.

> Rising rents and expensive development in central Austin are displacing musicians.

Recommended solutions reported in the article:

> Establish dedicated affordable housing for musicians through public-private partnerships; develop a long-term industry development strategy with intentional policy support; create economic clusters similar to the Innovation Zone or Film Society studios; formalize entertainment districts like Red River Cultural District.

## Why it matters

Local policy-desk account of the census that connects the income findings to specific municipal levers (permitting, sound ordinance enforcement, Red River Cultural District, musician housing). Notes the census was conducted by Titan Music Group under city commission.
