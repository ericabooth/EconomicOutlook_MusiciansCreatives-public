**Two corrections to the brief's premises:**
1. **HAAM publishes no exact median or mean member income.** It publishes share-below-a-threshold statistics, and the threshold moves every year. The best available proxy is the 2024 report's "half of HAAM musicians make less than $1,975 a month" (~$23,700/yr) and the 2025 report's "½ make less than $24,000 a year."
2. **HAAM publishes no share of members below the federal poverty line.** The only FPL reference is an *eligibility ceiling* (members below 400% FPL). Do not convert an eligibility rule into a poverty rate.

**Population caveat that applies to every HAAM figure:** HAAM's membership is a self-selected, income-screened program population (below 400% FPL), not a sample of Austin musicians. HAAM income statistics describe HAAM members and cannot be generalised.

**Vintage caveat:** HAAM reprinted 2015 Austin Music Census figures ("8,000+ working musicians," "$2 billion economic activity," "83% earning less than $24,000") in its reports as late as 2019. Those are census numbers, not HAAM measurements, and were four years stale at reprinting.

## 5. Data-access and methodology problems worth naming in the paper

1. **The 2014 microdata was never released.** October 2015: Texas State professor Cindy Royal filed a Texas Public Information Act request for de-identified quantitative data. Titan Music Group (a $45,000 city contract) argued the raw data was outside the Act and that release would "turn the entire process into an Internet parlor game of who is who." The City Attorney sided with disclosure. Source: GovTech, Oct 2015, https://www.govtech.com/dc/articles/austin-music-census-data-ignites-squabble-over-open-records-requests.html. Treat the published PDF as the only public version.
2. **The 2022 census deliberately removed the income questions.** Summary Report p.2: the 2022 survey "removed questions from the 2014 survey that aimed to quantify the income of music people in a way that turned off many respondents." **There is therefore no 2022 counterpart to any 2014 income or poverty statistic.**
3. **Both censuses are self-selected online convenience samples.** Neither supports population inference. The 2022 report frames its own 2014 comparisons as "directional shifts."
4. **Response fell 43%** between the two censuses (3,968 → 2,260), and 2022 outreach ran through established community partners — which plausibly contributes to the observed shift toward longer-tenured respondents.
5. **Neither HAAM nor the UT project publishes sample sizes, response rates, or field dates** for their member/interview statistics.
