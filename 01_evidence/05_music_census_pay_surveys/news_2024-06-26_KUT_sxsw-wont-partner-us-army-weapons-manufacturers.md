## Why it matters

Documents the **2024 SXSW boycott** and its resolution. Two facts matter for a pay-focused paper: **nearly 120 artists withdrew**, demonstrating that low-paid performers retain collective leverage; and the closing paragraph confirms SXSW "quietly raised musician wages in summer 2023" and **waived application fees for local musicians through HAAM** — the pay dispute and the sponsorship dispute resolved on the same track.

## Key figures

- **Nearly 120 artists** canceled their sets at SXSW Music 2024 in protest.
- Sponsors at issue: the **U.S. Army** and defense contractor **RTX** (formerly Raytheon).

## Key excerpts

> SXSW statement: "After careful consideration, we are revising our sponsorship model. As a result, the US Army, and companies who engage in weapons manufacturing, will not be sponsors of SXSW 2025."

> "We thought this victory was going to be a harder road. We thought it was going to take longer." —Zainab Haider, Austin for Palestine Coalition

> "We, the musicians, are soft capital, and we don't want to be used for one of the most disgusting things about America." —Thor Harris, percussionist

> Context noted by KUT: the festival "also quietly raised musician wages in summer 2023 and waived application fees for local musicians through the Health Alliance for Austin Musicians."

## Cross-reference

The summer-2023 wage raise referenced here is the $250→$350 (band) and $100→$150 (solo/duo) increase documented on the UMAW campaign page and reported by Billboard in June 2023.
