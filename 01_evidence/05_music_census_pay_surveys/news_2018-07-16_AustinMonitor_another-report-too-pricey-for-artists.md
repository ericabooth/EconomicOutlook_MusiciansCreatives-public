# Another report, same story: Austin's too pricey for artists

- **Title:** Another report, same story: Austin's too pricey for artists
- **Outlet:** Austin Monitor
- **Author:** Chad Swiatecki
- **Publication date:** 2018-07-16
- **URL:** https://www.austinmonitor.com/stories/2018/07/another-report-same-story-austins-too-pricey-for-artists/
- **Retrieval date:** 2026-08-01

## Key excerpts

Findings from the Economic Development Department's 2017 Creative Space Survey (over 500 artists):

> 51% of organizations reported insufficient space.

> 42% have considered relocating elsewhere.

> 23% operate on month-to-month leases.

> 12% lack secure space or face imminent displacement.

> Rising real estate costs, particularly in East Austin, continue displacing creative professionals. Suburban areas like Lockhart, Elgin, and Smithville are attracting Austin-based artists seeking affordable alternatives.

> Mayor Steve Adler emphasized that "the city is moving toward an ever-greater reliance on data to assess the effectiveness of where to spend funds."

> Jeanne Claire van Ryzin noted: "A lot of artists have given up on the prospect of anything from the city because there is so much issuing of studies or getting together a list of recommendations – and then nothing happening."

> A potential $12 million bond allocation for creative spaces was proposed for the fall election ballot.

## Why it matters

Documents the implementation gap between the 2015 census and 2019 Live Music Fund: three years of recommendations with little delivered, plus a second independent survey (2017 Creative Space Survey, 500+ artists) confirming displacement. The van Ryzin quote is the sharpest available statement of study fatigue, useful for a section on why recommendations stalled.
