## Why it matters

The 2025 sequel to the 2022 stagnant-pay series, and the clearest evidence that **live performance is no longer the economic core** of a sustainable Austin music career. Every one of the four profiled artists earns primarily from something other than gigs: teaching, session work, production, sync licensing, or nonprofit-backed presenting. KUT explicitly frames these strategies as responses to pay "unchanged for 40 years."

## Concrete numbers

- **Chinasa Broxton (Tribe Mafia): first sync placement paid $5,000**, plus subsequent royalties.
- **Carrie Rodriguez / Laboratorio: approximately $80,000 paid out to artists last year.**
- **Cactus Cafe capacity: 150 people** — cited as the structural revenue ceiling that pushed Laboratorio toward nonprofit status.

## Key excerpts

> "I kind of saw the writing on the wall with the gig economy pretty early and knew that it wasn't good for me and my brain space." —Sara L. Houser

> "I wanted to try to put it out there that I could be this one-stop shop" —Sara L. Houser, on founding Siren House to bundle live performance, teaching, session work, string arrangements, and studio production

> "When we got our first placement, we were like, what the? $5,000 for this?" —Chinasa Broxton, Tribe Mafia

> "Whatever we can get into artistically, we'll try to see a dollar sign in it" —Chinasa Broxton

> "None of that stuff matters. We just wanna make good music and as long as we're making good music, it's making sense, we'll do whatever we gotta do." —Carlos "Dashawn Daniels" Moore, Tribe Mafia

> "That's the hardest thing for musicians — to get paid what they really deserve to be paid." —Carrie Rodriguez

> "Suddenly I'm like — oh, I need to be home and I need to figure out how to get to know people here." —Carrie Rodriguez, on leaving touring

> "I think coming together with other musicians and artists can bring a lot of great things." —Carrie Rodriguez

## Structural detail worth noting

- Houser moved to Austin in 2009, worked at Guero's Taco Bar, performed eight years before adding teaching; credits include Golden Dawn Arkestra and Spoon's *Hot Thoughts*.
- Tribe Mafia deliberately **de-prioritized large live shows** in favor of writing for TV and film placement.
- Laboratorio's nonprofit conversion was enabled by **Austin Community Foundation** funding plus a **State Theater** partnership providing a guarantee and marketing.

## Caution

This is a "how they succeeded" feature, not a representative sample. Four self-selected successful cases cannot support any population-level income claim. Use it for mechanism, not magnitude.
