## Why it matters

Supplies the single most quotable affordability arithmetic in the KUT series: at the prevailing ~$100/gig rate, an Austin musician needs **45 gigs a month** to cover median rent alone. Also documents sync licensing as the substitute income stream — relevant to any argument that live performance is no longer the economic core of a music career.

## Key figures

- Average per-gig income: **approximately $100 per person**; range **$50–$100 per person** up to several hundred dollars.
- Documented low end: **$46** for a gig (musician Mike Booher).
- **Austin median rent: $1,500/month.**
- **45 gigs per month** required to cover that rent at $100/person.
- Tee Double (Terrany Johnson): **30 self-produced albums**, plus "five hard drives of just terabytes of songs."

## Key excerpts

> "Sometimes you walk out of there with freaking 46 bucks. It's heartbreaking." —Mike Booher

> "You have to be quick. So if I get my brief at 8 o'clock, I'm probably hitting them back with a song by 8:30, saying, 'Here, try this.'" —Tee Double, on turnaround time for music-supervisor briefs

> "They're very detailed. They'll say there's two kids walking down an alley…" —Tee Double, on how sync briefs are written

> "Sync has really saved my life, so to speak, my livelihood…" —Tee Double

> Tee Double reports "making more money doing sync than I would have been out rapping or performing."

## Caution

The "45 gigs a month" figure is KUT's arithmetic ($1,500 ÷ ~$33/gig would give 45; at $100/gig it would be 15). **Verify this calculation against the source audio before citing** — as extracted, $1,500 at $100/gig implies 15 gigs, not 45. The discrepancy may reflect a different rent figure, a post-tax assumption, or a per-band-split assumption in the original. Do not use the 45 figure until reconciled.
