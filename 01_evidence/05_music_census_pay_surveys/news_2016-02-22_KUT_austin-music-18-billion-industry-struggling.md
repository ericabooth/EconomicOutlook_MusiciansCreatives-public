# Report: Austin Music Is Now A $1.8-Billion Industry, But The Local Scene Is Struggling

- **Title:** Report: Austin Music Is Now A $1.8-Billion Industry, But The Local Scene Is Struggling
- **Outlet:** KUT 90.5
- **Author:** Emilie Mutert
- **Publication date:** 2016-02-22 (2:00 PM CST)
- **URL:** https://www.kut.org/post/report-austin-music-now-18-billion-industry-local-scene-struggling
- **Retrieval date:** 2026-08-01

## Key excerpts

> The study by TXP, Inc., commissioned by Austin Music People, reveals a paradox: while Austin's total music industry economic impact grew from $1.6 billion (2010) to $1.8 billion (2014), this growth masks decline in the local scene.

> Music tourism jobs increased by 3,780 (37% rise) from 2010-2014.

> Non-tourism music industry jobs dropped by 1,205 (15% decline) in the same period.

> "Music tourism is very strong. What we're not seeing is the same kind of investment in the local music industry, and that's troubling." — Houlihan (Austin Music People representative)

> The report identifies municipal permitting complexity as a barrier, with venues potentially needing "18 different permits from six different offices."

> Mayor Steve Adler's office indicated plans for "far-reaching and innovative effort" to stabilize the local music industry.

## Why it matters

The single cleanest statement of the "booming industry, broke musicians" paradox with a job-level decomposition: tourism jobs up 3,780 (+37%) while non-tourism music jobs fell 1,205 (-15%) over 2010-2014, even as headline impact rose from $1.6B to $1.8B. Documents the TXP 2016 update and Austin Music People's advocacy role.
