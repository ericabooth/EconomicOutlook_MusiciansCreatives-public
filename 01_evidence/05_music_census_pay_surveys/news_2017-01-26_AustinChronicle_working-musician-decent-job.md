# Can a Working Musician Find a Decent Job in This Town?

- **Title:** Can a Working Musician Find a Decent Job in This Town? (subtitle: Musicians looking to subsidize their poverty-level passion already have one strike against them)
- **Outlet:** The Austin Chronicle
- **Author:** Leslie Sisson
- **Publication date:** 2017-01-26 (print issue 2017-01-27)
- **URL:** https://www.austinchronicle.com/music/2017-01-27/can-a-working-musician-find-a-decent-job-in-this-town/
- **Retrieval date:** 2026-08-01

## Key excerpts

Statistics the article draws from the 2015 Austin Music Census:

> 56% of musicians worked full- or part-time jobs outside music.

> 20% earn below the $11,670 federal poverty level.

> ~75% earn below Austin MSA mean annual wage.

> 62%+ earn below Austin MSA median annual wage.

> Nearly 40% of full-time musicians earn $15,000 or less annually.

> Nearly two-thirds earn less than $25,000 yearly.

Quotes:

> Britt Daniel (Spoon): "The whole thing is a struggle. I've been very fortunate. It's a combination of luck and working hard, and occasionally getting inspired."

> Sabrina Ellis (Sweet Spirit): "A musician has to sleep less, work faster. Unless the industry miraculously changes and money abounds for musicians, an artist has to be a lot harder than anybody else."

> John Wesley Coleman: "You almost feel like an ex-con being a musician because people are afraid to hire you."

> Jason McNeely (Hotel Vegas co-owner): "The solution is simple. People and businesses benefiting from and exploiting the music culture in Austin need to consider giving back."

## Why it matters

Best single source for the day-job dimension of Austin musician economics, and it restates the census income distribution in a form usable for a paper: 20% under the $11,670 federal poverty threshold, ~75% under MSA mean wage, 62%+ under MSA median wage, ~40% of full-time musicians at $15,000 or less, two-thirds under $25,000. The explicit $11,670 poverty threshold is useful for dating the census income question to the 2014 tax year.
