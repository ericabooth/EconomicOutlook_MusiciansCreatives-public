# Findings — Austin Cost of Living / Housing (09_cost_of_living)

Retrieval date 2026-08-01. See `_sources.md` for full provenance and caveats on every number below; every bullet here names the specific file it comes from.

## Zillow ZORI (rent index) and ZHVI (home values), Austin metro vs. peers

- **Austin home values have fallen further from peak than any comparison metro.** Zillow ZHVI (all-homes, seasonally adjusted) for the Austin-Round Rock metro peaked at $581,636 in June 2022 and had fallen to $426,944 by June 2026 — a **-26.6% decline from peak**, roughly 3x the drop seen in Dallas (-8.5%), San Antonio (-9.7%), Houston (-4.4%), or Nashville (-2.3%) over the same window. Source: `zillow_zhvi_allhomes_seasadj_5metro_long.csv`, from `Metro_zhvi_uc_sfrcondo_tier_0.33_0.67_sm_sa_month.csv` (files.zillowstatic.com, retrieved 2026-08-01).
- **Austin rents (ZORI) also corrected the most of the five metros, though more modestly than home values.** Austin ZORI (smoothed, seasonally adjusted) peaked at $1,854/month in July 2022 and sat at $1,653/month in June 2026, a **-10.8% decline from peak** — versus roughly flat-to-down-1% for Dallas, Houston, and Nashville, and -4.6% for San Antonio. Source: `zillow_zori_seasadj_5metro_long.csv` (retrieved 2026-08-01).
- **Austin rent is still up sharply versus the pre-pandemic/pre-boom baseline.** Even after the post-2022 correction, Austin ZORI of $1,653 (June 2026) is **+34.7% above its January 2015 level** ($1,228) — i.e., the 2022-2026 pullback has not undone the 2015-2022 run-up. Source: `zillow_zori_seasadj_5metro_long.csv`.
- Austin ZHVI over the same 2015-2026 window is up even more in dollar terms: from ~$259,900 (Jan 2015) to $426,944 (June 2026), **+64% cumulative**, despite the -26.6% pullback since the 2022 peak. Source: `zillow_zhvi_allhomes_seasadj_5metro_long.csv`.

## HUD Fair Market Rents (FY1983-FY2026), Travis County / Austin-Round Rock MSA

- **HUD's official 1-bedroom Fair Market Rent for Austin more than doubled between FY2015 and FY2026: $834 to $1,562, up 87%** (2-bedroom: $1,050 to $1,852, up 76%; studio/efficiency up 116%, from $681 to $1,474). This is a regulatory/administrative benchmark (used to set Section 8 voucher payment standards), not a market-transaction price, but it is HUD's own read on typical gross rent for recent movers and it corroborates the market-based Zillow series above. Source: `hud_fmr_1983_2026_5area_long.csv`, from HUD USER `FMR_All_1983_2026.csv` (retrieved 2026-08-01).
- **The FMR series confirms a post-2024 Austin rent cooling, consistent with the Zillow ZORI pullback.** Austin FMRs peaked in the FY2024-FY2025 vintages and then declined into FY2026: 1BR fell from $1,650 (FY2025) to $1,562 (FY2026), a **-5.3% year-over-year decline**; 2BR fell from $1,949 to $1,852 (-5.0%). All five bedroom sizes (studio through 4BR) show FY2024-as-recent-peak, then a FY2026 pullback of roughly -3% to -5%. Source: `hud_fmr_1983_2026_5area_long.csv`.

## ACS 5-year estimates: median gross rent and median home value, Travis County / Austin MSA vs. peers

- **By the Census Bureau's own household-survey measure (not a market index), Austin-area median gross rent rose from $1,398/month (2017-2021 5-yr estimate) to $1,726/month (2020-2024 5-yr estimate) at the MSA level, a +23.5% increase**, while median home value rose from $326,400 to $465,000, **+42.5%**. Travis County alone (city-adjacent, not full metro) shows a similar pattern: rent +22.6% ($1,422 to $1,744), home value +42.1% ($368,000 to $523,000). Source: `acs_median_rent_homevalue_5geo_long.csv`, built from Census Bureau ACS table-based Summary File tables B25064 and B25077 (retrieved 2026-08-01).
- **On this slower-moving ACS measure, Austin's rent and home-value growth over 2017-2021 to 2020-2024 is NOT dramatically out of line with peer metros** — Dallas-Fort Worth grew rent even faster (+27.7%) and Nashville's home-value growth (+42.8%) essentially matched Austin's (+42.5%). This is a meaningfully different picture than the Zillow monthly-index finding above (where Austin's post-2022 correction was 3x sharper than peers), because ACS 5-year estimates are rolling averages that blend the pre-boom, boom, and early-correction years together and cannot show a sharp peak/trough the way a monthly index can. Both readings are correct for what they measure; a policy narrative should specify which time resolution supports which claim. Source: `acs_median_rent_homevalue_5geo_long.csv`.

## BEA Regional Price Parities (RPP) and Implicit Regional Price Deflators, Austin vs peers

- **Austin looks average (even slightly cheap) on overall cost of living, but distinctly expensive on housing specifically — a genuinely important distinction for a musician-economics paper.** BEA's 2024 all-items Regional Price Parity for the Austin MSA is 98.1 (US average = 100.0, so Austin is about 1.9% below the national average across all goods and services combined), while Austin's housing-services RPP is 120.4 (20.4% above the national average). No other comparison metro shows as large a gap between its "everything" price level and its "housing only" price level: Dallas-Fort Worth is 103.1 all-items vs. 117.9 housing (a 14.8-point gap), Nashville is 96.3 vs. 104.6 (8.3-point gap), Houston is 98.6 vs. 104.5 (5.9-point gap), San Antonio is 94.7 vs. 94.6 (basically no gap). Source: `bea_rpp_marpp_5metro_long.csv`, from BEA `MARPP.zip` (retrieved 2026-08-01).
- **BEA's independent, survey-based measure corroborates the Zillow/HUD finding of an Austin housing-cost peak in 2022-2023 followed by a pullback**: Austin's housing RPP rose from 108.1 (2010) to a peak of 126.4 (2023), then fell to 120.4 (2024) — a third, methodologically distinct data source (BEA's regional accounts, built partly from ACS housing-cost microdata) telling the same directional story as the market-rate Zillow index and the administrative HUD FMR series. Source: `bea_rpp_marpp_5metro_long.csv`.
- **Austin's housing-cost premium over the national average has been remarkably persistent since 2010, while Nashville's is a more recent phenomenon.** Austin's housing RPP was already 108.1 in 2010 (8% above the US average) and has stayed above 108 in every year since; Nashville's housing RPP was 91.5 in 2010 (8.5% below the US average) and only crossed 100 around 2016-2017, reaching 104.6 by 2024. In other words, Nashville has been catching up toward Austin's housing-cost tier over 15 years, but Austin still sits meaningfully higher (120.4 vs. 104.6 in 2024). Source: `bea_rpp_marpp_5metro_long.csv`.
- Bonus/context: BEA's Implicit Regional Price Deflator (`bea_irpd_mairpd_5metro_long.csv`) is available if the white paper wants to convert nominal musician-income series into real, region-and-inflation-adjusted dollars for Austin specifically across 2008-2024 — not analyzed further here since it is a wage-side tool, not a cost-of-living finding per se.

## MIT Living Wage Calculator, Travis County (optional item, current as of build date 2026-02-15)

- **A single working adult in Travis County with no dependents needs $23.69/hour ($49,279/year before taxes) just to cover a no-frills basic-needs budget**, per the MIT Living Wage Calculator (retrieved 2026-08-01). That is more than 3x the $7.25 federal minimum wage that applies statewide in Texas, and more than 3x the $7.67 poverty-wage benchmark. This is a useful, citable anchor for framing what "getting by" costs in Austin independent of any specific musician-income figure. Source: `mit_living_wage_travis_county_20260801.md`.
- MIT's implied housing cost for that budget ($18,213/year, ~$1,518/month) sits close to, though modestly below, this folder's other Austin housing-cost reads for the same period (HUD FY2026 1BR FMR $1,562/month; Zillow Austin ZORI June 2026 $1,653/month) — three independently constructed sources landing within about $150/month of each other is a reasonable degree of convergence and worth noting as a robustness point in the white paper. Source: `mit_living_wage_travis_county_20260801.md` cross-referenced with `hud_fmr_1983_2026_5area_long.csv` and `zillow_zori_seasadj_5metro_long.csv`.
