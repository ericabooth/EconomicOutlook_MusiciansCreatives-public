## Caveats

- MIT's Living Wage Calculator is a budget-based (expenditure-minimum) measure, not a market-observed statistic — it models a no-frills basic-needs budget (food, housing, medical, transportation, child care, civic, internet/mobile, taxes) and solves for the hourly wage needed to cover it working full-time (2,080 hours/year). It is a different concept from Zillow's market-rate rent index, HUD's FMR administrative benchmark, or Census ACS actual-reported median rent — useful as a "what does it take to get by" anchor, not as a direct rent/price observation.
- The implied monthly housing figure here ($1,518) is broadly consistent with (slightly below) the other three cost-of-living sources gathered in this folder for the same period — HUD's FY2026 1-bedroom FMR for Austin is $1,562, and Zillow's Austin ZORI (seasonally adjusted) was $1,653 as of June 2026 — which is a reasonable cross-check, not an exact match (the calculator's housing line item likely reflects a specific unit-size/sharing assumption not fully re-derived here).
- This snapshot captures ONE point in time (as built 2026-02-15) for ONE geography (Travis County). It is not a time series; MIT does not publish an easily accessible historical archive of past calculator vintages on this same page, so no trend was attempted here given the optional/time-remains status of this item.
- Only the 1-adult household figures were extracted in detail; 2-adult household figures are visible on the source page but not transcribed here, for time reasons.
