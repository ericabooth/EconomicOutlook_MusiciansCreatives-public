# Georgia administrative rules — Musical Tax Credit

Two rules govern the credit: the Department of Revenue rule (tax administration) and the Department
of Economic Development rule (project certification).

---

## 1. Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. R. 560-7-8-.61 — Musical Tax Credit (Department of Revenue)

- **URL:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/georgia/Ga-Comp-R-Regs-R-560-7-8-.61
- **Retrieved:** 2026-08-01
- **Publisher:** Cornell Legal Information Institute (LII) reproduction of the Georgia Administrative Code
- **Rule vintage:** originally adopted November 17, 2017, effective December 7, 2017; amended June 4, 2025, effective June 24, 2025

### Extracted substance

**Credit rate.** Base credit is 15 percent of the qualified production expenditures. An additional
5 percent applies for qualified production expenditures incurred in a tier 1 or tier 2 county
(maximum combined rate 20 percent).

**Expenditure thresholds by project type.**

| Project type | Minimum annual qualified production expenditures |
|---|---|
| Musical or theatrical performance | $500,000 |
| Recorded music incorporated into film / TV / interactive entertainment | $250,000 |
| Other recorded musical performance | $100,000 |

**Annual aggregate caps.**

| Taxable year | Aggregate cap | Per-company limit |
|---|---|---|
| 2018 | $5 million | 20% of the annual aggregate |
| 2019 | $10 million | 20% of the annual aggregate |
| 2020–2022 | $15 million | 20% of the annual aggregate |

**Sunset language (quoted).** "The musical tax credit shall not be available for taxable years
beginning on or after January 1, 2023."

**Preapproval process.** Companies obtain pre-certification from the Department of Economic
Development, then electronically submit Form IT-MC-AP through the Georgia Tax Center. Credits are
allocated on a "first-come, first-served basis," with submission date determining priority.

**Carryforward.** Credits may be carried forward for five years from the close of the taxable year in
which earned. No carryback.

**Withholding offset.** Excess credits may offset future withholding tax obligations via Form IT-WH,
filed within the statute of limitations period.

## 2. Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. R. 159-1-2-.01 — Musical Tax Credit (Department of Economic Development)

- **URL:** https://rules.sos.ga.gov/gac/159-1-2
- **Retrieved:** 2026-08-01
- **Publisher:** Georgia Secretary of State, Georgia Administrative Code (Subject 159-1-2)

### Extracted substance

**Three-stage process.**

1. **Pre-certification.** Production companies apply to the Department of Economic Development
   before the project begins. GDEcD has 30 days to issue a pre-certification letter.
2. **Preapproval.** With the pre-certification letter, companies apply to the Department of Revenue.
   This stage is first-come, first-served subject to the annual cap.
3. **Certification.** After project completion, companies reapply to GDEcD within 60 days. GDEcD
   verifies the finished project met all requirements and issues a certification letter, which DOR uses
   to administer the credit.

**Credit rate.** 15 percent of the qualified production expenditures; "an additional 5 percent shall be
allowed for qualified production expenditures incurred in a tier 1 or tier 2 county." Tier designations
are set by the Commissioner of Community Affairs.

**Project type definitions.**

1. *Musical or theatrical performance* — expenditures incurred to prepare a performance, or series of
   performances, for a paying live audience.
2. *Recorded musical performance (media-integrated)* — recorded music incorporated into or
   synchronized with a movie, television, or interactive entertainment production.
3. *Other recorded musical performance* — any other recorded music project meeting the $100,000
   threshold.

**Caps and sunset.** Same schedule as the DOR rule; sunset for taxable years beginning on or after
January 1, 2023.

---

## Caveats

- Note a discrepancy worth flagging: the Georgia DOR web page and several secondary summaries
  describe the bonus as a **tier 1** county bonus, while both administrative rules and the state Tax
  Expenditure Report describe it as applying to **tier 1 or tier 2** counties. The rules and the Tax
  Expenditure Report are the more authoritative sources; treat the credit as 15% base plus 5% for
  tier 1 **or** tier 2 county spend.
- Neither rule contains utilization data.
