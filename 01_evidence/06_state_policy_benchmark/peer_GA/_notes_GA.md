## 1. Headline

**RESOLVED 2026-08-01 — the DOAA evaluation flagged below as missing was subsequently retrieved. It changes the headline. Read this section, not the "under $1 million" framing that follows in section 4.**

Georgia's flagship music incentive, the **Georgia Music Investment Act** credit, is **sunset and gone** — and over its entire five-year life it **awarded exactly zero dollars.**

From the Georgia Department of Audits and Accounts evaluation, *Georgia Music Tax Credit: Economic and Fiscal Impact Analysis*, prepared by Georgia Southern University's Center for Business Analytics and Economic Research, dated **December 8, 2023**, verbatim:

> "The analysis revealed that throughout the GMTC's active period, **no tax credits were awarded**. Despite receiving **six applications** for the tax credit, **none of these were approved**, resulting in no company using the credit provisions."

Mechanism of failure, verbatim from the report's Economic Contribution Analysis:

> "This analysis is based on the six applications retained by the DOR for the GMTC program. These six applications **lacked the pre-certification letter** for the GDEcD required under the Georgia Music Tax Credit rules. These applications were forwarded to GDEcD and were **denied the pre-certification**. Following another search of records by DOR, it was determined that **no tax credit was issued** under this tax credit."

Scale of what was even requested: had all six applications been approved, "the total amount of tax credit issued would have reached **$561,000**" across 2018–2022 combined. Against a $15,000,000 annual cap in the final three years alone, that is about **3.7 percent of a single year's ceiling**, spread over five years. The report models a hypothetical of roughly 45 jobs and $6.89 million in economic impact had the credits been granted, generating $160,900 in combined state and local tax revenue ($103,700 state, $51,200 local) over the whole period.

**Why it failed, per the evaluators (verbatim):**
> "The GMTC's required minimum qualified expenditure of $500,000 was notably higher than that of other states' programs. Further, the tax credit of 15 to 20 percent was less attractive compared to the 25 to 35 percent incentives available in other states. Industry professionals cite implementation issues as a primary reason for the GMTC's limited success. Initially, the credit was intended to boost recorded music performances for productions shot outside of Georgia, while in-state productions could benefit from the existing Film Tax Credit. This dual focus led to confusion among applicants and reviewers alike."

Additional design exclusions that narrowed the funnel: the credit excluded recorded music as such; excluded single musical performances not intended to tour; excluded music and cultural festivals not intended to tour; excluded industry seminars, trade shows, and markets. Live productions had to originate and premiere in Georgia and the resulting tour had to run 12 months or longer.

**This is the single most useful peer-state fact in the file.** Georgia enacted a headline music tax credit with a $15 million annual cap and delivered nothing, because the eligibility architecture and the two-agency pre-certification handoff screened out every applicant. It is the cleanest available counterexample to reading a statutory cap as evidence of state support.

## 3. Credit design

**Rate.** 15 percent of qualified production expenditures, **plus an additional 5 percent** for qualified
production expenditures incurred in a **tier 1 or tier 2 county** (maximum 20 percent).

> Discrepancy flagged: some secondary summaries say the 5-point bonus applies only in tier 1
> counties. Both administrative rules and the state Tax Expenditure Report say "tier 1 or tier 2."
> Use tier 1 or tier 2.

**Minimum spend thresholds** (minimum annual qualified production expenditures, by project type):

| Project type | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Musical or theatrical performance (live, for a paying audience) | $500,000 |
| Recorded musical performance synchronized with / incorporated into a movie, television, or interactive entertainment production | $250,000 |
| Other recorded musical performance | $100,000 |

**Annual aggregate cap schedule and per-company maximum:**

| Taxable years beginning | Annual aggregate cap | Per-company max (= 20% of aggregate) |
|---|---|---|
| 1/1/2018 – before 1/1/2019 | $5,000,000 | $1,000,000 |
| 1/1/2019 – before 1/1/2020 | $10,000,000 | $2,000,000 |
| 1/1/2020 – before 1/1/2023 | $15,000,000 | $3,000,000 |

**Allocation.** First-come, first-served by preapproval submission date.

**Carryforward.** Five years from the close of the taxable year in which earned; no carryback.

**Other.** Excess credit may offset the production company's Georgia withholding tax liability
(Form IT-WH).

## 5. Sunset / current status as of 2026-08-01

**Status: SUNSET. Not enacted-and-funded. No successor tax credit.**

- The credit is unavailable for taxable years beginning on or after **January 1, 2023**. This sunset was
  written into HB 155 at enactment; it was a five-year program by design.
- **It was not extended.** A 2023 effort to renew the credit did not pass. Confirmed by the Georgia
  Tax Expenditure Report FY 2025, which states flatly: "This credit was repealed effective
  January 1, 2023."
- **HB 1181 (2024) did not extend or revive it.** HB 1181 shortened carryforward periods for most
  Georgia income tax credits (10 years to 5) and for the Qualified Education Expense and Film credits
  (5 years to 3), and added 2029 sunset dates to several other incentives, effective January 1, 2025.
  The musical credit had already expired and is not among the programs HB 1181 touches.
  *Caveat: this is drawn from law-firm summaries of the 2024 session, not from the bill text itself.*
- Justia's code database shows O.C.G.A. § 48-7-40.33 as "[Reserved]" in the 2024 Georgia Code, with
  the section removed by **2023 Ga. Laws 353, § 7, effective July 1, 2023** — a housekeeping repeal of
  the already-expired section. *Caveat: obtained from a search-result summary of Justia; the Justia
  page itself was Cloudflare-blocked and could not be verified directly.*
- **No 2025 or 2026 amendment reinstating the musical tax credit was found.** Separately, Georgia
  HB 463 repealed 17 tax credits effective January 1, 2026 — the musical credit is not among them
  because it was already gone.

## 6. Non-tax Georgia music policy

**HB 353 (2025–2026 Regular Session) — Georgia Music Office.** Would establish a **Georgia Music
Office within the Department of Community Affairs**, tasked with promoting music-related
investment, workforce development, and interagency coordination; and would create a **Music Ready
Communities / "Music Friendly Georgia Certified Communities"** certification program for local
governments. Reporting indicates the bill cleared a House committee in February 2025, passed both
chambers, and was transmitted to Governor Kemp in **April 2026**. Reporting also indicates the office
was **not immediately funded** — appropriations were expected to be taken up in the following session,
and full operation was described as at least a year out.

> **STATUS UNVERIFIED — treat as provisional.** Whether Governor Kemp signed HB 353, the Act
> number, and the effective date could not be confirmed. legiscan.com, the Governor's signed-legislation
> archive, and the Georgia General Assembly legislation API were all blocked or returned 404. The
> only source located is a secondary news aggregator (hoodline.com, April 2026), which is **low
> reliability**. Before citing HB 353 in the white paper, verify against the Georgia General Assembly
> bill page or the Governor's office. Classify as **enacted-but-unfunded at best**, and possibly
> **passed-but-unsigned**, pending verification.

Also relevant but not researched in depth here: the **Georgia Council for the Arts** (grantmaking, within
GDEcD) and **Georgia Music Partners** (georgiamusicpartners.org), the private advocacy group that
drove the Georgia Music Investment Act.

## 8. Terminology discipline for the white paper

Georgia's musical credit had five distinct stages. With the DOAA evaluation in hand, the pipeline is
now fully documented, and it terminates at stage 2:

1. **Statutory cap** — $5M (TY2018), $10M (TY2019), $15M/year (TY2020–2022). A ceiling, not a spend.
2. **Applied** — six applications reached DOR across the credit's entire five-year life. All six lacked
   the required GDEcD pre-certification letter; GDEcD **denied pre-certification on all six**.
3. **Preapproved** (DOR reserves cap space, first-come first-served) — **none**.
4. **Certified** (GDEcD confirms the completed project) — **none**.
5. **Claimed** (taxpayer uses the credit against income tax or withholding) — **$0**.

Reconciliation of the two utilization sources: the Tax Expenditure Report's "(m) — less than
$1 million" is not a small positive number. It is censoring **zero**. The DOAA figure is the one to
cite; the Tax Expenditure Report corroborates it as an upper bound but cannot express the true value.

Language to use: "no credits were ever awarded," "zero dollars claimed," "all six applications denied
pre-certification." Language to avoid: "underused," "modestly used," "under $1 million per year" —
each implies some nonzero take-up. Also avoid citing the report's 45 jobs / $6.89M / $160,900 figures
as Georgia outcomes; they are the evaluators' counterfactual of what *would* have happened had the
six applications been approved.
