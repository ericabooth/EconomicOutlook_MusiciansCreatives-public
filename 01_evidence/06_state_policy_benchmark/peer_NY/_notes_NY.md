# New York — State Music/Live-Entertainment Policy Benchmark

Evidence notes for Texas 2036 white paper on state music-economy policy.
Compiled 2026-08-01. All retrieval dates 2026-08-01 unless noted.

---

## CRITICAL CORRECTION UP FRONT

New York runs **two separate and distinct** musical/theatrical production credits. The
research brief conflated them by citing "§ 24-a" for the NYC program. That is wrong:

| | **NYC program** | **Upstate program** |
|---|---|---|
| Statute | **Tax Law § 24-c** | **Tax Law § 24-a** |
| Common name | New York City Musical and Theatrical Production Tax Credit | Empire State Musical and Theatrical Production Tax Credit |
| Enacted | 2021 | 2015 |
| Geography | Manhattan only | Upstate NY qualified regional theaters |
| Cap structure | $550M **aggregate, program-lifetime** | **$8M per year** |
| Purpose | Broadway / Off-Broadway runs | Pre-tour activity + technical rehearsals for touring shows |

The ESD document titled "Musical and Theatrical Tax Credit Annual Report" is the
**upstate (§ 24-a)** report. ESD does **not** appear to publish an equivalent public
annual report for the NYC (§ 24-c) program — see "Gaps" below.

Also note for framing: **neither program is a music-economy program in the
recorded-music / commercial-music sense.** Both subsidize live theatrical stage
productions. § 24-c expressly *excludes* "ballet, opera, musical solo, group, band, or
orchestra performance," and stand-up comedy. If the white paper is benchmarking
support for musicians and commercial music, NY's relevance is as a *live-performance
sector subsidy* analogue, not a music-industry incentive.

## PROGRAM 1 (primary): New York City Musical and Theatrical Production Tax Credit

### Authority and administration
- **Statute:** N.Y. Tax Law **§ 24-c**. Cross-references in the statute text are to
  Tax Law § 210-B(57) and § 606(mmm).
  - *Caveat:* the NYS Tax Department's Tax Expenditure Report cites this credit as
    "Tax Law §§ 210-B(40), 606(xxx)." The statute text and the TER disagree on the
    subdivision letters/numbers. Cite § 24-c as the primary authority and verify the
    cross-references before publication.
- **Enacting vehicle:** FY2022 New York State Enacted Budget, April 2021. Statutory
  reference date for a qualifying "first performance" is **after April 19, 2021**.
  - *Not verified:* the specific chapter/part designation (commonly reported as
    Chapter 59 of the Laws of 2021). **Not found** in primary sources this pass.
- **Administering agency:** Empire State Development (ESD). Allocation is by ESD;
  the credit is claimed on the tax return.
- **Effective window:** tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2021 and before
  January 1, 2028 (NYS Tax Expenditure Report, FY2027 edition).

### Rate and caps
- **Credit rate:** **25%** of qualified production expenditures. Fully **refundable**.
- **Per-production caps:**
  - **Level 1** facility (Manhattan, bounded by and including 41st–54th Streets and
    6th–9th Avenues; 500+ seats; live theater ≥75% of gross receipts): **up to $3,000,000**.
  - **Level 2** facility (Manhattan; 100+ seats; live theater ≥75% of gross receipts):
    **up to $350,000**, and the production must have a budget ≥ $750,000 **and** incur
    ≥ $750,000 in qualified production expenditures.
  - Personal compensation expenses are capped at **$200,000 per week** in the
    qualified-expenditure base.
- **RESOLVED — the $1.5M question.** The brief asked whether the per-production cap
  changed to $1.5M or $1.75M. Findings:
  - The **current statute text of § 24-c contains no $1,500,000 figure** and sets no
    date-based distinction. Caps are $3M (Level 1) / $350,000 (Level 2), flat.
    (Verified 2026-08-01 against nysenate.gov TAX/24-C.)
  - However, the **FY2026 Executive Budget tax expenditure proposals** page
    (tax.ny.gov, published Jan 2025) described the credit as
    "$3 million (productions before 1/1/2023) or **$1.5 million** (productions on/after
    1/1/2023)." The **FY2027** edition of the same page describes it as a flat
    "$3 million / $350,000" with no date split.
  - **Award data contradicts a live $1.5M cap:** shows that opened in 2024 received
    $2.5M ("Tammy Faye") and $2.6M ("Lempicka") — above $1.5M.
  - **Reading:** a reduced $1.5M cap for post-1/1/2023 productions appears to have
    existed at some point in the statute's amendment history but is **not in force now**.
    **$1.75M is not supported by any source found.** Do not publish the $1.5M figure as
    current law. Recommend a targeted check of the 2022 and 2023 budget-bill amendment
    history before making any claim about the cap's trajectory.

### Aggregate program cap — history
| Step | Amount | Vehicle / timing | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial | **$100M** (one year) | FY2022 Enacted Budget, Apr 2021 | Press-reported (NY Focus) |
| Increase | **$200M** | (year not verified) | **Not verified** — press-reported only |
| Increase | **$300M** | reflected in ESD guidelines rev. 07/05/2024 | Primary (ESD guidelines PDF) |
| Increase | **$400M** | FY2026 Enacted Budget, signed May 9, 2025 | Primary (TER: "for tax years beginning or after January 1, 2025, an additional $100 million in total credit is available") |
| Increase | **$550M** | FY2027 Enacted Budget, May 2026 (+$150M) | **Primary — now in statute** |

- **Current statutory language:** "The aggregate amount of tax credits allowed under
  this section … shall be **five hundred fifty million dollars**." (§ 24-c, verified 2026-08-01)
- This is a **program-lifetime aggregate**, not an annual cap. Per NY Focus, recent
  extensions effectively ran the program at roughly **$50M/year**, down from the
  original $100M/year; the Broadway League is seeking restoration to $100M/year.

### Sunset / status
- **Credit period ends September 30, 2027.**
- **Statute repealed January 1, 2028** ("NB Repealed January 1, 2028").
- **All initial applications must be submitted before June 30, 2027.**
- Application timing (ESD program page, as of 2026-08-01):
  - First paid performance Dec 1, 2025 – Jun 30, 2026: initial application requirement
    waived up front, but must be submitted by June 30, 2026.
  - First paid performance July 1, 2026 onward: initial application required in advance.
- **STATUS AS OF 2026-08-01: ENACTED, FUNDED, AND ACTIVE**, with a $550M lifetime cap
  and a hard sunset 14 months out (Sept 30, 2027).

### Distinctive design features worth flagging for TX
- **Clawback / revenue-share:** a production earning ongoing revenue ≥ **200%** of its
  ongoing production costs must contribute **up to 50%** of total credits received to the
  **New York State Council on the Arts cultural program fund**, payable from 25% of net
  operating profits, monthly, until the 50% threshold is reached. Funds are earmarked for
  arts education and arts/cultural programs.
  - **Utilization of the clawback: ZERO.** Per ESD, more than five years in, **no show has
    ever triggered the provision** (ESD, reported by NY Focus, 2026-03-19). This is a
    strong cautionary data point for any TX design that relies on a profit-sharing payback.
- **Equity conditions:** initial applications must include (1) a diversity and arts job
  training program plan (a paid multi-year fellowship; pre-approved programs are The
  Theatre Leadership Project and the Black Theatre Coalition Fellowship Program), and
  (2) a public access plan for low- or no-cost access for low-income New Yorkers.
- **Nonprofit exclusion + observed workaround:** nonprofit-produced shows are ineligible;
  several nonprofit theaters reportedly created for-profit corporations to capture the
  credit (reported by BroadwayWorld, via NY Focus).
- **Functions as a grant, not a tax offset:** because it is refundable, the state
  reimburses expenses regardless of tax liability.

## UTILIZATION — the three different numbers (do not conflate)

This is the most important analytical point in the NY file. **Authorized ≠ allocated ≠ claimed.**

### (a) AUTHORIZED (statutory lifetime cap)
- **$550,000,000** as of 2026-08-01.

### (b) ALLOCATED / AWARDED (ESD administrative records)
Source: New York Focus, "New York Gave These Broadway Shows Millions — And They Flopped
Anyway," Nick Garber, **2026-03-19**, based on a list **obtained via Freedom of
Information Law request from ESD**. ESD does not publish this list.

- **$308 million awarded** to **150 productions** between **August 2022 and February 2026**
  - **129 Broadway shows** + **21 Off-Broadway shows**
- **At least $179 million** went to Broadway shows with **fewer than 200 performances each**
- **135 of the 150** shows (all but 15) had **closed** as of the reporting date
- Selected per-show awards:

| Production | Award | Run length noted |
|---|---|---|
| "Mrs. Doubtfire" | $3.0M (max) | 77 performances |
| "The Lion King" | $3.0M (max) | long-running hit |
| "The Book of Mormon" | $3.0M (max) | long-running hit |
| "Wicked" | $3.0M (max) | long-running hit |
| "Chicago" | $3.0M (max) | long-running hit |
| "Lempicka" | $2.6M | ~1 month, 2024 |
| "Tammy Faye" | $2.5M | 29 performances |
| "KPOP" | $1.9M | 17 performances (~$110,000+/performance) |

- **Fund exhaustion:** the fund **unexpectedly ran dry** roughly two years before its
  June 2027 end date, prompting Gov. Hochul's January 2026 executive-budget proposal
  for the additional $150M.

### (c) CLAIMED on tax returns (NYS Department of Taxation and Finance)
Source: **Annual Report on New York State Tax Expenditures, FY2027 edition**, Table 8
("2026 New York State cross-article tax credits estimates, in millions of dollars").
Retrieved and parsed directly from tax.ny.gov 2026-08-01.

**NYC musical and theatrical production tax credit (§ 24-c) — actual claims:**

| Tax year | Personal income tax | Corporate franchise tax | **Total** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | $0.0M | $14.5M | **$14.5M** |
| 2022 | $8.9M | $76.5M | **$85.4M** |
| 2023 | $2.7M | *not yet available* | *incomplete* |
| **Forecast 2026** | $10.0M | $90.0M | **$100.0M** |

- The 2023 column carries footnote 1: **"Data for non-personal income tax items are not
  yet available."** So 2023 is a **partial** figure — do not present $2.7M as the 2023 total.
- **Vintage revision to note:** the prior (FY2026) edition of the same table forecast
  **$50.0M** for 2025. The FY2027 edition forecasts **$100.0M** for 2026. The state
  roughly doubled its own forward estimate year over year.
- **Reconciliation caveat:** claims ($14.5M + $85.4M ≈ $100M through 2022) run well
  behind awards ($308M through Feb 2026) because credits are allocated on completion
  and claimed in later tax years. The two series are **not** directly comparable in the
  same year. Say so explicitly if both appear in the paper.

### Program effectiveness evidence
- A **2023 state-commissioned study** reportedly found the credit generated
  **23 cents for every dollar** the state invested. Cited by NY Focus (2026-03-19).
  - **The study itself was NOT obtained.** Treat the 23-cent figure as secondhand until
    the underlying report is retrieved. This is the single highest-value outstanding item
    in the NY file.
- The **Citizens Budget Commission** (fiscally conservative watchdog) accepted the
  program as a 2021 emergency measure but **opposes its expansion** now that the
  industry has recovered.
- **State Senator James Skoufis** (D, Hudson Valley), a critic, argues the per-job
  subsidy is understated because theatrical jobs are inherently short-lived:
  sustaining one career may require "10, 11, 15 shots of the Broadway tax credit."
- ESD's defense (spokesperson Emily Mijatovic): the program brought back stagehands,
  musicians, costumers and crews after a ~2-year shutdown; ESD argues focusing on short
  runs is misleading because limited runs are normal in live theater.
- At an eight-hour budget hearing in Feb 2026, ESD President Hope Knight reportedly
  offered **no specific figures** when asked what data supported the expansion.

## PROGRAM 2: Empire State Musical and Theatrical Production Credit (upstate, § 24-a)

- **Statute:** N.Y. Tax Law §§ **24-a**, 210-B(47), 606(u). Refundable.
- **Effective:** tax years beginning on/after Jan 1, 2015 and before Jan 1, 2026
  (TER FY2026 edition). The FY2026 Executive Budget proposed a **four-year extension
  through tax year 2029**; enactment of that extension **not independently verified**.
- **Agency:** Empire State Development.
- **Rate:** 25% of qualified production expenditures **plus transportation expenditures**.
- **Cap:** **$8 million per year** (annual, unlike the NYC program's lifetime cap).
- **Purpose:** incentivize touring productions to do technical rehearsals and pre-tour
  activity, and to perform, at qualified regional theaters in Upstate NY — explicitly
  positioned to compete with other Northeast states for tour launches.

### Actual utilization — CY2024 (ESD Annual Report, authored Jan 2025)
Downloaded: `ESD_MT-Annual-Report-2024.pdf`

**Initial applications, CY2024:** 7 productions applied. Estimated:
- $23M production expenditures at qualified production facilities (incl. labor)
- $3M additional NYS expenditures (transportation, travel, hotels)
- 565 hires by qualified touring productions
- 448 hires by qualified production facilities
- $6.5M in estimated tax credits

**Credits ACTUALLY ISSUED, CY2024: three, totaling $2,142,537.**

| Project | Company | Credit-eligible hours | Credit-eligible wages | Tax credit issued | Total qualified costs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frozen – 2019 Tour | Buena Vista Theatrical Group Ltd. | 6,880 | $4,762,400 | $1,401,042 | $5,604,166 |
| Oklahoma | Cain't Say No LLC | 12,672 | $327,800 | $386,253 | $1,545,013 |
| Pretty Woman | Pretty Woman Productions Inc | 15,665 | $465,304 | $355,242 | $1,420,968 |
| **Totals** | | **35,217** | **$5,555,504** | **$2,142,537** | **$8,570,147** |

- **Key observation:** only **$2.14M of the $8M annual authorization was actually
  issued** in CY2024 — roughly **27% utilization**. The upstate program is
  chronically undersubscribed relative to its cap.

### Claimed on tax returns (TER FY2027, Table 8)
| Tax year | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | Forecast 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total | $0.8M | $2.3M | $0.0M | $1.6M | $0.7M | $8.0M |

(PIT amounts were below $0.1M in 2018, 2019 and 2022; 2020 was $0.0M — the COVID shutdown year.)

- **The forecast of $8.0M equals the statutory cap** — i.e., the state forecasts full
  utilization, but realized claims have never exceeded $2.3M in any year observed.
  Flag this as an example of a tax-expenditure forecast that tracks the cap rather
  than behavior.

## GAPS / NOT FOUND

1. **No public ESD annual report for the NYC (§ 24-c) program.** § 24-c does not appear
   to impose the annual reporting requirement that § 24-a does. ESD does not publish the
   per-show award list; NY Focus had to FOIL it. **This is itself a finding** — the larger
   of New York's two programs is the less transparent one, by an order of magnitude
   ($308M allocated vs. $2.1M issued upstate in CY2024).
2. **The 2023 state-commissioned effectiveness study (23 cents per dollar) was not
   obtained.** Highest-value outstanding retrieval.
3. **Aggregate cap history $100M → $200M → $300M is not fully documented from primary
   budget sources.** The $400M and $550M steps are solid; the $200M step is press-reported only.
4. **Enacting chapter/part citation for the 2021 statute not verified.**
5. **The $1.5M per-production cap amendment history is unresolved** — the figure appears
   in a 2025 state budget document but not in current statute, and award data contradicts it.
6. **ESD annual reports for 2025 and 2026 do not exist** on the ESD media center
   (`.../musical-and-theatrical-tax-credit-annual-report-2025` returns HTTP 404 as of
   2026-08-01). The 2024 edition is the most recent.
7. Optional context items (NYC Mayor's Office of Media and Entertainment "Made in NY"
   music initiatives; NYSCA music funding) **not pursued** — the session's web-search
   budget was exhausted before reaching them.

## METHOD NOTE
esd.ny.gov and nysenate.gov sit behind a Cloudflare challenge that blocks plain `curl`.
PDFs and statute text were retrieved through the WebFetch tool and text-extracted
locally with `pdftotext`. No bot-detection was circumvented. Table 8 figures were parsed
programmatically from the saved HTML rather than read off a rendered page, and the
FY2026 numbers were re-verified after an initial misread (an early pass mistakenly
assigned the "Forecast 2025" column value of $50.0M to tax year 2022).
