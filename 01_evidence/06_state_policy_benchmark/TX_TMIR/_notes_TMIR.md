- **HB 2806 (86R, 2019)**, Morrison, carried the identical caption ("Relating to the creation of the Texas music incubator rebate program…"). Passed Senate April 26, 2021 (22-9); passed House May 8, 2021 (102-40, one present not voting). **Effective September 1, 2021.** Codified at **Government Code ch. 485, Subchapter C, §§ 485.041–485.047.**
  Saved: `SB609_87R_enrolled.pdf` / `.txt`. Source: https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/87R/billtext/pdf/SB00609F.pdf

Use SB 609 / 2021 in the paper. HB 2806 is worth one sentence as the two-session lead-up.

## Funding mechanism (this is unusual and worth explaining)

TMIR is **not** funded by a discretionary appropriation of general revenue. SB 609 §§ 2–3 created a permanent statutory diversion of two existing alcohol taxes into a dedicated account:

| Source | Statute added by SB 609 | Amount per fiscal year |
|---|---|---|
| Mixed beverage gross receipts tax (Tax Code ch. 183) | Tax Code § 183.023(c) | **$10,000,000** |
| Sales tax on beer and wine (Tax Code ch. 151) | Tax Code § 151.801(f) | **$100,000** |
| **Total** | | **$10,100,000/year = $20,200,000/biennium** |

Both flow to the **Texas Music Incubator Account No. 5193**, a dedicated account in the general revenue fund (Gov't Code § 485.046), administered by Governor – Fiscal (Agency 300). Money in the account "may be appropriated only to the office for the purpose of paying rebates."

**Sweep provision (§ 485.046(d)):** "On the last day of each state fiscal biennium, the comptroller shall transfer any money deposited to the account… that is unobligated and unexpended on that date to the general revenue fund." Unspent rebate money does not roll forward — it reverts. Comptroller's Manual of Accounts confirms: "Accumulative. At the end of each biennium, any unexpended balance in the fund is transferred to General Revenue."
Source: https://fmcpa.cpa.state.tx.us/fiscalmoa/fund.jsp?num=5193 (retrieved 2026-08-01)

**LBB fiscal note (SB 609, as introduced, April 6, 2021):** estimated "a negative impact of ($20,200,000) through the biennium ending August 31, 2023," and a five-year table showing ($10,100,000) GR loss / $10,100,000 gain to the Texas Music Incubator Account in each of FY2022–FY2026. Saved: `SB609_fn_I.html`.
Source: https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/87R/fiscalnotes/html/SB00609I.htm

## Enacted 2021, but not funded until 2023 — a two-year gap

This is the sharpest "enacted vs. funded" fact in the Texas file.

- SB 609 § 4 required the office to **establish the program by September 1, 2022** and to **begin accepting applications September 1, 2022**.
- SB 609 § 5 made that contingent: "The Music, Film, Television, and Multimedia Office and the comptroller of public accounts are required to implement a provision of this Act only if the legislature appropriates money specifically for that purpose."
- **The 2022-23 General Appropriations Act (SB 1, 87R) contains no appropriation for the Texas Music Incubator Account.** Verified by full-text search of the enacted GAA; the only music mention in the Governor's trusteed bill pattern is the combined $45,000,000 GR for "the Texas Film Commission, the Texas Music Office, and the Moving Image Industry Incentive Program (MIIIP)."
- The first appropriation appears in the **2024-25 GAA (HB 1, 88R), Rider 39**: "$10,100,000 in General Revenue - Dedicated Texas Music Incubator Account No. 5193 in each fiscal year of the biennium."
- Consequence: applications did not open September 1, 2022 as the statute directed. **The first round ran September 1 – November 30, 2023**, one year late, announced by the Governor's office on September 1, 2023.

## Program rules (Gov't Code §§ 485.042–485.045)

**Award ceiling (§ 485.042(b)):** the lesser of (1) mixed beverage gross receipts taxes and beer/wine sales taxes the applicant remitted in the preceding fiscal year, or (2) **$100,000**. One rebate per tax ID per fiscal year.

**Eligibility (§ 485.043(a)) — all four, for at least the two years before applying:**
1. Was a mixed beverage gross receipts tax permittee or a beer/wine sales tax permit holder.
2. If a music venue: a retail establishment with **dedicated audience capacity of not more than 3,000 persons**.
3. If a music festival promoter: held a festival in a **county with population under 100,000**.
4. Had a **written contract with a musical performance artist** for a live performance under which the artist received "a specified percentage of ticket sales for or other sales during the performance, or a guaranteed amount in advance."

**Plus at least five of eight operational criteria (§ 485.043(a)(5)), one of which must be (A) or (B):**
- (A) marketing of live music performances through printed or electronic listings
- (B) live music performances **five or more nights per week**
- (C) employing or contracting someone doing two or more of: sound engineer, booker, promoter, stage manager, security
- (D) having live performance and audience space
- (E) technical sound and lighting support, in-house or by contract
- (F) storage space for audio equipment or instruments
- (G) cover charges via ticketing or a front-door entrance fee
- (H) hours of operation coinciding with show times

**Disaster waiver (§ 485.043(b) and § 485.044(d)):** the office may, at its discretion, rebate an applicant that fails eligibility *solely* because it sits in a county declared a disaster area by the governor or president in the preceding two years, and may expedite those applications.

**Selection standard (§ 485.045(a)):** among eligible applicants, the office "shall grant rebates to eligible music venues and music festival promoters that the office determines provide or have committed to provide the most economic benefit to the communities in which the music venues are located or the festivals are held… and to the Texas music industry, including live music performers." This is a **discretionary merit standard with no published scoring rubric** — worth flagging, because it means eligibility does not equal entitlement.

**Administration:** the comptroller issues a warrant drawn on Account 5193 at the office's direction (§ 485.045(b)). Applications go through an online portal (§ 485.044(a)(2)): https://tmir-portal.gov.texas.gov/

## Design features worth interrogating in the paper

1. **The rebate is proportional to alcohol sales, not to artist payments.** A venue's award is capped by the alcohol tax it remitted. A venue that pays artists well but sells little alcohol gets little. A high-volume bar with a stage gets more. The statute requires *a* written artist contract, not a threshold level of artist compensation.
2. **The festival test is a rural test, not a size test.** Festival promoters qualify only in counties under 100,000 population. That excludes every festival in Harris, Dallas, Tarrant, Bexar, Travis, Collin, Denton, El Paso, Hidalgo, Fort Bend, and Williamson counties — including Austin's, which is the state's marquee music-festival market.
3. **The 3,000-capacity ceiling** targets independent venues and excludes arenas and amphitheaters. This is the clearest pro-small-venue design element.
4. **The two-year operating history requirement** excludes new venues, i.e. the ones with the highest failure risk.
5. **Money reverts at biennium end.** No multi-year smoothing.

## Awards actually made

| Cycle | Application window | Applications | Recipients | Total awarded | Average | Geography |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Round 1 ("FY2023" tax year, first awards) | Sept 1 – Nov 30, 2023 | **241** | **156** | **~$10 million** (reported) | **$53,000** (reported) | not published |
| Round 2 | Sept 4 – Nov 30, 2024 | 153 as of Nov 7, 2024 (final count not found) | not found | not found | not found | not found |
| Round 3 → **FY2026 awards** | Sept 2025 window; determinations from Feb 1, 2026 | not found | **180** | **$10.1 million** | **$56,111** (computed) | **60 Texas cities** |
| Round 4 | opens Sept 1, 2026 | — | — | — | — | — |

**Arithmetic caution on Round 1:** 156 recipients × $53,000 average = **$8,268,000**, not "approximately $10 million." If the true total were $10.1M the average would be $64,744. The $53,000 and the "$10 million" cannot both be right. The Denton Record-Chronicle carried both in the same article. **Treat Round 1's total as unverified**; the defensible statement is "156 recipients out of 241 applicants." Requesting the official round-by-round award table from TMO would resolve it.

**Round 1 named recipients (Denton only, from local reporting):** Andy's Bar, Dan's Silverleaf, Rubber Gloves Rehearsal Studios, Steve's Wine Bar — average award about $40,000 across the four.

**Outcome color:** KCBD (Aug 27, 2025) reported that "roughly half of 2024's applicants across the state used the money to increase the amount they pay to artists." This is the only artist-pass-through evidence located and it is second-hand reporting of a TMO statement, not a published evaluation.

**GAP — no public recipient list.** Unlike the film incentive (TMIIIP), which the 2026-27 GAA Rider 40 requires the Governor's office to report semi-annually to the LBB with "a listing of entities that were awarded funding, the date the award was announced, and the amount awarded to each recipient," **no equivalent reporting rider exists for TMIR.** There is no statutory requirement in Gov't Code ch. 485 Subchapter C to publish recipients or evaluate the program. Recipient-level data would require a Public Information Act request to the Office of the Governor.

**Utilization check:** FY2026 awards of $10.1M against a $10.1M annual dedication means the account was fully drawn in FY2026. Round 1's 156 awards out of 241 applications (64.7% approval) suggests demand exceeded the merit bar rather than the money running out, but without the total awarded this cannot be confirmed.
