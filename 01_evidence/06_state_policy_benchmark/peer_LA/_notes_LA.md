# Louisiana — state music-economy policy benchmark

Evidence notes. Compiled 2026-08-01. Evidence collection only; no analysis.

---

## 1. Headline

Louisiana's two music tax incentives — the **Sound Recording Investor Tax Credit** (R.S. 47:6023) and
the **Musical and Theatrical Production Tax Credit** (R.S. 47:6034, marketed as the Live Performance
Production Program) — **both closed to new applications on June 30, 2025**. The sound recording
sunset was imposed by the 2024 Third Extraordinary Session tax reform package. A 2025 bill to
extend and expand the sound recording credit through 2030 **passed both chambers by wide margins
and was vetoed by the Governor on June 20, 2025**.

Utilization was very low. Louisiana's own Tax Exemption Budget puts the sound recording credit's
realized revenue loss at **$47,229 (FYE 6/30/2023), $22,642 (FYE 6/30/2024), and $60,460
(FYE 6/30/2025)** against a $2.16 million annual certification cap — roughly **1 to 3 percent of the
cap**. The live performance credit ran **$758,797, $852,752, and $2,143,446** over the same three
years against a $10 million annual cap.

Neither program has ever been large enough to trigger Louisiana's mandatory return-on-investment
analysis, which by statute covers only incentives with revenue loss above $1 million.

## 2. Program A — Sound Recording Investor Tax Credit

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Program name | Sound Recording Investor Tax Credit (marketed as the Sound Recording Incentive Program) |
| Statute | La. R.S. 47:6023 |
| Enacting act | Acts 2005, No. 485 |
| Later amending acts | Acts 2007 No. 368; 2009 No. 475; 2013 No. 385; 2015 Nos. 125, 357, 412; 2016 (1st Ex. Sess.) No. 29; 2017 Nos. 275, 323, 400; 2019 No. 363; 2021 No. 401; 2024 (3rd Ex. Sess.) Nos. 5 and 11 |
| Administering agency | Louisiana Economic Development (LED), Office of Entertainment Industry Development (OEID), branded "Louisiana Entertainment," in consultation with the Louisiana Department of Revenue (LDR). LDR issues the rebate check. |
| Rules | Louisiana Administrative Code, Title 61, Part 1, Chapter 65 |

### Design

- **Project-based production credit:** 18 percent of base investment, for applications received on or
  after July 1, 2017.
- **Base investment minimum:** in excess of $25,000 generally; in excess of $10,000 for a Louisiana
  resident investor where the project is a resident copyright.
- **Company-based QMC payroll credit:** Tier 1 — 10 percent per new job with QMC payroll of
  $35,000–$66,000/year. Tier 2 — 15 percent per new job with QMC payroll of
  $66,000–$200,000/year. Minimum three new jobs at the Tier 1 wage level.
- **Annual aggregate cap:** $2,160,000 in credits **certified** per calendar year, of which **50 percent is
  reserved for Qualified Music Companies**.
- **Per-project cap:** $100,000 per project, per calendar year.
- **Allocation:** first-come, first-served.
- **Form of benefit:** issued as a rebate.

### Utilization

Source: Louisiana Tax Exemption Budget 2025–2026, LDR. Figures are **revenue loss actually realized**
(credits claimed / rebates issued), **not** credits certified and **not** the cap.

| Fiscal year ended | Revenue loss | Basis | Share of $2.16M cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6/30/2023 | $47,229 | actual | 2.2% |
| 6/30/2024 | $22,642 | actual | 1.0% |
| 6/30/2025 | $60,460 | actual (all corporate income tax) | 2.8% |
| 6/30/2026 | $22,000 | projected | 1.0% |
| 6/30/2027 | $22,000 | projected | 1.0% |

TEB narrative, verbatim: "During FYE 6-25, $60,460 in rebates were issued."

TEB footnote applying to the projections: "The estimated revenue loss for FYE 6-26 and FYE 6-27 may
not reflect all tax reform changes due to insufficient data currently available to fully assess their
impact."

### Status as of 2026-08-01

**SUNSET.** Statutory language, quoted exactly: "No credits shall be granted pursuant to the provisions
of this Section for applications received on or after July 1, 2025."

Louisiana Entertainment's program page, quoted: "The program sunsets on 6/30/25. No new
applications will be accepted after 6/30/25."

Credits may still be **earned and claimed** on applications submitted before the cutoff, so small
amounts will continue appearing in LDR reporting for several years.

## 3. Program B — Musical and Theatrical Production Tax Credit

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Program name | Musical and Theatrical Production Income Tax Credit (marketed as the Live Performance Production Program) |
| Statute | La. R.S. 47:6034 |
| Effective | Expenditures incurred on or after July 19, 2007 |
| Administering agency | Louisiana Economic Development (LED), Office of Entertainment Industry Development (OEID) |

### Design

**Credit rates by tier** (certified Louisiana expenditures):

| Certified LA expenditures | Rate |
|---|---|
| $100,000 – $300,000 | 7% |
| $300,000 – $1,000,000 | 14% |
| Over $1,000,000 | 18% |
| Louisiana resident payroll | additional 7% |

- **Minimum in-state expenditure:** $100,000
- **Annual program cap:** $10,000,000 granted by LED per fiscal year, with **$5,000,000 reserved for
  productions by nonprofit organizations**
- **Per-project cap:** $1,000,000 per year
- **Allocation:** first-come, first-served. Per the TEB: if requests exceed the $10 million annual cap,
  excess applications carry over and are treated as if submitted on the first day of the following
  fiscal year.
- **Limitation:** the credit cannot exceed the total base investment made in the production.
- **Transferability:** one-time transfer permitted to an individual or entity including a bank or lender,
  effective on written notice to LDR.

### Utilization

Source: Louisiana Tax Exemption Budget 2025–2026, LDR. Figures are **revenue loss actually realized**
(credits claimed), **not** credits certified and **not** the cap.

| Fiscal year ended | Revenue loss | Basis | Share of $10M cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6/30/2023 | $758,797 | actual | 7.6% |
| 6/30/2024 | $852,752 | actual | 8.5% |
| 6/30/2025 | $2,143,446 | actual (all corporation income tax) | 21.4% |
| 6/30/2026 | $5,616,000 | projected | 56.2% |
| 6/30/2027 | $5,504,000 | projected | 55.0% |

TEB narrative, verbatim: "During FYE 2024-2025, $2,143,446 in corporation income tax credits were
claimed."

Note the sharp projected rise in FYE 2026 and 2027. This is consistent with a pre-sunset application
surge working through certification and then being claimed, not with an expanding program. Same TEB
footnote caveat about tax reform applies to the projections.

### Independent corroboration of the R.S. 47:6034 design

Because the full statutory text of R.S. 47:6034 could not be retrieved (see source list item 8), the
design parameters above rest on the LDR Tax Exemption Budget and the Louisiana Entertainment
program page. A **third, independent source** corroborates them: the Georgia Department of Audits
and Accounts evaluation of Georgia's music credit (December 2023, saved in `../peer_GA/`) includes a
peer-state comparison table describing Louisiana's program as:

- 7 percent when investment is greater than $100,000 and less than or equal to $300,000
- 14 percent when investment is greater than $300,000 and less than or equal to $1 million
- 7 percent of payroll for Louisiana residents
- Additional credit of 0.072 percent of the amount spent to employ college technical students
- "$1 million per project"; "$10 million for all projects per given fiscal year"
- Payroll credit capped at $1 million paid to any single individual
- Credit "cannot exceed base investment in production or infrastructure projects"

Two points of difference from the Louisiana Entertainment page worth noting. The DOAA table does
**not** show the 18 percent tier for expenditures over $1 million, and it does **not** mention the
$5 million nonprofit reserve; it does add a **college technical student employment credit
(0.072 percent)** and an **infrastructure/construction eligibility** that the agency page omits. The
DOAA table is abbreviated and reflects law as of December 2023, so treat the Louisiana Entertainment
page as controlling for rates and the DOAA table as confirming order of magnitude on the caps.

### Status as of 2026-08-01

**SUNSET.** TEB, verbatim: "No new applications accepted after June 30, 2025."

Louisiana Entertainment, verbatim: "The Live Performance, Sound Recording and Qualified Music
Company programs sunset on 6/30/25. No new applications for these three programs will be accepted
after 6/30/25."

## 4. Louisiana's 2024 tax reform — the mechanism that closed the sound recording credit

The **2024 Third Extraordinary Session** overhauled Louisiana's tax code. Per LDR's own legislative
summary (Act 5, House Bill 2):

> "Establishes a June 30, 2025 sunset for multiple incentive programs including the Louisiana work
> opportunity tax credit, Louisiana Qualify Jobs Program, Angel Investor Tax Credit Program, Sound
> Recording Investor Tax Credit, and Enterprise Zones program. No credits can be granted to
> applications submitted after that date; however, credits can continue to be earned pursuant to
> applications and contracts submitted prior to June 30, 2025."

The same act also repealed "expired and underutilized income and franchise tax credits and
incentives." The sound recording credit survived outright repeal but received a hard sunset.

For context on how the same package treated the larger entertainment incentives: the motion picture
production credit's annual issuance cap was cut from $150M to $125M beginning July 1, 2025, the
claiming cap cut from $180M to $125M, and cap rollover prohibited.

**Caveat:** LDR's summary lists the sound recording credit but not the musical and theatrical credit
among the programs given a June 30, 2025 sunset. The June 30, 2025 sunset for R.S. 47:6034 is
documented in the LDR Tax Exemption Budget and on the Louisiana Entertainment page, but the
specific act that imposed it was **not identified**. Worth pinning down before publication.

## 5. HB 653 (2025 Regular Session) — the extension that was vetoed

This is the most consequential recent development and it is easy to get wrong, because the bill passed
overwhelmingly and is widely reported as an expansion.

**What HB 653 would have done** (from the enrolled text, saved as `LA_HB653_2025_enrolled.pdf`):

- Transferred administration of the sound recording credit from **Louisiana Economic Development**
  to the **office of cultural development** within the **Department of Culture, Recreation and Tourism**
- Raised the project-based production credit from **18 percent to 25 percent**
- Lowered the base investment minimum from **$25,000 to $10,000** generally, and from **$10,000 to
  $5,000** for Louisiana residents with a resident copyright
- Raised the QMC payroll credit from **10 percent to 15 percent** (Tier 1) and from **15 percent to
  20 percent** (Tier 2)
- **Kept** the $2,160,000 annual aggregate cap, the 50 percent QMC reserve, and the $100,000
  per-project per-calendar-year cap unchanged
- Extended the application window: no credits granted for applications received on or after
  **July 1, 2030** (replacing July 1, 2025)
- Authorized emergency rulemaking, subject to oversight by the House Committee on Ways and Means
  and the Senate Committee on Revenue and Fiscal Affairs
- Applied to taxable periods beginning January 1, 2025; effective on the governor's signature

**No Act number was assigned.** Sponsors: Representatives Davis and Mandie Landry; Senators Foil,

plausible and should be checked directly against the Louisiana Legislature's 2025 veto session record
and the 2026 Regular Session bill index before publication.

## 6. ACTUAL UTILIZATION — consolidated, with source discipline

Both series come from the same source and the same measurement basis.

| Program | FYE 6/30/2023 | FYE 6/30/2024 | FYE 6/30/2025 | FYE 6/30/2026 (proj.) | FYE 6/30/2027 (proj.) | Annual cap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sound Recording Investor Tax Credit | $47,229 | $22,642 | $60,460 | $22,000 | $22,000 | $2,160,000 certified/CY |
| Musical & Theatrical Productions Tax Credit | $758,797 | $852,752 | $2,143,446 | $5,616,000 | $5,504,000 | $10,000,000 granted/FY |

**Units and basis.** Nominal dollars. State fiscal years ending June 30. The measure is LDR's estimated
**revenue loss** — the value of credits actually **claimed** against tax liability (or, for sound recording,
rebates issued) in that fiscal year. FYE 2026 and 2027 are **LDR projections**, not actuals.

**Do not conflate with:**
- the **statutory cap** ($2.16M and $10M) — a ceiling on certification, never approached by claims;
- credits **certified** by LED — no public year-by-year series was found;
- credits **granted / initially certified** — no public year-by-year series was found.

**Cross-check within the source.** The FYE 6/30/2025 figures appear twice in the Tax Exemption Budget
and agree: once in the five-year revenue-loss table (Sound Recording $60,460; Musical & Theatrical
$2,143,446) and once in the by-tax-type table, where both are attributed entirely to **corporation
income tax** ($0 individual, $0 fiduciary, N/A franchise). The program narratives repeat the same two
figures in prose.

### The ROI report gap — important negative finding

The LDR **"Return on Investment Analysis for Selected Louisiana Tax Incentive Programs"** report
(the known-good URL supplied for this task) was downloaded and read in full. **It does not cover
either music program.**

The edition retrieved is the **2024** analysis, covering **FY 2023**, published January 2025. Under
R.S. 47:1517.1, LDR must perform ROI analysis only "for all tax incentives for which the revenue loss
was one million dollars or more in the previous fiscal year." The report accordingly covers ten
programs: Motion Picture Investor, Enterprise Zone, Quality Jobs, Digital Interactive Media & Software,
Rehabilitation of Historic Structures, Industrial Tax Equalization, Retention and Modernization,
Procurement Processing Company Rebate, Research and Development, and Angel Investor.

Both music programs fell **below the $1 million statutory trigger in FY 2023** ($47,229 and $758,797),
so neither was analyzed. **There is therefore no ROI ratio and no jobs estimate for either program**,
and none has ever been published. This is itself a finding worth reporting: Louisiana's music
incentives were too small to qualify for the state's own effectiveness review.

The musical and theatrical credit crossed $1 million in FYE 2025 ($2,143,446). If LDR follows the
statute, it should appear in a future ROI edition. No newer ROI edition exists as of 2026-08-01
(probed for a January 2026 edition; 404).

## 7. Administering agency structure

Louisiana has **no standalone state music office**. Music incentives sit inside the economic
development agency:

- **Louisiana Economic Development (LED)** — the department
- **Office of Entertainment Industry Development (OEID)** — the division within LED that administers
  film, digital media, live performance, sound recording, and Qualified Music Company programs
- **"Louisiana Entertainment"** (louisianaentertainment.gov) — the public-facing brand for OEID
- **Louisiana Department of Revenue (LDR)** — issues rebates and administers the credits on the tax side
- Division Director for the music and live performance programs: Amanda Hafford

Vetoed **HB 653** would have moved the sound recording program out of LED and into the **office of
cultural development** within the **Department of Culture, Recreation and Tourism** — a shift from an
economic-development home to a cultural-agency home. That structural question is live in Louisiana
and is directly relevant to Texas benchmarking, even though the bill failed.

## 9. Terminology discipline for the white paper

Louisiana's credits pass through distinct stages with different dollar figures:

1. **Statutory cap** — $2.16M/CY certified (sound recording); $10M/FY granted (live performance).
   A ceiling, never approached.
2. **Initially certified** — LED confirms project eligibility up front. *No public series found.*
3. **Finally certified** — LED confirms verified expenditures after CPA cost report. *No public series found.*
4. **Claimed / rebated** — the taxpayer uses the credit, or LDR issues the rebate check. **This is the
   only stage with a published series**, and it is what the table in section 6 reports.

Describe the section 6 figures as "credits claimed" or "realized revenue loss." Do not call them
"credits awarded," "credits certified," or "program spending."
