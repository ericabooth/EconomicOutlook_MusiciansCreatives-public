**Caveat:** NASAA measures the state *arts agency*, not music policy. Texas's music-specific spending (TMIR, TMO) sits in the Governor's office, not the Texas Commission on the Arts, so NASAA's Texas figure **excludes** the $10.1M/year music rebate program entirely. Same issue for Louisiana and Tennessee, whose music incentives sit in economic development / entertainment commissions. Use NASAA as a general-arts baseline, not as the music benchmark.

## 2. NCSL trackers — GAP

No NCSL arts, culture, creative-economy, or entertainment-incentive tracker was located. NCSL's top-level policy navigation (checked at https://www.ncsl.org/ on 2026-08-01) lists 29 topic areas and contains no arts/culture/creative-economy category. Two candidate URLs returned HTTP 404:
- https://www.ncsl.org/labor-and-employment/creative-economy-and-the-arts
- https://www.ncsl.org/fiscal/state-film-production-incentives-and-programs

NCSL appears to have retired its film-incentive tracking page (it existed in earlier years). **Recommend substituting** one of: NCSL's general bill-tracking database with a keyword query; the Americans for the Arts state policy pulse; or NASAA's own "State Legislative Roundup 2025" and "FY2027 State Arts Agency Legislative Appropriations Preview" (https://nasaa-arts.org/nasaa_research/state-arts-agency-legislative-appropriations-preview-fy2027/). Note: this session exhausted its web-search budget before these substitutes could be pursued.

Also noted but not retrieved (member-gated): NASAA **Public Funding Sourcebook**, an Excel file of state-by-state appropriations from 1970 to present, available for purchase/members at https://nasaa-arts.org/nasaa_research/sourcebook-data/. This is the right source if the paper needs a long time series.

## 2b. Substitute cross-state tracker found: the Georgia DOAA evaluation

The best cross-state inventory of **music-specific** incentives located in this sweep is not from NCSL. It is Table 5 of the Georgia Department of Audits and Accounts evaluation, *Georgia Music Tax Credit: Economic and Fiscal Impact Analysis* (Georgia Southern University CBAER, December 8, 2023). Saved at `../peer_GA/GA_DOAA_Tax_Incentive_Evaluation_Musical_Tax_Credit_2023-12-14.pdf`.

It inventories two families of programs. Vintage is late 2023 — **re-verify each before publication**, since several have since changed or sunset.

**Family 1: live musical / theatrical production credits**

| State | Program | Credit rate | Minimum spend | Caps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Illinois | Live Theater Production Tax Credit Act (35 ILCS 17/) | 20% of IL labor and production spending; +15% for labor paid to high-poverty/high-unemployment residents | $100,000 on labor and marketing | $500,000 per applicant per tax year; $2M all applicants per fiscal year |
| Louisiana | Musical and Theatrical Production Income Tax Credit (R.S. 47:6034) | 7% ($100k–$300k investment); 14% (>$300k to $1M); +7% of LA-resident payroll; +0.072% for employing students | — | $1M per project; **$10M all projects per fiscal year**; payroll credit capped at $1M per individual |
| Maryland | Theatrical Production Tax Credit (Md. Tax-Gen. § 10-756) | 25% of direct costs in Maryland | >$100,000 direct Maryland cost | $2M single production; $5M all productions per fiscal year |
| New York | Empire State Musical and Theatrical Production Tax Credit (5 NYCRR § 240) — the **outside-NYC** program | 25% of qualified expenditures; $25M infrastructure incentive for reallocation | 8 shows in more than 3 locations | **$8M all productions per year**; salaries capped at $200,000/week |
| New York | NYC Musical and Theatrical Production Tax Credit (N.Y. Tax Law § 24-a) | 25% of expenses | Level 1: Manhattan facility, 500+ seats | Level 1 up to $3M; Level 2 up to $350,000 where production budget is $750,000. Requires state-approved diversity and arts jobs training plan; productions with revenue exceeding 200% must contribute 50% of the credit to the Arts Cultural Program Fund |
| Missouri | Entertainment Industry Jobs Act (SB 170) | 30% of qualified expenses | — | $1M if expenses <$4M; $2M if $4M–$8M; $3M if ≥$8M |
| Ohio | Motion Picture Tax Credit (Ohio § 122.85) | 30% of eligible expenditures | $300,000 per production | $50M/yr total; **$5M for theatrical productions**, $45M motion pictures |
| Pennsylvania | Entertainment Economic Enhancement Program (§ 8774-D) | 25% class 1–2 venues; 30% class 1 and 3; 35% class 1 and 3 not serving alcohol; +5% for two or more class 2–3 venues | $3M in PA equipment; 10 days rehearsing in-state | **$8M annual cap**; single tour $0.8M–$2.0M |
| Rhode Island | Musical and Theatrical Production Tax Credits (R.I. Gen. Laws § 44-31.3-2) | 30% of total eligible expenditures | $100,000 minimum | $5M per taxpayer; **$15M all taxpayers** |
| Georgia | Musical Tax Credit (O.C.G.A. § 48-7-40.33) | 15%, +5% tier 1–2 counties | **$500,000** live; $250,000 synchronized recording; $100,000 other recording | $15M/yr cap in final years — **$0 ever awarded** |

DOAA's own synthesis, verbatim: "Most states commonly include a 25 to 35 percent tax credit for all qualified expenditures… The annual cap for all theatrical productions ranges from $5 million to $15 million, with $1 million through $5 million usually being the cap for a single theatrical production."

Refundability and transferability, per DOAA: refundable in **Louisiana, Maryland, New York, Ohio**; transferable in **Louisiana, Illinois, Rhode Island, Missouri, Pennsylvania**; carryforward five years in Illinois and Missouri, three years in Rhode Island and Pennsylvania.

**Family 2: recorded-music incentives**

| State | Program | Design |
|---|---|---|
| Louisiana | Sound Recording Incentive Program | 18% credit; project minimum $25,000 total with at least $10,000 from Louisiana residents; **$100,000 cap per project; $2.16M total program cap per year** |
| Tennessee | Tennessee Entertainment Tax Credit (F&E credit) | Post-production, VFX, and **music scoring**; $50,000 minimum for post-production; 40–50% of qualified payroll expenses depending on county of residence. DOAA cites video game scores (Call of Duty, Madden, Star Wars) and Lost in Space s2–3, Fargo s1, The Shack |
| Oklahoma | Filmed in Oklahoma Act of 2021 | Music as an uplift inside the film credit: base 20%; **+2 percentage points if at least 1% of qualified expenditures go to music in Oklahoma** |

**Note the structural point for Texas:** every program in Family 1 subsidizes *production companies mounting touring or Broadway-style shows*. None of them subsidizes an operating small venue the way Texas's TMIR does. Texas's rebate is closer in kind to a venue-operating subsidy than to a production credit, which makes head-to-head dollar comparisons with GA/NY/LA misleading unless the difference in target is stated. Texas's TMIR has no real analogue in this table.

Also useful from the same report — **Oxford Economics, *The Concerts and Live Entertainment Industry*, July 2021**, 2019 reference year (DOAA Table 4). Texas is not in DOAA's sample, but the comparators are: New York $7.8B output / 48,264 jobs / $509M state and local taxes; Illinois $3.9B / 28,192 / $294M; Pennsylvania $3.9B / 25,291 / $315M; Ohio $2.9B / 22,467 / $170M; Georgia $2.7B / 24,124 / $113M; Missouri $1.6B / 12,071 / $115M; Maryland $655M / 5,139 / $41M; Rhode Island $551M / 4,772 / $28M; Louisiana $543M / 4,511 / $57M. If the Oxford Economics source report includes Texas, it would give a **methodologically consistent** cross-state live-music benchmark — better than comparing TXP's Texas-only study to anything else. Worth chasing.
