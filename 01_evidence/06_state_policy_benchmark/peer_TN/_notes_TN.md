# Tennessee — State Music/Entertainment Policy Benchmark

Evidence notes for Texas 2036 white paper on state music-economy policy.
Compiled 2026-08-01. All retrieval dates 2026-08-01 unless noted.

---

## HEADLINE FINDING

Tennessee's music-specific state incentive is the **TEC Scoring Incentive Program** — a
**25% cash grant** for producing **original scores** for film, television, animation,
commercials, gaming and multimedia in Tennessee.

**Two framing corrections matter for the white paper:**

1. **It is a scoring incentive, not a music-industry incentive.** It subsidizes music
   made *for visual media*. It does not cover record production, artist development,
   touring, live music venues, or songwriting outside a scoring project. **Music videos
   are expressly ineligible** under the companion film incentive.
2. **It has no separate appropriation.** The scoring incentive and the film/TV production
   incentive are both paid out of a single budget line — **330.17 Film and Television
   Incentive Fund**. State budget documents do not disaggregate music dollars from film
   dollars. **Any claim about "what Tennessee spends on music" cannot be sourced from the
   budget document.** See "Gaps."

## PROGRAM 1: TEC Scoring Incentive Program (the music-specific one)

### Authority and administration
- **Administering agency:** **Tennessee Entertainment Commission (TEC)**, also styled the
  **Tennessee Film, Entertainment and Music Commission**, operating within the
  **Tennessee Department of Economic and Community Development (TNECD)**.
  Grants are awarded by the **TNECD Grants Committee**; recipients enter a Grant Contract with TNECD.
- **Funding authority, verbatim from the guidelines (§ 1.1.2):**
  > "The TEC Scoring Incentive Program is funded through the **Visual Content Modernization
  > Act 2018**. Funding is determined by the Tennessee General Assembly on an annual basis."
- **CORRECTION to the research brief:** the brief suggested the **Visual Content Act of
  2006** / T.C.A. § 4-3-4801 et seq. Both matter, but they are different laws:
  - The **2006 law** established the **Film and Television Incentive Fund** itself. The TN
    budget document states: "The Film and Television Incentive Fund, **established by a
    2006 law**, provides incentive grants to encourage the production of films … and
    **stand-alone post-production scoring and editing** in Tennessee."
  - The **Visual Content Modernization Act of 2018** is the authority the scoring
    guidelines cite for the scoring program specifically.
  - **The precise T.C.A. section numbers for either act were NOT verified in this pass.**
    Do not publish "T.C.A. § 4-3-4801 et seq." without checking it. Treat the statutory
    citation as an open item.

### Rate, thresholds and caps
- **Rate:** **up to 25%** grant on Qualified Tennessee Expenditures (QTE).
- **Minimum spend (verbatim, § 2.2 "Minimum Spend & Program Cap"):**
  > "Those Grantees recording in **Primary markets** are required to have a minimum of
  > **$50,000 per project** in Qualified Tennessee expenditures (QTE). Recordings in
  > **Secondary markets** are required to have a minimum of **$25,000 per project** in (QTE).
  > … The rebate is **capped at $500,000 per company/per year**."
- **Market definitions (§ 9, verbatim):**
  - **Primary Market:** "Counties defined as the **Nashville Metropolitan Area**"
  - **Secondary Markets:** "Counties defined as the Metropolitan Areas of **Knoxville,
    Chattanooga and Memphis** which are located within the boundaries of the State"
  - This confirms the brief's "$50,000 in Nashville-area / $25,000 elsewhere" — with the
    nuance that "elsewhere" means the other three *metros*, not the whole rest of the state.
- **Per-company annual cap:** **$500,000**.
- **Per-resident wage limit:** eligible spend is limited to the **first $50,000** in gross
  wages, salaries, fees, per diem and fringe benefits paid to any one Tennessee resident.
- **Aggregate program cap:** none stated. Funding is "subject to annual appropriations by
  the State Legislature," and receipt of a Certificate of Conditional Eligibility (CCE)
  "does not guarantee availability of the incentive funds nor the amount of funds."
  Effectively a **discretionary, appropriation-limited grant**, not an entitlement.

### Qualifying activity and expenditures
- **Eligible projects:** original scores for **film, television, animation, commercials,
  gaming and multimedia** projects produced in Tennessee.
- **Qualified expenditures:** studio rental fees and associated fixed costs; **artist and
  musician salaries**; mixing and mastering; instrument and equipment rentals. Goods or
  services must be purchased from a **Tennessee vendor** or paid to a **Tennessee resident**.
- Expenditures incurred before the Grant Contract Effective Date do not qualify.
- **Verification:** projects of **$250,000+ require a CPA review**; under $250,000 require
  TNECD review.
- **Timeline:** project must begin within **120 days** of the Effective Date; final
  materials due by the **18-month** submission date; qualification period runs from the
  Effective Date through a 12-month completion date.

### Status as of 2026-08-01
**ENACTED, FUNDED, AND ACTIVE.** Current guidelines are revision **July 1, 2025**.
Marketing examples cited by TEC include Netflix's "Straw," EA Sports, and "Lost in Space,"
all scored at Ocean Way Studio in Nashville.

## PROGRAM 2: TEC Production Incentive (film/TV) — relevant context

- **Rate:** up to **25%** grant on Qualified Tennessee Expenditures.
- **Minimum spend — SOURCE CONFLICT, flag before publishing:**
  - The **guidelines PDF** (rev. 2024-07-10), § 2.2: "Scripted Television Series, Feature
    Films and Television Pilots are required to have a minimum of **$200,000** in Qualified
    Tennessee Expenditures (QTE)."
  - The **live TEC website** (tnentertainment.com/film/incentives, accessed 2026-08-01):
    "If you spend **$500,000 or more** on Qualified Tennessee Spend (QTE), either per
    episode or per project, you are eligible for a cash rebate in the form of a 25% grant."
  - These do not reconcile. Verify with TEC before citing a film threshold.
- **No per-project cap** on the film incentive, per the website. Explicitly **not**
  first-come-first-served; awards are at TEC/TNECD discretion.
- **Music expenditures are eligible QTE within a film project:** "Music related
  expenditures purchased from a Tennessee Vendor or Tennessee Resident including
  **licensing, studio rentals, studio musicians, and equipment rental**." TEC application
  forms carry a distinct **"TN Music Amount"** field, and the budget-line detail separates
  vendor spend "excluding music." **Sideline musicians** are an eligible payroll line item.
- **Music videos are NOT eligible** for the production incentive — listed among excluded
  formats alongside concerts, awards shows, and unscripted television.

### Companion Tennessee tax provisions (from the TEC film incentives page)
- **Franchise & excise tax credit** offsetting up to **50%** of F&E liability for
  compensation earned in Tennessee on a Qualified Production — **Tenn. Code Ann. § 67-4-2109(j)(2)**.
- **Point-of-purchase sales tax exemption** for taxable goods and services used in a Qualified Production.
- **Hotel occupancy tax rebate** after 30 continuous days; **state occupancy sales tax
  rebate** after 90 continuous days — **Tenn. Code Ann. § 67-6-205(c)**.
- **No state income tax on wages**; **no state permitting fees**; most state-owned
  property available for filming free of charge.

## APPROPRIATIONS — actual dollars (highest priority)

**Budget line: 330.17 Film and Television Incentive Fund**, Department of Economic and
Community Development. This single line funds **both** the film/TV production incentive
**and** the scoring/music incentive.

Program description, verbatim from the FY2026-27 Budget Document (p. B-343):
> "The Film and Television Incentive Fund, established by a 2006 law, provides incentive
> grants to encourage the production of films, movies, television shows, computer-generated
> imagery, interactive digital media, streaming platforms, and **stand-alone post-production
> scoring and editing** in Tennessee. To be eligible, such productions must meet criteria
> established by the Tennessee Film, Entertainment and Music Commission."

**Two caveats on this series.**
1. **Estimates overstate.** FY2024-25 was *estimated* at $10,668,300 in the FY2026 budget

   Use actuals where available and label estimates as estimates.
2. **The recurring base is far smaller than the headline.** The FY2026-27 base is
   $8,687,100; the $28.7M figure is base plus a **one-time** $20M. Presenting $28.7M as
   Tennessee's ongoing commitment would misstate it.

## GAPS / NOT FOUND

1. **Music dollars cannot be isolated from film dollars.** The scoring incentive has no
   separate budget line. **This is the single most important limitation in the TN file.**
   Getting an actual music/scoring award total would require a public-records request to
   TNECD or a TEC grant-award list. Recommend requesting it if the paper needs a real number.
2. **No TEC award list or annual report was located.** Unlike NY's upstate program,
   Tennessee does not appear to publish per-project scoring grant awards. **Not found.**
3. **Statutory citations unverified.** Neither the Visual Content Act of 2006 nor the
   Visual Content Modernization Act of 2018 was traced to specific T.C.A. sections.
   The brief's suggested "T.C.A. Title 4, Ch. 3, Part 48 / § 4-3-4801 et seq." was
   **not confirmed**.
4. **No Tennessee live-music-venue grant program was found.** Nothing surfaced in the TEC
   materials or the budget documents. Absence of evidence here is weak evidence of absence —
   the search was constrained (see method note).
5. **Film-incentive minimum spend is contradictory** across TEC's own guidelines
   ($200,000) and website ($500,000).
6. **TN Arts Commission music-specific grants** — none identified; the Arts Commission's
   grant categories were not enumerated in detail.

## METHOD NOTE
The session's web-search budget (200 searches) was exhausted partway through this work.
Tennessee material was gathered by directly crawling tnentertainment.com, createtn.com and
tn.gov with `curl`, and by downloading and text-extracting the budget PDFs locally with
`pdftotext`. Budget figures were read from the extracted text of the official PDFs, not
from any secondary summary. Items marked "not found" reflect a constrained search, not a
confirmed absence.
