# Texas Music Office (TMO) — office profile and appropriation history

Retrieved 2026-08-01. Sources listed at bottom.

## What the office is

- Formally the **Music, Film, Television, and Multimedia Office** within the Office of the Governor, established under **Government Code ch. 485**. "Texas Music Office" is the music-side brand; the **Texas Film Commission** is the film/TV side. Both sit in the same statutory office and the same appropriations strategy.
- Founded 1990. The Austin Tourism Commission presentation (undated, content places it around 2023) says "For 34 years the Texas Music Office has served the Texas music industry."
- Core functions per that presentation: clearinghouse for industry information via the **Texas Music Industry Directory** (a referral network of **16,000-plus Texas music businesses**); liaison between music businesses and government; publicize industry developments; attract music industry to Texas.
- Programs listed by TMO: Music Friendly Texas community certification; Texas Music Incubator Rebate (TMIR); Music Education & Community Grant Program (the "License Plate Grant"); Texas Music Industry Database; music business guides; concert-safety/production guides; internships.

## Appropriation history (General Appropriations Act, Trusteed Programs Within the Office of the Governor)

**Important structural caveat: the Texas Music Office does not have its own line item.** Its operating money is bundled with the Texas Film Commission and the film incentive program inside a single appropriations strategy. Only the TMIR rebate money is separately identifiable.

| Biennium | GAA | Strategy | Combined TFC + TMO + film-incentive amount | Texas Music Incubator Account No. 5193 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022-23 | SB 1, 87R | C.1.1 Create Jobs and Promote Texas | **$45,000,000** General Revenue (biennium) | **$0 — no appropriation** |
| 2024-25 | HB 1, 88R | C.1.1 Create Jobs and Promote Texas | **$45,000,000** General Revenue (biennium) | **$10,100,000 each fiscal year** = $20,200,000 biennium (Rider 39) |
| 2026-27 | SB 1, 89R | C.2.1 Music Film Television Multimedia | **$55,757,969 All Funds** (biennium) per Rider 40 | **$10,100,000 each fiscal year** = $20,200,000 biennium |

Verbatim, 2026-27 GAA Rider 40 (Texas Music, Film, Television, and Multimedia):
> "Included in amounts appropriated above in Strategy C.2.1, Music Film Television Multimedia, is $55,757,969 in All Funds for the 2026-27 biennium, for the Texas Film Commission, the Texas Music Office, and the Moving Image Industry Incentive Program (TMIIIP) as authorized under Government Code, Chapter 485. This amount includes $10,100,000 in General Revenue-Dedicated Texas Music Incubator Fund Account No. 5193 in each fiscal year of the 2026-27 biennium to establish and administer the Texas Music Incubator Program … and $30,000,000 from the General Revenue Fund identified in Rider 45, Higher Education Film Workforce Pilot Program."

Decomposition of the 2026-27 $55,757,969:
- $20,200,000 — Texas Music Incubator rebates (GR-Dedicated Account 5193)
- $30,000,000 — Higher Education Film Workforce Pilot Program (Rider 45, FY2026 only, film only)
- **~$5,557,969 residual** — everything else, i.e. Texas Film Commission plus Texas Music Office operations plus TMIIIP administration for the two-year period.

Strategy C.2.1 printed amounts: $42,890,406 (FY2026) and $12,867,563 (FY2027); sum = $55,757,969.

## The 2025 restructuring: film pulled out, music left in place

**SB 22, 89th Legislature, Regular Session (2025)** created a new **Texas moving image industry incentive fund** (Gov't Code § 485.0225) and directed the comptroller, verbatim:
> "Not later than the 30th day of each state fiscal biennium, the comptroller shall deposit to the credit of the Texas moving image industry incentive fund … **$300 million** of the proceeds from the collection of the taxes imposed by this chapter."

SB 22 sunsets the fund provisions effective September 1, 2035. Because of SB 22, GAA footnote 3 records "net decreases of $21,550,629 in FY 2026 and $20,573,472 in FY 2027 out of General Revenue Funds" in Strategy C.2.1 — the film incentive left the strategy for its own dedicated fund.

**Benchmark for the paper:** within the same statutory office (Gov't Code ch. 485), Texas now dedicates **$300 million per biennium to moving-image production** and **$20.2 million per biennium to live-music venue rebates** — a ratio of roughly **14.9 to 1**.

## Music Education & Community Grant Program (License Plate Grant)

Funded not by appropriation but by specialty plate fees: **$22 of the $30 fee** from each Texas music specialty license plate goes to music instruments and lessons for Texas schoolchildren and to nonprofit partnerships in under-served communities. Administered by TMO. No public total awarded figure was located.

## TMO-commissioned economic impact study (the number most often cited)

"The 2024 Economic Impact of Music in Texas," TXP, Inc., **January 2025** (fifth in a biennial series since 2015).

Direct footprint, 2024:
| Segment | Jobs | Earnings | Revenue/Sales |
|---|---|---|---|
| Music-related business | 71,385 | $4.039B | $11.327B |
| Music-related education | 15,447 | $0.842B | n.a. |
| Music-related tourism | n.a. | n.a. | $1.206B |

Total impact including indirect and induced, 2024:
| Segment | Jobs | Earnings | Revenue/Sales | TX tax revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Music-related business | 145,855 | $8.121B | $24.706B | $0.407B |
| Music-related education | 24,476 | $1.510B | $4.132B | $0.076B |
| Music-related tourism | 25,648 | $0.853B | $2.827B | $0.081B |
| **Total** | **195,979** | **$10.484B** | **$31.655B** | **$0.564B** |

Caveats worth flagging in the paper:
- Job counts are built from the **TMO's own Texas Music Directory** (a self-listed business referral network), crossed with QCEW wages and 2017 Economic Census revenue ratios — not from a probability sample. The directory is a marketing asset of the agency being evaluated.
- 2024 is the **first year tourism was included**, so the jump from the 2023 study (192,000 total jobs, $26.6B activity, ~$470M tax revenue) to the 2024 study (195,979 jobs, $31.7B, $564M) is partly a definitional change, not growth. TXP itself notes "the number of overall jobs directly associated with music in Texas has stagnated."
- Revenue ratios anchored to the **2017 Economic Census** are eight years stale as of the 2024 reference year.
