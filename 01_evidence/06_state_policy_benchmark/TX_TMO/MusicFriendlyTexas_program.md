# Music Friendly Texas Certified Community Program

- **Source URL:** https://gov.texas.gov/music/page/music-friendly-communities
- **Retrieved:** 2026-08-01
- **Administering agency:** Texas Music Office, Office of the Governor

## Scale

- **92 certified communities** as of retrieval.
- Growth benchmark: the TMO's Austin Tourism Commission presentation (content ~2023) lists **59 certified communities**. So roughly 33 communities added in about three years.
- Most recent designations named on the page: Brownwood (12/8/2025), San Augustine (12/1/2025), Leakey (10/8/2025). Governor's-office press releases place Buda as the 78th (April 2025), Garland 83rd (May 2025), Fate 84th (June 2025), Bandera 85th (June 2025), Pasadena 86th (June 2025), Hallettsville 88th (August 2025), Borger 89th (September 2025), Brownwood 92nd (December 2025).
- Program created **2016**.

## What certification requires (five steps)

1. **Establish an MFT advisory board and designate a Music Friendly Liaison.** Verbatim: "Each certified MFT Community must have a central point of contact, their Music Friendly Liaison, who is employed within a division of city government."
2. **Submit the MFT Workshop Petition** (form: https://gov.texas.gov/uploads/files/music/Music_Friendly_Texas_Certified_Community_Program_Workshop_Petition.pdf).
3. **Host a virtual workshop.** "The workshop typically lasts 30–45 minutes and offers an opportunity for the community's advisory board to learn about the program basics."
4. **Execute a memorandum of understanding** covering data exchange with the Texas Music Industry Database.
5. **Register with the Texas Music Industry Database.**

## Analytical read for the paper

This is a **zero-dollar designation program**. Certification carries no grant, no tax benefit, and no statutory entitlement. The consideration a city gives is a staff liaison, a local advisory board, an MOU, and data submitted to a state directory. The consideration the state gives is the designation itself, listing in the state database, and a Governor's press release. It is a coordination and branding instrument, not a funding instrument, and should not be counted as music spending. Its measurable output is the liaison network and the directory, which in turn feeds the TXP economic impact study's job estimates — a circularity worth flagging.

No published evaluation of the program's effect on local music employment, venue counts, or venue survival was located.
