# Texas: other state-level levers touching working musicians

Retrieved 2026-08-01.

## 1. Hotel occupancy tax — the arts/music authorization cities actually use

**Tax Code § 351.101(a)(4)** is the operative subsection. Verbatim:

> "(4) the encouragement, promotion, improvement, and application of the arts, including instrumental and vocal music, dance, drama, folk art, creative writing, architecture, design and allied fields, painting, sculpture, photography, graphic and craft arts, motion pictures, radio, television, tape and sound recording, and other arts related to the presentation, performance, execution, and exhibition of these major art forms;"

Key points for the paper:
- The chapeau of § 351.101(a) restricts all municipal HOT revenue: it "may be used only to promote tourism and the convention and hotel industry." Every music expenditure must clear that two-part test — the arts purpose in (a)(4) **and** the tourism nexus.
- **There is no music-specific subsection.** Music is one item in a long enumerated list of art forms inside the general arts paragraph. Cities fund live music by reading themselves into (a)(4), not by pointing to a music line in statute.
- Austin's own legal analysis cites **§ 351.101(a)(1), (a)(5), and (b)** in addition to (a)(4) for venue-related uses — (a)(1) for facility acquisition/construction/operation, (a)(5) for historical restoration and preservation. See City of Austin, "Legal Framework for Funding Venues Under the Texas Local Government [Code]," https://services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=305078 (not downloaded; retrieved reference only).
- **Allocation caps:** Tax Code § 351.103 caps how much of HOT revenue certain municipalities may spend on (a)(4) arts purposes. This limit was not pulled in this pass and should be checked before the paper asserts what Austin can spend — **open item**.

Sources: https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._tax_code_section_351.101 and https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/tax-code/tax-sect-351-101/ (both retrieved 2026-08-01). Official text: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov (Tax Code ch. 351).

## 2. The film comparison — SB 22 (89R, 2025)

Same statutory chapter (Gov't Code ch. 485), same office, radically different scale.

**SB 22, 89th Legislature, Regular Session (2025)**, "relating to the Texas moving image industry incentive program and the establishment and funding of the Texas moving image industry incentive fund." Saved: `SB22_89R_Texas_moving_image_incentive_fund.pdf`
URL: https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/pdf/SB00022F.pdf (retrieved 2026-08-01)

Verbatim, § 151.801(g) as added by SB 22 art. 2:
> "Not later than the 30th day of each state fiscal biennium, the comptroller shall deposit to the credit of the Texas moving image industry incentive fund established under Section 485.0225, Government Code, **$300 million** of the proceeds from the collection of the taxes imposed by this chapter."

- Sunset: SB 22 art. 3 makes the relevant sections expire **September 1, 2035**.
- Content condition worth noting: SB 22 § 1.01 states legislative intent that the office "may deny an application because of inappropriate content or content that portrays Texas or Texans in a negative fashion," and "shall consider general standards of decency and respect for the diverse beliefs and values" of Texans. No parallel content screen exists in the music rebate statute.
- **Ratio: $300M per biennium for moving image vs. $20.2M per biennium for music, both administered by the Music, Film, Television, and Multimedia Office — 14.9 to 1.**

## 3. Texas Commission on the Arts (TCA)

- FY2026 legislative appropriation: **$18,288,573**, up 27.7% from $14,319,358 in FY2025 (NASAA Table 1). Per capita $0.58, **rank 39 of 50** (NASAA Table 6). 0.019% of state general fund expenditures (NASAA Table 7).
- NASAA note: the increase comprises "$7.9 million funding increase for the next two years, with $2 million allocated to cultural tourism grants and the remainder directed to general support for arts organizations."
- Programs relevant to musicians: **Texas Touring Roster** (approved artists/companies for 2026-2028 are eligible for performance-support grants that help communities book them), **Cultural Districts** designation, rural initiatives, and the **Young Masters** program ($5,000 grants to high school artists).
- **GAP: no music-specific grant line or music share of TCA grant dollars was located.** TCA's public site does not break grants out by discipline. Getting the music share would require the TCA grant database (https://www.arts.texas.gov/current-grants/) or an open-records request. TCA's own site cites "$7 billion annually" for the arts and culture industry in Texas without a linked source.
- Source: https://www.arts.texas.gov/ (retrieved 2026-08-01).
