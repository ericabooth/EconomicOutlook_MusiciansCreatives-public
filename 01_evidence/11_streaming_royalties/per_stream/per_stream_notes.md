# Per-Stream Economics — Secondary/Trade Sources

Retrieved 2026-08-01. No PDFs available for these (web-based reports/blogs); notes compiled from source pages directly.

## 1. Duetti "2024 Music Economics Report" (published Jan 23, 2025)
**Source & method**: Duetti is a company that **buys/acquires independent artists' music catalogs** (raised $90M then $200M in funding for catalog acquisition, per Business Wire/MBW coverage) — i.e., it is a market participant with a direct financial interest in catalog valuations, which hinge on per-stream payout assumptions. Report methodology described as "proprietary data and analytics, based on millions of data points," pooled-royalty-divided-by-total-streams (not a literal per-play rate — Duetti's own report acknowledges DSPs don't pay per-stream). Report covers creators earning between $100 and $350k annually; report page: report.duetti.co.

**Per-1,000-streams payout table (2024, USD):**
| Platform | $/1,000 streams |
|---|---|
| Amazon Music | $8.80 |
| TIDAL | $6.80 |
| Apple Music | $6.20 |
| YouTube | $4.80 |
| Deezer | $4.80 |
| Pandora | $3.20 |
| Spotify | $3.00 |
| TikTok | $0.50 (methodologically different — paid per video creation, not per stream; not directly comparable) |

Industry-wide blended average: $3.41/1,000 streams for independent artists in 2024, down <2% from 2023 (described as "stabilizing" after years of decline).

**Bias/pushback**: Spotify publicly disputed the report as "ridiculous and unfounded," arguing (a) it doesn't pay per-stream at all — it distributes a monthly royalty pool by streaming share — and (b) Duetti's calculated average is dragged down by Spotify's large free/ad-supported user base (402M free vs 252M subscribers at Q3 2024), which is a real driver of blended-rate differences across platforms, not necessarily evidence of "underpayment." (Source: Music Ally, "Spotify slams 'ridiculous and unfounded' Duetti report," Jan 24 2025.)

## 2. The Trichordist (artist-advocacy blog; category page "Royalty Rates")
**Source & method**: Self-described artist/songwriter advocacy site ("Artists For An Ethical and Sustainable Internet," #StopArtistExploitation tag line) — explicitly an advocacy, not neutral-analyst, source. Data described as drawn from "confidentially supplied" figures from mid-sized independent labels; calculates rates as total revenue ÷ total streams per service (same pooled-average approach as Duetti, different underlying data).

**Historical per-stream figures cited (older vintage, mostly pre-2020, illustrating the "penny problem" narrative):**
- Spotify: ~$0.00408 (2015, 2017) → $0.00331 (2018) → $0.00348 (2019)
- Apple Music: ~$0.00495 (2018) → $0.00675 (2019)
- YouTube (Content ID): generated only 6.4% of revenue despite ~51% of all streams in 2019 — often cited as the "value gap" statistic
- General framing used elsewhere citing Trichordist-sourced data: Spotify average ~$0.00348/stream ≈ $3,300-3,500 per million streams

**Caveat**: These figures are noticeably older (2015-2019 vintage) than the Duetti 2024 figures above and use a different, non-transparent data-collection method. Treat as directionally illustrative of the "fractions of a cent per play" framing rather than as a rigorous current-year comparison to Duetti/MIDiA numbers. thetrichordist.com/category/royalty-rates-2/

## 3. MIDiA Research
**Source & method**: Independent music-industry analyst/research firm (subscription reports + free blog commentary). Cited in a 2024 Variety piece ("Songwriters Are Getting Screwed by Streaming Even Worse Than They'd Thought") analyzing an artist-payout study; also referenced in relation to a SoundCloud "Fan-Powered Royalties" white paper covering ~118,000 artists.

**Figures found (via secondary aggregator, not MIDiA's own site directly — flagged as lower-confidence)**: one 2026-vintage secondary summary (Chartlex blog) cites a per-stream range of ~$0.0007-$0.015 across platforms, with Tidal highest (~$0.012-0.015), Apple Music mid (~$0.006-0.010), Spotify lowest (~$0.003-0.005) — directionally consistent with Duetti's platform ranking (Tidal/Apple > YouTube/Deezer > Pandora/Spotify) but the specific numbers come from a secondary blog aggregating MIDiA references, not a MIDiA-published report pulled directly. **Recommend re-verifying against a MIDiA-authored report/PDF before citing a specific number in the white paper** — midiaresearch.com/reports/category/streaming has a paywalled report list; the free blog (midiaresearch.com/blog/category/streaming-royalties) has commentary but a specific 2025/2026 per-stream table was not confirmed in this pass.

## Synthesis for sidebar
All three sources agree, independent of methodology, on:
1. Streaming payouts are **pooled averages**, not literal fixed per-play rates — any "$X per stream" figure is an ex-post calculation (total payout ÷ total streams), which all three sources' own methodology notes concede.
2. **Platform rank order is consistent**: Amazon Music/Tidal/Apple Music pay noticeably more per 1,000 streams than Spotify/Pandora, largely because of subscriber mix (higher ARPU markets, fewer/no free ad-tier listeners) rather than differential "generosity."
3. Blended per-1,000-stream rates cluster in the **~$3-$9 range** for major on-demand platforms in the current (2024-2025) period, i.e., roughly $0.003-$0.009 per stream — down from a wider, generally similar range cited by Trichordist for 2015-2019.
4. Every source here has a disclosed or evident interest: Duetti profits from catalog acquisition (incentive to show rates as low/volatile to justify buyouts, though it also has incentive to show "stabilizing" trend to attract sellers); Trichordist is artist-advocacy (incentive to emphasize low rates); MIDiA is a paid-research firm (less direct financial stake in the rate level itself, but commercial incentive to produce attention-getting findings). Recommend flagging all three as directionally informative but not authoritative/audited figures in the white paper text.
