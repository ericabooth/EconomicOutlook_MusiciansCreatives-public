# Texas / Austin Allocation Inputs

Retrieved 2026-08-01. PDF saved: `texas_allocation/TXP_TX_Music_Impact_Winter_2025.pdf`.

## Texas Music Office (TMO) — Texas Music Industry Directory
- TMO is a state-funded business-promotion/information clearinghouse for the Texas music industry, part of the Office of the Texas Governor, based in Austin. Director: Chip Adams.
- TMO's Texas Music Industry Directory (self-reported/unverified listings, businesses submit their own entries) totals **6,955 "talent" listings** statewide (musicians/performers specifically), within a broader database of 15,000+ listings across artists, venues, studios, publishers, legal/tour services, radio stations, etc.
  - Regional talent-listing counts: Austin area 2,509; Dallas/Fort Worth 1,869; Houston/Galveston 1,064; Northeast Texas 232 (remainder spread across other regions).
  - TMO reports "more than 14,000 individual clients" use its resources/assistance annually (broader than the directory-listing count; likely includes venues, businesses, etc., not just musicians).
  - **Caveat**: directory listings are opt-in/self-submitted and not verified by TMO — this is a floor/visibility measure, not a comprehensive census of working musicians in Texas or Austin.

## Texas Music Industry Economic Impact Report (biennial; TXP, Inc. for TMO, economist Jon Hockenyos)
Latest edition: "The 2024 Economic Impact of Music in Texas," published January 2025 (data vintage: 2024).
- **Direct footprint** (Table 1 of report): Music-related business = 71,385 jobs, $4.039B earnings, $11.327B revenue/sales; Music-related education = 15,447 jobs, $0.842B earnings; Music-related tourism = $1.206B in direct spending (jobs N/A for this category).
- **Total impact** (direct + indirect + induced, via input-output/multiplier modeling): ~196,000 permanent jobs, ~$10.5B in earnings, ~$31.7B in annual economic activity (per gov.texas.gov press coverage of the report).
- Methodology: job estimates come from the TMO's own Texas Music Directory (see above) crossed with Texas Workforce Commission QCEW wage data; revenue estimated via 2017 Economic Census ratios; this is the **fifth** iteration of this biennial study (same methodology used across editions since inception), so a Texas-only time series exists back at least ~8-10 years if needed for trend framing.
- Prior (2023) edition, for trend context: ~192,000 total jobs, $9.4B earnings, $26.6B economic activity (per secondary coverage — not independently verified from a saved PDF of that edition).
- **Caveat for the streaming sidebar**: this is a broad economic-impact study (jobs/wages/output across business+education+tourism), not a musician-headcount or royalty-income study. It is useful context for "how big is the Texas music economy" framing, but it does not let you compute a clean "% of national streaming royalties that should flow to Texas artists" allocator on its own. If an artist-share allocator is needed, the most defensible proxy from this report is: 71,385 direct music-business jobs statewide (2024) as a share of a national employed-musician estimate (e.g., BLS OEWS musicians-and-singers occupational counts, not yet pulled in this pass — flagging as a possible next step).

## AFM (American Federation of Musicians) locals — union membership as a lower-bound headcount
- **AFM Local 433 (Austin Federation of Musicians)**: ~350 members (per search-result summary of afm433.com / AFM.org local listing). This is a small fraction of the working-musician population in Austin (union density in live/session music is low relative to total gigging musicians), so treat as a floor, not an estimate of Austin's musician population.
- **AFM Local 65-699 (Houston Professional Musicians' Association)**: jurisdiction covers greater Houston/Southeast Texas plus parts of Southwest Louisiana (Harris, Fort Bend, Galveston, Montgomery, Brazoria, Brazos counties, etc., plus several LA parishes). **Specific current membership count was not found in public sources** in this pass — dues structure found ($165/yr or $85 semi-annual, plus one-time $35 local + $65 AFM federation initiation fees) but not a member headcount. Would require a direct records request or a citation from AFM's own membership disclosures (e.g., LM-2 filings with the Department of Labor, since AFM locals are labor organizations subject to OLMS reporting) to get an authoritative number — flagging as a gap/next step if a Houston comparison point is needed.

## ASCAP / BMI member counts by state
- **Not published.** Both ASCAP and BMI publish aggregated national repertoire/data-transparency tools (their joint "Songview" platform, covering 38M+ musical works) for **ownership-share/work-level data**, not member counts broken out by state or metro area. Searches turned up no public state-level membership statistics for either PRO.
- **Recommendation**: if a Texas/Austin songwriter-population number is needed, the AFM local counts and/or TMO directory counts above are the only public proxies found; ASCAP/BMI would need to be approached directly (unlikely to disclose) or estimated indirectly (e.g., via BLS OEWS "Musicians and Singers" / "Music Directors and Composers" state occupational employment estimates, which were not pulled in this pass but are a plausible next step).
