# Spotify Loud & Clear — Royalties Transparency Report

Retrieved 2026-08-01 from loudandclear.byspotify.com (main page + /takeaways/). Report reflects **2025 royalty data** (this edition of Loud & Clear was published in early 2026, consistent with Spotify's pattern of releasing the report ~March following the reporting year — cf. the "As Spotify Turns 20" Spotify Newsroom post dated 2026-03-11 and Music Ally/MBW/Billboard/Variety coverage dated March 2026).

## Headline figures (2025 data, as reported by Spotify)
- Spotify paid **$11B+** to the music industry in 2025 (described in coverage as "more than any platform has ever paid in a year"); lifetime total paid since Loud & Clear tracking began: **~$70B**.
- Royalty payout growth (~10%+ YoY) outpaced overall reported music-industry revenue growth (~4%) per Spotify's own framing.
- Nearly half of all royalties in 2025 are attributed to independent artists and labels (per Spotify framing).

## Artist earning tiers (2025)
- **$1,000,000+**: 1,500+ artists generated over $1 million in royalties from Spotify alone in 2025.
- **$10,000,000+**: 80+ artists generated $10M+ from Spotify in 2025 (per Music Ally/MBW coverage of the report).
- **$100,000+**: 13,800+ artists generated at least $100,000 in 2025 — about 1,400 more than the prior year (per Billboard coverage).
- **$10,000+**: Spotify states artists from 150+ countries generated at least $10,000 on the platform; more than a third of artists earning $10,000+ in 2025 were DIY or started that way (self-released).

## Historical (10-year) comparison Spotify highlights
- The "100,000th highest-earning artist" (i.e., the artist at that specific earnings rank) generated **>$7,300** in 2025 royalties, versus **~$350** for the equivalent-rank artist 10 years earlier (2015) — a 20x+ increase Spotify attributes to catalog/market growth. (This is a rank-based comparison, not the same artist over time, and doesn't control for the much larger number of tracks/artists on the platform in 2025 vs 2015 — i.e., competition for streams has also grown enormously over the same period. Worth noting as a caveat if citing this stat.)

## Caveats — platform-reported data
- **This is Spotify's own self-published, self-audited report.** It is not independently audited or verified by a third party; Spotify controls what metrics to publish and how to frame them (e.g., which earnings tiers to highlight, which historical comparisons to draw).
- Figures represent gross royalties attributed to a rightsholder account, which is not the same as artist take-home pay — labels, distributors, publishers, and (for featured artists) any recoupable advances or per-agreement splits are taken out before an individual musician sees a share. An "artist" hitting $100K+ in Spotify-attributed royalties could be a full band, a label's roster account, or a rights administrator aggregating many acts — Loud & Clear does not clarify entity-level detail behind each earnings-tier count.
- No Texas- or Austin-specific artist breakdown is published.
- Full historical takeaways page (loudandclear.byspotify.com/takeaways/) uses interactive graphics for its "10 takeaways" — several exact figures embedded in on-page graphics were not machine-extractable via text fetch; the numbers above were corroborated across multiple press write-ups (Billboard, Variety, Music Ally, MBW) rather than pulled as raw text from Spotify's page.
