# Findings — Streaming Royalties Sidebar ("Streaming Pennies" Math)

Compiled 2026-08-01. See `_sources.md` for full citations/vintages/caveats and the per-topic notes files for supporting detail.

- **RIAA: streaming's share of the US recorded-music dollar roughly doubled from 2015 to now.** Streaming was 34.3% of US recorded music revenue in 2015 (retail basis, $7.0B total, first year streaming became the largest format) versus 82-84% in 2024-2025 (84% of $17.7B retail in 2024; 82.1% of $11.5B wholesale in 2025 under RIAA's new wholesale-reporting basis, +3.1% YoY). **Caveat**: RIAA changed its reporting basis from retail to wholesale between the 2024 and 2025 reports, so the dollar totals are not directly comparable year-over-year — the percentage-share comparison is the more basis-robust claim.

- **SoundExchange (statutory/webcasting royalties to artists and labels — not interactive services like Spotify) distributed $1.054B in 2024**, per its own audited annual report, up from $1.005B (2023) and $959M (2022) — a 5.2% administrative-cost rate in 2024. Unaudited 2025 figures show a dip to ~$991.5M (-5.9%), attributed partly to a SiriusXM underpayment dispute. Cumulative distributions since 2003 inception have passed **$13 billion**, to a claimed 700,000-800,000+ music creators (figure varies across 2025 press releases).

- **MLC (mechanical/songwriter royalties, separate pool from SoundExchange) has distributed roughly $3 billion cumulatively since its 2021 launch through late 2025**, growing to $4B+ in cumulative royalties *processed* by early 2026 (definitional note: MLC's own materials switch between "distributed" and "processed" as headline terms — flagged for care in the white paper). 2025 alone: $943M+ processed. Membership has grown from ~47-50K (end of 2024) to 74,000+ (end of 2025).

- **MLC's "black box" of historical unmatched royalties (2008-2020 usage) totaled ~$397-424M** at time of transfer to MLC, split roughly Apple Music $148M, Spotify $146M, Amazon $37M, Google/YouTube $30M, Pandora $15M. As of mid-to-late 2025, roughly 56-57% of that historical pool had been matched and distributed to rightsholders — meaning **well over $150M in songwriter/publisher money remained unmatched/undistributed** even ~5 years after collection began, illustrating the "streaming pennies get lost in the system" problem beyond just low per-stream rates.

- **Per-stream payouts cluster in the $0.003-$0.009 range for major platforms in 2024**, per Duetti's report (Spotify $3.00, Pandora $3.20, YouTube/Deezer $4.80, Apple Music $6.20, TIDAL $6.80, Amazon Music $8.80 — all per 1,000 streams; industry blended average $3.41/1,000). Older Trichordist-sourced figures (2015-2019 vintage) show a similar-order Spotify rate (~$0.0033-0.0041/stream). **All of these are pooled-average calculations (total payout ÷ total streams), not literal fixed per-play rates** — every source's own methodology note concedes this, and Spotify has publicly disputed the framing (arguing its low blended rate reflects a large free/ad-tier user base, not "underpayment").

- **Spotify's Loud & Clear (self-reported, 2025 data): 13,800+ artists earned $100,000+, 1,500+ earned $1 million+, and 80+ earned $10 million+** from Spotify alone; Spotify paid $11B+ industry-wide in 2025 (~$70B lifetime). Spotify highlights that the 100,000th-highest-earning artist made >$7,300 in 2025 vs. ~$350 a decade earlier — a striking stat, but it's a rank-based (not same-artist) comparison and doesn't control for the much larger pool of competing artists/tracks on the platform today. This is platform-reported, unaudited data.

- **Texas music industry (broad economic-impact framing, not artist-specific): 71,385 direct jobs, $4.0B in direct earnings, $11.3B in direct revenue in the "music business" category alone (2024 data)**, per the Texas Music Office's biennial TXP, Inc. impact study; total impact (direct+indirect+induced) reaches ~196,000 jobs and ~$31.7B in economic activity. This is useful "how big is the Texas music economy" context but does not itself yield a clean musician headcount or royalty-allocation share.

- **No usable Texas/Austin-specific share-of-national-streaming-royalties figure exists in any of the six source categories searched.** The best available Texas musician-count proxies are all partial/low-confidence: Texas Music Office directory lists 2,509 "talent" listings for the Austin region (self-submitted, unverified) out of 6,955 statewide; AFM Local 433 (Austin) has ~350 union members (a floor, given low union density among gigging musicians); AFM Local 65-699 (Houston) membership count could not be found publicly; ASCAP and BMI confirmed do not publish state-level member counts. **Any Texas allocation math in the sidebar will need to rely on a modeled proxy (e.g., BLS OEWS "Musicians and Singers" state employment share, not yet pulled) rather than a direct reported figure.**
