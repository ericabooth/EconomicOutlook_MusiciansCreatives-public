# Precedent (negative case): private audio schools and mentorship programs are NOT registered apprenticeships

These are commonly confused with apprenticeships in casual discussion. Neither is a DOL
Registered Apprenticeship; both are useful as "what fills the gap instead" evidence.

## The Blackbird Academy (Nashville, TN)
- Private, for-profit audio engineering school founded 2013 by engineer John McBride and
  Martina McBride, tied to Blackbird Studio.
  https://theblackbirdacademy.com/
- Studio & Live Sound Engineering program: ~6 months full-time, 700+ instructional hours,
  30-40 hrs/week, tuition approximately $23,100.
  https://theblackbirdacademy.com/program/studio-engineering/
- Structure: tuition-funded private certificate program, not an employer-sponsored,
  wage-progression Registered Apprenticeship. Apprentices in a true RA earn wages while
  training and progress on a defined wage scale; Blackbird students pay tuition with no wage
  component built into the credential itself.

## GRAMMY U Mentorship Program (Recording Academy)
- A 6-month, one-on-one mentorship pairing GRAMMY U (student/early-career) members with
  Recording Academy Voting/Professional members.
  https://www.grammy.com/membership/grammy-u/
  https://www.grammy.com/news/grammy-u-mentorship-program-2025-2026/
- Structure: informal, unpaid, relationship-based mentorship tied to Recording Academy
  membership — not a registered training program, not wage-earning, no defined competency
  standard or completion credential recognized by DOL.

## Why this matters for the white paper
The existence of well-known private programs (Blackbird) and industry mentorship (GRAMMY U)
shows the music industry already has *some* structured skill-building infrastructure — just
not organized around the registered-apprenticeship wage-and-credential model. This supports a
framing of "the industry has built informal/private substitutes because the registered
apprenticeship model doesn't fit gig-based creative work well," rather than "no training
infrastructure exists at all."
