# Precedent: IATSE stagehand/entertainment-technician apprenticeships

International Alliance of Theatrical Stage Employees (IATSE) locals — representing stagehands,
lighting, sound, rigging, and other live-event/entertainment-technology crafts — run the most
substantial structured, multi-year training pathways we found that are adjacent to music/live
event work.

- **IATSE Local One (New York City)** runs a 2-3 year apprenticeship covering lighting,
  sound, rigging, carpentry, and emerging technologies, with trades including lighting
  directors, stage electricians, set carpenters, sound technicians, and riggers.
  https://www.iatselocaloneapprenticeship.com/
  - The program's own public page describes apprenticeship structure, testing, and trade
    placement, but does **not** state on its face whether it is a DOL- or New York
    State-registered apprenticeship (i.e., appears in RAPIDS) or is a purely
    union/CBA-internal training structure. This should be treated as **unconfirmed
    registration status** rather than assumed either way.
- Other locals (e.g., IATSE Local 122, Local 38) run parallel "pre-apprentice" and
  apprenticeship testing processes with similar craft coverage.
  https://www.iatse122.org/online-application-form/ ,
  https://www.iatse38.org/?zone=/unionactive/view_page.cfm&page=How20To20Join
- We found indirect evidence that at least some theatrical-craft apprenticeships ARE
  formally registered with a state apprenticeship agency: California's Division of
  Apprenticeship Standards (DAS) lists a "Theatrical Training Corp" apprenticeship standard
  (file: 101291_SD_Theatrical_Training_Corp_Standards.pdf on dir.ca.gov/das/standards/), which
  is the kind of document a state-registered program produces. We were not able to retrieve
  the PDF content directly in this session (link returned an error on re-fetch), so this is
  flagged as a **lead to verify**, not a confirmed detail.

## Relevance to Texas
We found no IATSE Texas local (Austin, Houston, Dallas) apprenticeship page confirming DOL/SAA
registration in the time available. IATSE has locals in major Texas markets (stagehands at
venues/touring), but confirming registered-apprenticeship status for any Texas local would
require a follow-up check directly with DOL's Office of Apprenticeship or the specific local.

## Takeaway for the white paper
IATSE-style crew/stagehand apprenticeships are the closest real-world analogue to a
"structured pipeline" model for live-event/entertainment work, but they train **crew
occupations** (sound, lighting, rigging) rather than **performing musicians**. This
distinction is itself a useful frame: registered apprenticeship as a model fits
W-2, employer-sponsored, skills-ladder occupations reasonably well; it fits solo/gig
performing-musician work poorly because there is no single "employer-sponsor" analogous to a
construction contractor or theater.
